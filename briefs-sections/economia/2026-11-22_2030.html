Update: US intelligence chief says the best Nvidia chips stay in America; Beijing says controls no longer hold China back

On Sunday’s US talk shows, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton set out the administration’s line on chip exports: “We want to reserve the most exquisite computing capability for the U.S. … So, we do want U.S. chips — just not the most exquisite” (Fox News).

On ABC’s This Week he said export rules on advanced technology “are restrictions, they are not bans”, that Commerce and Treasury are “in the business of metering” access, and confirmed that the Justice Department is investigating Alice Huang, a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed International, and her company. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CBS the administration “still needs answers” from Nvidia on its chip sales (Bloomberg). A Global Times editorial (Chinese state media) replied that “the marginal impact of further US export controls on China’s AI industry is diminishing rapidly” and that the restrictions will weaken US chipmakers in global markets.

Context: the BIS remote-access rule of 16 November also covers data centres in the EU; California is investigating whether Nvidia illegally sold advanced chips to China.

Why it matters for Europe: Washington decides which tier of computing power each country receives, and Europe, without a top-end chip of its own, gets the “less exquisite” tier unless licensed otherwise.

What to watch: Commerce’s next steps on licence enforcement; the DOJ probe; any MOFCOM statement on Monday.

Sources: Jay Clayton on Fox News, ABC News transcript, Bloomberg, The Straits Times, Global Times

US group IQVIA nears a $1.1 billion takeover of the UK’s Quotient Sciences

IQVIA Holdings, the US clinical-research group, is closing in on a takeover of about $1.1 billion of Quotient Sciences (Nottingham, UK), a contract development and manufacturing organisation for early-stage medicines, and a deal could be announced as soon as Monday 23 November, according to people familiar with the matter (Bloomberg).

Quotient helps drug-makers formulate new medicines, manufacture them for clinical trials and test how they behave. It has been owned by the private-equity firm Permira since 2019. IQVIA and Permira declined to comment.

Context: the 14:30 edition covered US takeovers of European defence suppliers (Steyr Motors, Truflo Marine, Tekne); CEAFE is tracking US acquisitions of strategic European companies.

Why it matters for Europe: another link in a strategic European supply chain, early-stage drug development and clinical-trial manufacturing, would pass to US ownership.

What to watch: a formal announcement; any review under the UK National Security and Investment Act or by the CMA.

Sources: Bloomberg, Bloomberg Law

Thailand plans to curb exports of chemicals used in Myanmar’s rare-earth mining

Thailand will restrict the export and transit of chemicals used in rare-earth mining and processing to Myanmar and other “relevant countries”, Deputy Prime Minister Yodchanan Wongsawat said, after arsenic contamination of the Kok, Sai and Ruak rivers in northern Thailand was linked to rare-earth mining in Myanmar’s Shan state (Reuters).

The measure is planned, not yet in force: the National Security Council is compiling a list of chemicals, with no start date published. Thailand, China and Myanmar hold ministerial talks on 24 November and working-level talks on 25–26 November.

Why it matters for Europe: Myanmar’s mines are a major source of the heavy rare earths refined in China; a curb could tighten supply of the magnets on which European wind, automotive and defence industries depend, and Europe has little refining capacity of its own.

What to watch: the trilateral talks; the published chemical list.

Sources: Reuters, Bangkok Post, The Nation, Thai PBS World
