US jury convicts Huawei of defrauding banks over its Iran business and stealing trade secrets

A federal jury in Brooklyn (New York) found Huawei guilty on Tuesday 24 November of 10 of the 12 counts it faced, including bank fraud, wire fraud, sanctions violations, conspiracy to defraud the United States and conspiracy to steal trade secrets; it acquitted the company of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice (Reuters via Investing.com; France 24/AFP; CNN). According to the Department of Justice, Huawei “lied about its activities in Iran to banks to obtain billions of US dollars and access to US-dollar clearing services”; the trade-secret counts concern source code and manuals for routers, antenna technology and robot-testing technology taken from US firms. The case began in 2018; Huawei was put on the US Entity List in 2019. Sentencing by Judge Ann Donnelly is set for 14 September 2027; penalties are not yet known. US Attorney General Todd Blanche: “No person or company is above the law.” Huawei said the acquittal on racketeering “vindicates our consistent position over the past eight years”, called the remaining charges “without legal or factual merit” and said it will take “all necessary legal actions”; its lawyer said it expects to appeal. China’s response: the Chinese embassy in Washington did not immediately comment, and there was no Foreign Ministry statement yet at the time of writing; in September the MFA said China “firmly opposes the US side’s suppression and containment of Chinese enterprises”. @F_CEAFE has not posted this story.

Why it matters for Europe: the verdict confirms how far Washington’s reach goes: a Chinese company is convicted in a US court for doing business with a third country (Iran) because that business passed through US-dollar clearing. Any European company or bank using the dollar is exposed to the same logic, which is why European firms comply with US sanctions on Iran even when the EU disagrees with them. The EU has a Blocking Statute but has never made it bite; it has no comparable tool to project its own law. On Huawei specifically, many European governments have already removed it from 5G networks at US urging, without asking for anything in return.

Key facts:

  • Court: US District Court for the Eastern District of New York; trial of more than two months, more than 60 prosecution witnesses and 15 for the defence.
  • Verdict: guilty on 10 of 12 counts; acquitted of racketeering (RICO) conspiracy and obstruction conspiracy.
  • Sentencing: 14 September 2027 (CNN/Reuters).
  • Reaction: Huawei statement rejecting the remaining charges; no Chinese government statement yet — watch today’s MFA press conference and MOFCOM.

Update: US Section 301 “overcapacity” tariffs on China, the EU and 14 others still not published

The announcement expected on Tuesday night did not come. At 07:05 Madrid, USTR’s press-release page showed nothing newer than 19 November. Overnight, AP and Reuters again reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that the administration is “set to unveil” new tariffs after its probe into excess factory capacity, covering China, the European Union and 14 other economies. Markets reacted to the prospect: US stocks fell on Tuesday, led by chipmakers, as “new US plans for tariffs reignited trade concerns” (Bloomberg), and gold held its decline as the tariff plans reinforced expectations of a further Fed rate rise (Bloomberg). Separately, Bloomberg reports that cheaper Asian steel imports are gaining ground with US buyers despite Section 232 tariffs, because the data-centre construction boom is squeezing domestic supply.

Why it matters for Europe: the EU still does not know the rate it will pay under the new legal basis, a year after accepting a 15% ceiling in its 2025 deal. Uncertainty itself is a cost for European exporters; a common EU answer must be ready before the list is published, not after.

Key facts:

  • Status at 07:05 Madrid: no USTR determination, rate or product list published.
  • 16 economies under investigation since 11 March 2026 (see 20:30 edition, topic 1).
  • Reaction: no EU or Chinese statement yet.

North Korea expels Switzerland’s envoy over Swiss sanctions on a camp linked to deported Ukrainian children

North Korea’s Foreign Ministry declared Sylvain Astier, head of Switzerland’s newly reopened diplomatic office in Pyongyang, persona non grata, gave him 48 hours to leave and demanded the office’s closure (KCNA, North Korean state media). The stated reason is Switzerland’s decision to put the Songdowon International Children’s Camp (Wonsan) on its sanctions list; Bern says the camp is part of programmes bringing Ukrainian children from Russian-occupied territory to North Korea for pro-Russian indoctrination. The UK sanctioned the camp in May 2026 and the EU in September 2026; Switzerland, which adopts EU Russia sanctions, followed. The office had reopened on 19 October during Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis’s visit (Euronews). Kim Yo Jong had threatened the move on 24 November (noted in the 08:30 edition of 24 Nov). Separately, Bloomberg reports that Russian assets frozen by Switzerland under the EU-aligned sanctions have risen to $10.2 billion.

Why it matters for Europe: a non-EU European state that aligns with EU sanctions pays a diplomatic price on its own, losing one of the few Western channels to Pyongyang. It shows both the reach of EU sanctions when partners align and the limited weight of any single European state when the target retaliates.

Key facts:

  • Retaliation: envoy expelled (48 hours) and office closure demanded, 25 November (KCNA).
  • Sanctioned entity: Songdowon International Children’s Camp — UK (May 2026), EU (September 2026), Switzerland (November 2026).
  • Swiss freeze of Russian assets: $10.2 billion (Bloomberg; date of the figure as reported on 24 November).

EU will not return to Russian energy whatever the US–Russia talks produce, says Energy Commissioner Jørgensen

EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen told Euronews (24 November) that the EU will “never again” import Russian energy, regardless of the outcome of US-led peace talks: “We will never again, if it’s up to me, import as much as one molecule from Russia.” He stressed that the EU has adopted legislation permanently phasing out Russian gas — “stronger than sanctions because sanctions have to be renewed every six months” — and that no Washington–Moscow deal can override EU law. Euronews notes that the US administration eased restrictions on Russian diesel last month and has indicated it could support Russia’s return as an energy player, including to Europe, after a peace deal. The EU has cut Russian gas from 45% to about 10% of its imports and expects to end them next year. Jørgensen warned of a difficult winter at the pump; average European gas prices rose 163% year on year in October. He rejected an EU-wide windfall tax requested by Austria, Germany, Italy, Poland, Portugal and Spain, preferring national decisions and lower electricity taxes (about 29% of the average household bill).

Why it matters for Europe: the peace table is being set in Washington and Moscow, but the EU’s energy rules are made in Brussels — the one area where the bloc has turned a sanctions policy into permanent law. The cost is real (prices up 163% year on year in October), and European dependence has partly shifted to US LNG.

Key facts:

  • Russian share of EU gas imports: from 45% (before the full-scale invasion of February 2022) to about 10% (2026); full end expected in 2027.
  • Six member states, including Spain, asked for an EU windfall levy; the Commission declined.

US–China managed trade: first agricultural working group under the “Board of Trade”, and China’s biggest Boeing order since 2017

China and the United States held the first meeting of the agricultural working group under the China–US Board of Trade in Beijing on 23–24 November, with “candid, in-depth, constructive” exchanges on farm-product regulation, market access and trade, MOFCOM said on 25 November. On the same evening, Xiamen Airlines (55% owned by China Southern) signed a contract for 50 Boeing 737-8 aircraft, list price about US$7.4 billion, with deliveries in 2031–2034; the actual price is lower because of Boeing discounts, and the deal still needs Chinese state approval (Hong Kong exchange filing). Aviation Week calls it the largest Boeing order from China since 2017. Global Times (Chinese state media) linked it to Boeing’s efforts to rebuild its China business.

Why it matters for Europe: while Washington prepares to tariff the EU as an “overcapacity” economy, it is negotiating sector-by-sector purchases with Beijing — farm goods and aircraft, the latter in direct competition with Airbus, whose China market share is a European industrial asset. A US–China managed-trade arrangement can be made at Europe’s expense.

Key facts:

  • Board of Trade first met by video on 13 November (MOFCOM, 19 November).
  • Boeing order: 50 × 737-8; catalogue US$7.4 bn (about RMB 49.7 bn); state approval and shareholder approval pending.

Iraq moves to take control of Kurdish oilfields and rejects OPEC+ capacity assessment before the weekend meeting

Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the oil ministry to manage all fields it owns that are currently under the Kurdistan Regional Government, and federal forces to take control of oilfields and border crossings in Kurdish-controlled areas, citing alleged oil smuggling (Bloomberg; Al Jazeera). Iraq has also rejected an assessment of its production capacity commissioned by OPEC+, setting up a potential clash at this weekend’s meeting on output targets (Bloomberg). Oil prices fell more than 1% on Wednesday morning as the prospect of a supply glut outweighed fears of Middle East disruption (Reuters). ExxonMobil said Guyana output will top 1 million barrels per day within weeks (posted by @F_CEAFE).

Why it matters for Europe: Europe imports almost all its oil; OPEC+ quarrels and new non-OPEC supply (Guyana, US) drive the prices Europeans pay this winter, already pressured by gas.

WHO chief calls on the US to lift its fuel blockade on Cuba

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, visiting Havana, urged the US to lift its fuel blockade on Cuba, saying “the intensification of fuel restrictions and sanctions is causing direct harm to the Cuban people, especially the most vulnerable”; the UN allocated US$2 million for immunisation and medical equipment (Al Jazeera; Reuters). OFAC issued new Cuba FAQs on 23 November (OFAC recent actions).

Why it matters for Europe: US secondary pressure on third countries’ shipping and trade with Cuba also hits European firms (notably Spanish ones present on the island), and the EU has never effectively used its Blocking Statute to protect them.
