Huawei’s patent licences exceed US$6.9 billion, with Qualcomm, HP Inc. and Harman among its licensees

At its seventh Innovation and Intellectual Property Forum in Beijing on 24 November, Huawei said that the cumulative value of its outbound patent-licensing agreements now exceeds US$6.9 billion. Its patent-licensing revenue in 2025 was about US$800 million, and it signed 20 new licensees that year in network equipment, consumer electronics and computing, IoT, automotive, streaming and semiconductors (Huawei newsroom). According to Global Times [Chinese state media], foreign companies that took licences directly from Huawei in 2025–26 include Qualcomm, HP Inc. and Harman in the US, Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen Group companies in Germany, and Seiko Solutions, Mitsubishi Fuso and Fujitec in Japan. The US deals are public. On 26 August 2026 Huawei and HP Inc. signed a multi-year global cross-licence that includes certain Huawei Wi-Fi patents. On 5 October 2026 Huawei and Qualcomm announced a multi-year, broad cross-licence covering 5G, compute, AI and networking, together with Qualcomm’s purchase of certain Huawei US patents. Huawei said that deal would close after regulatory approval (Huawei newsroom). Terms are confidential, and neither side has published amounts. A Qualcomm spokesperson said that “reports characterizing Qualcomm as a net payor on the license agreement are incorrect” and that the patents it is buying are “non-cellular US patents” (ip fray; Huawei Central). Huawei’s chief IP officer Alan Fan (Fan Zhiyong) said: “At Huawei, a patent represents more than a legal right. It represents an innovative achievement and a source of pride for the people behind it.” Huawei added that it received 3,052 US patents in 2025, ranking fourth in the US according to IFI Claims (Global Times).

Linked story (Update, US case against Huawei): on 24 November (US Eastern time) a jury in the US District Court for the Eastern District of New York convicted Huawei of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and of fraud linked to its business in Iran, but acquitted it of the racketeering (RICO) charge (AP; BBC; TIME). In its official statement Huawei said: “Huawei welcomes this decision. It confirms that these allegations lack factual basis and vindicates our consistent position over the past eight years.” It added: “We believe the government’s remaining charges are equally without legal or factual merit… We will thoroughly review the relevant verdicts and take all necessary legal actions to defend our rights and interests.” Lead defence counsel Brian Heberlig called the acquittal a “profound rejection” of the government’s case (AP; BBC; statement posted by @Huawei on X). China’s Foreign Ministry had already said on 25 November that it “firmly opposes US suppression of Chinese companies” (14:30 edition, 25 Nov).

Background (CEAFE archive): On 27 August 2026 @F_CEAFE reposted the announcement that Huawei and HP Inc. had signed a multiyear global patent cross-licence (repost), one of the licensing deals now counted in the total.

Why it matters for Europe: a company that Washington has been trying to cut off from US technology since 2019, through the Entity List and the foreign direct product rule, now collects royalties from US champions such as Qualcomm and HP. The flow of dependence in standard-essential technologies (5G, Wi-Fi, video codecs) has partly reversed. Huawei says its patented technologies were used in more than 3.2 billion 5G devices by the end of 2025. Europe’s car industry is also paying: Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen are on the licensee list. Meanwhile European governments have been excluding Huawei from 5G networks at Washington’s urging. Here is another case where Europe applies US-driven restrictions while US companies themselves keep doing business with the target. A Qualcomm purchase of Huawei US patents still needs regulatory clearance, which turns a court and export-control battle into a commercial negotiation.

Key facts

  • Cumulative value of Huawei’s outbound patent-licensing agreements: over US$6.9bn (to November 2026); licensing revenue about US$800m in 2025 (Huawei, 24 Nov).
  • Huawei says it holds over 165,000 active granted patents and has invested more than US$270bn in R&D since 2007 (Huawei statement, 25 Nov).
  • Share of active granted patents: wireless communications 41%, terminal hardware 16%, AI and computing 13% (Huawei).
  • US licensees named: Qualcomm (cross-licence plus patent purchase, 5 Oct 2026), HP Inc. (cross-licence incl. Wi-Fi, 26 Aug 2026) and Harman (named by Global Times; Harman is US-based and owned by Samsung Electronics). No amounts disclosed.
  • Qualcomm’s purchase of Huawei patents is subject to regulatory approval, which ip fray says is an antitrust (HSR Act) review.

US chip curbs and anti-distillation defences slow Chinese AI models; Moonshot looks to rent computing power in Europe

Reuters reports, citing people familiar with the matter, that Z.AI (Zhipu) has fallen behind schedule on its next flagship model, GLM-5.5, with about 3 trillion parameters. The model was due this month, and the new US rule could push it into the first quarter of 2027 or later. Moonshot AI expects the interval between its major releases to double from six months to a year or more if it loses access to overseas computing power, and its Kimi K4 could slip to around mid-2027. The cause is twofold. A Commerce Department rule in force since 19 November requires licences for Chinese access to large amounts of US-chip computing power in overseas data centres. And OpenAI and Anthropic have strengthened their defences against “distillation”, i.e. training models on the answers of stronger ones. Anthropic said in September it had seen nearly 200 million exchanges with Claude linked to suspected distillation campaigns, which it attributed to Chinese developers including Alibaba, Moonshot and Z.AI. According to Reuters, Moonshot has stepped up efforts to rent computing power in Europe and plans to approach Chinese chipmakers about training on their processors. No official Chinese response yet.

Why it matters for Europe: European data centres are becoming the next front of US extraterritorial controls. If Chinese developers turn to rented European compute, the remote-access rule and future US pressure will land on European cloud operators, who would have to police their customers on Washington’s terms. This follows the Nvidia shipment freeze to South-East Asia (topic 2, 08:30 edition).

Key facts

  • US Commerce compute-access licensing rule in force since 19 Nov 2026 (Reuters).
  • Z.AI released GLM-5.4 on 23 Nov, built on a 750bn-parameter model (Reuters).
  • Moonshot releases: K2 (July 2025), K2.5 (January 2026), K3 (July 2026) (Reuters).

Update: UniCredit obtains every clearance for Commerzbank; new shares to be credited by 7 December

UniCredit announced at 07:45 on 26 November that the Polish Financial Supervision Authority (KNF) has approved the indirect change of control of Commerzbank’s Polish operations, including mBank (69.1% owned by Commerzbank, about 5.9 million customers). With that, all regulatory clearances are in place and all conditions of the exchange offer launched on 5 May 2026 are met. New UniCredit shares for the tendered Commerzbank shares are expected to be credited by 7 December 2026. UniCredit has asked Commerzbank to convene an extraordinary general meeting to elect new shareholder representatives to the supervisory board. The European Commission had cleared the deal unconditionally on 16 November, and the US Federal Reserve also approved it (Euronews). The breakthrough came from the deal with Berlin in mid-November. In a letter of 18 November to Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil, CEO Andrea Orcel set out eight “binding principles”: Frankfurt as German headquarters, keeping the listing and the AG legal form, German nationals on UniCredit’s board from the 2027 renewal, and support for Mittelstand exporters. The German state still holds about 13.3% of Commerzbank and has not announced a sale. Reuters Breakingviews values the deal at nearly US$45bn.

Why it matters for Europe: this is a major cross-border banking combination inside the euro area. It shows that European champions can be built across borders, though only after two years of political resistance from Berlin. It is an example of the intra-European consolidation CEAFE considers necessary, since no single member state’s banking sector can match the scale of the US giants on its own. Not posted by @F_CEAFE.

Update: Beijing answers the EU hybrid-car safeguard: “protectionism leads nowhere”

At MOFCOM’s regular press conference on 26 November, spokesperson He Yadong responded to the Commission’s preliminary ruling of 24 November. The ruling sets provisional quotas for hybrid vehicles from 1 December for up to 200 days, with China’s share about 192,000 units during the period (350,000 annualised), the largest of nine suppliers. He said the two sides had held pre-initiation consultations and “reached an understanding on hybrid vehicle trade arrangements” on three principles: WTO compliance, non-discrimination and respect for facts. China “hopes the EU will earnestly abide by the consensus” and investigate fairly under WTO rules, and “will keep a close watch… and firmly protect the legitimate rights and interests of Chinese industries” (MOFCOM; Xinhua [Chinese state media]). An opinion piece in Global Times [Chinese state media] the same day accused the Commission of having “waved the white flag” to Washington’s tariffs while acting as its “accomplice”.

Why it matters for Europe: the measure (14:30 edition, 25 Nov) is a global safeguard, not an anti-China duty, and Beijing’s tone is measured because it says a prior understanding exists. CEAFE’s point is the contrast: Brussels uses trade defence against China while accepting the US Section 301 “overcapacity” tariff of 15% without retaliation.

Gulf supply shock: Houthi strike on Ras Tanura and mines in Hormuz lift Brent towards US$95; Qatar’s LNG exports reach a war-time high at much higher insurance costs

Brent closed 3.5% higher on Wednesday 25 November and WTI 2% higher, after Yemen’s Houthis said they had attacked Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery and Riyadh’s financial district. Iranian state media and the Iranian army, cited by Fars, said tankers struck mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz. Early on Thursday Brent traded at US$94.71 and WTI at US$84.03 (CNBC/Reuters). Prices later eased (Reuters, 13:00 Madrid). OPEC+ ministers meet on 29 November to set 2027 output, without the UAE, which has left the group. Qatar’s LNG exports are on track for their busiest month since the US–Iran war began at the end of February. Kpler counts 36 berthings at Ras Laffan so far in November, against 35 in October and 21 in September. Exports remain at around 40% of pre-war levels (analysts via Reuters), or about half (Bloomberg). War-risk insurance for ships entering the Gulf has risen from about 0.25% of hull value at the start of 2026 to as much as 5%, adding US$2.5–12.5 million per voyage for a modern US$250 million LNG carrier (Wood Mackenzie via Reuters).

Why it matters for Europe: Europe competes with Asia for the same scarce LNG cargoes, and the insurance premium ends up in European gas and electricity prices. As ECFR warned on 25 November, the US could supply up to 80% of EU LNG imports by 2030. Every Gulf shock deepens Europe’s dependence on US LNG.

Germany’s 2027 budget: record €561 billion, €210 billion of borrowing and €139 billion for defence, more than double France’s €63.4 billion

The Bundestag is holding its final debates on the 2027 federal budget. Spending will be just under €561bn, a record, with record borrowing of about €210bn (DW). Defence gets €109bn in the core budget, €27bn more than in 2026. With the debt-financed Bundeswehr special fund, the total reaches €139bn (DW; Defence24 gives €139.4bn = €109.7bn + €29.6bn). France’s 2027 defence budget is €63.4bn, up from €57bn in 2026 (Politico Europe). Chancellor Merz said that “if we don’t change course, in a few years… all federal revenue will have to be spent on social programmes, interest payments, and defence.” The labour and social affairs budget exceeds €200bn. The AfD called it a “debt budget” and attacked aid to Ukraine (posts reposted by @F_CEAFE).

CEAFE comment: No member state, on its own, has the scale of the powers it hopes to sit beside at the same table.

Why it matters for Europe: a single member state spending more than twice as much as France changes the balance within the EU. Even so, it does so on debt and at the expense of social spending, proof that no member state can carry rearmament alone. The case for common European financing and joint procurement is stronger than for national budget races.

Key facts

  • 2027 federal spending about €561bn; new borrowing about €210bn (DW, 25 Nov).
  • Defence 2027: €109bn core + special fund = €139bn (DW); France €63.4bn (Politico Europe).
