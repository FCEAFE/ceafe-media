## Update: Nvidia chips for China: US senator demands "hard questions" for Commerce Secretary Lutnick

**Republican Senator John Kennedy said on Sunday that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick should face "hard questions" from Congress about whether his department allowed Chinese companies to "circumvent" export rules and obtain Nvidia AI chips (The Washington Post).**

"It appears to me, I emphasize 'appears', that the Department of Commerce has allowed the Communist Party of China…", he said. The remarks follow Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's statement that Washington "still needs answers" from Nvidia (20:30 edition of 22 November) and the House Foreign Affairs Committee's disclosure of 879,868 Nvidia and AMD chips licensed by the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) for China (20 November). No new BIS or OFAC action was published in the window.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the debate in Washington is now whether Commerce licensed too much to China; any tightening would also apply to data centres in EU member states under the BIS remote-access rule of 16 November, a decision in which Europe has no say.

**What to watch:** whether Congress calls Lutnick to testify; any BIS tightening; China's reaction at Monday's MFA briefing.

## Update: AI sell-off spreads to Asia: Kospi falls as much as 5.8%

**Asian markets extended Friday's tech-led rout on Monday: Seoul's Kospi, dominated by Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, slid as much as 5.8% before paring losses to 3.2% at 05:30 Madrid time (Al Jazeera).**

The Hang Seng and the CSI 300 fell about 0.6%, while Taiwan's Taiex reversed to a 2.5% gain; Tokyo was closed for a holiday. On Friday the S&P 500 had closed 3.7% lower, entering a correction, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 5.3%; on Monday morning S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures were up 2.2% and 2.9% (Al Jazeera). The WSJ reports the debt market expects Anthropic's bonds to be rated junk. South Korea's annual exports passed $1 trillion for the first time, with chip exports in the first 20 days of November up 294.4% year on year (Bloomberg).

**Context:** the correction began after Anthropic's market debut on 19 November.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the boom and the correction both turn on US and Asian chip and AI companies; Europe has few large players in the race.

**What to watch:** the European open; Tokyo's reopening on Tuesday; chip stocks.

## Europe's forward power prices near €200/MWh: a German steel mill stops by the hour

**Forward power contracts for each of the next three months are trading around €200 per megawatt-hour, roughly double last winter's levels, driven by surging gas prices, Bloomberg reports in what it calls "Europe's second major energy crisis in less than five years".**

The Feralpi Group steel plant in Riesa, Germany, owned by Italy's Feralpi, checks next-day power prices twice a day and has repeatedly halted production this autumn even with orders to fill. European gas rose early on Monday on forecasts of colder weather, with inventories already depleted by export disruptions linked to the US–Iran war (Bloomberg). Libya's oil output dropped after a pipeline from its largest field was disrupted.

**Context:** EU gas storage stood at 70.5% on 21 November, and the TTF benchmark had been near €90/MWh last week (21 November edition).

**Why it matters for Europe:** energy-intensive industry in EU member states stops producing when power prices spike; Europe replaced much of its Russian gas with US LNG and pays the price premium of the Gulf war.

**What to watch:** today's TTF settlement; EU storage withdrawals.

## Update: COP31 ends without an agreement; the EU objects to a text without fossil fuels

**COP31 in Antalya closed on Sunday without an overarching agreement after two days of overtime, the longest UN climate summit on record (UN News).**

The European Union formally objected to the cover decision, saying it could not accept a text that omitted any reference to fossil fuels or a transition away from them, an omission it called "unconscionable"; the Marshall Islands also withheld support. Saudi Arabia, for the Arab Group, defended references to carbon capture; Reuters reports that Saudi influence loomed large over the outcome. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was disappointed, and China (Global Times, Chinese state media) voiced "deep disappointment". COP32 will be held in Addis Ababa.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the EU held its position but could not carry the conference, while its own climate rules weigh on energy costs at home (see the previous story).

**What to watch:** EU Council conclusions; the debate on the EU's 2040 target.
