Lukoil International changes hands: a US-led consortium with a direct US Government stake signs to buy Lukoil’s foreign assets, including refineries in Bulgaria, the Netherlands and Romania

Lede. A consortium led by Todd Boehly’s Eldridge signed a definitive agreement to acquire LUKOIL International GmbH, the portfolio of Russia’s Lukoil outside Russia. Its partners are the US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC), which takes an equity interest and a board seat, UAE-based Catena Investment Holding and Xtellus Partners. The consortium says the aim is “to secure critical global energy assets under American and allied ownership”.

Context. Payment is a swap of frozen Lukoil shares (restricted Lukoil and other Russian shares) worth about $10 billion at face value, held in a UAE custodial account for five years or until sanctions are lifted (Reuters). The portfolio includes three refineries — Burgas in Bulgaria (the largest in the Balkans), Zeeland in the Netherlands and Petrotel in Romania — with about 350,000 barrels a day of capacity. It also includes the LITASCO trading arm, more than 2,000 retail stations in 16 countries and upstream production of about 350,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day. Reuters values the portfolio at about $16 billion. CSIS puts the DFC’s profits interest at about 15%, with no upfront cost. A DFC official told Reuters that “moving strategic energy infrastructure assets into US or allied partners’ hands is a key policy priority of the administration”. The deal is pending approval by the US Treasury’s OFAC and other regulators; closing could take up to a year. CSIS notes that the US Government is “both bidder and decider”.

Background (CEAFE archive): @F_CEAFE has followed the sale since Lukoil was sanctioned by OFAC on 22 October 2025: Carlyle (13 Nov. 2025) and Chevron (18 Nov. 2025) explored bids, Kazakhstan asked Washington for the local assets (28 Jan. 2026), and Reuters reported Boehly’s planned securities swap on 3 November 2026.

CEAFE comment: Figures that illustrate, once again, why European sovereignty is only credible if it is exercised together.

Why it matters for Europe: refineries on EU soil and fuel networks in the Balkans and Northwest Europe move from Russian to US control, with a direct stake for the US Government and the final decision taken in Washington by OFAC. No European buyer leads the deal. Europe swaps one energy dependency for another and deepens its dependence on the United States, which is also set to become its main LNG supplier (see LNG item below). The case strengthens the argument for investment screening in Bulgaria, the Netherlands and Romania and for a common policy of European energy sovereignty.

What to watch: the OFAC review and other regulatory approvals; the positions of the Bulgarian, Dutch and Romanian governments and of the Commission; any reaction from the Kremlin or Lukoil (none found by 19:00 Madrid).

Tariffs — USTR proposes a 10% tariff on Brazilian aircraft; Embraer falls almost 10%

In a separate step on the same day, USTR proposed to modify its Section 301 action against Brazil by ending the exemption for finished aircraft and applying a 10% ad valorem tariff on Brazilian aircraft imports, including Embraer’s (USTR Federal Register notice; Aviation Week). Comments are due by 30 December 2026, with a public hearing on 14 January 2027. Embraer shares fell almost 10% in São Paulo (Bloomberg); the company said it is analysing the proposal and defends the long-standing zero-tariff regime for civil aircraft (G1).

Why it matters for Europe: the zero-tariff regime for civil aircraft is also what protects Airbus’s US sales; Washington is showing it will break it as leverage on a third country.

Energy — Le Figaro/ECFR: the US could supply 80% of the EU’s LNG imports by 2030

Le Figaro reports, citing an ECFR study, that EU import dependence stands at 96.6% for oil products and 88% for gas, and that the US, which supplied 58% of EU LNG imports in 2025, could reach up to 80% by 2030. The think-tank IEEFA had projected the same 75–80% LNG share by 2030 if contracted US volumes materialise, equivalent to about 40% of total EU gas imports (not 80% of all gas, as some posts claim). @F_CEAFE reposted a post on the Figaro piece (“traded a bear for an eagle”). Separately, Bloomberg reports restrictions on tankers entering waters near some of the UAE’s main export hubs, as the Gulf producer protects its infrastructure; Houthi drone strikes on Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery reported by accounts @F_CEAFE reposted are not independently verified here (see the Conflicts section).

Why it matters for Europe: it quantifies the CEAFE energy line: Russia replaced by a single US supplier, at prices several times those paid by US industry.

Key facts

  • EU import dependence: 96.6% oil products, 88% gas (ECFR via Le Figaro).
  • US share of EU LNG imports: 58% in 2025; up to 80% by 2030 (Le Figaro; IEEFA).

Macro — US Q3 growth revised up to 4.3%; AI sell-off puts Nasdaq on course for worst November since 2008

The BEA’s second estimate puts US real GDP growth in the third quarter at an annualised 4.3%, revised up 0.9 points from the advance estimate on stronger investment and consumer spending (Q2: 2.2%). Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 5.8%. Reuters and AFP report that inflation is broadening, with the Fed’s PCE gauge at a three-year high (AFP). On markets, the Nasdaq sank about 4% and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq were on track for their worst November since 2008, with chipmakers leading (Reuters).

Why it matters for Europe: a US economy growing above 4% while the euro area’s largest economy grew 0.1% in Q3 (14:30 edition) widens the transatlantic growth gap that underpins investment flows out of Europe.

Technology sovereignty — Commission proposes the Quantum Act and a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund

The Commission proposed the Quantum Act, built on three pillars (research excellence, uptake of quantum technology, secure systems and supply chains) and delivered through a “Quantum Europe Framework Initiative” aligning EU and national programmes, including preparation for post-quantum cryptography (Commission press release). The proposal also lays the groundwork for a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund (STEF); the Brussels outlet 2eu.brussels reports an indicated €50bn target, a figure not in the Commission release excerpt read here.

CEAFE comment: These contrasts explain why CEAFE insists that Europeans organise ourselves as a single actor, not as twenty-seven markets.

Why it matters for Europe: quantum is one of the few frontier fields where Europe leads in research; the Act is an attempt to stop that lead from turning into US-owned products, as in AI and cloud.

Banks are offering CVC Capital Partners up to €11bn in debt financing for a possible take-private of the home-security group Verisure; no bid has been made and Hellman & Friedman (US), which owns about 44%, is expected to stay invested (Bloomberg via swissinfo).
