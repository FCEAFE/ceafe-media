## Colby to EU ministers: the US "strongly opposes" excluding American firms from European arms markets

**Elbridge Colby, US Under Secretary of War for Policy, addressed the EU Foreign Affairs Council (Defence) and then reporters at NATO headquarters on 17 November.**

In his prepared remarks (published by the US Department of War) he said Washington is "more receptive to the EU in this arena than American governments have been in the past, provided the EU approach is non-exclusionary", but that "we strongly oppose measures to exclude American firms from competition in European markets", extending the point to other non-EU NATO allies. He argued that excluding "the world's highest quality defense industrial base" would be "self-defeating", and offered instead "transatlantic joint ventures, licensed co-production, and co-development", especially in integrated air and missile defence, artillery and precision munitions, and supply chains. To reporters (Reuters) he added: "American companies make the best weaponry in the world. If you're going to have a secure Europe in NATO 3.0, you need things like the F-35." He said a review of US force posture in Europe should still meet its 1 December deadline; Germany hosts about 35,000 of the 80,000 US troops in Europe.

**Why it matters for Europe.** This is a direct US intervention in the design of EU defence-industrial policy (European preference in SAFE, EDIP and EDF). Washington asks Europe to stay open while its own rules keep its market closed: Buy American requirements, ITAR controls on any US component, CFIUS screening and FOCI arrangements that oblige foreign firms to set up US subsidiaries with US boards before they can win Pentagon contracts. A "pragmatic, balanced relationship", in CEAFE's reading, would mean the same rules on both sides of the Atlantic. Licensed co-production keeps the design authority, the export veto and often the software keys in the US. Colby himself said "many European states share our opposition", which shows how a divided EU can be played off against its own industrial policy.

**What to watch.** Watch whether the Commission or member states respond to Colby. Also watch the European-preference clauses in EDIP and SAFE, the US posture decision (1 Dec) and the UK–EU summit on SAFE participation (20 Nov).

## State Department completes ITAR "reform sprint": a general licence for trusted partners and a smaller Munitions List

**The State Department said on 17 November that a 90-day sprint (30 July – 28 October), led by Under Secretary Thomas G. DiNanno, produced a dozen regulatory actions to reform the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR).**

Eight Federal Register notices are already published. The reforms "right-size" the US Munitions List (USML) so that controls cover only technologies with "a critical military or intelligence advantage". They also create an "ITAR General License" that will pre-approve transfers of some defence articles to "some of America's most trusted partner governments" (not named). Licence decisions would target 50 days instead of 60 where practicable, with a presumption of approval for clean applications. The department adjudicated about 25,000 ITAR licences worth about $200bn in FY2026. It frames the work under Executive Orders 14268 (April 2025) and 14383 ("America First Arms Transfer Strategy", Feb 2026). Next on the list are FMS process reform and US export policy under the Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR). An October proposal moving some items from the USML to Commerce controls is open for comment until 30 November (IANS).

**Why it matters for Europe.** The aim is stated plainly: "Every unnecessary regulatory burden pushes America's partners toward foreign suppliers." Easier ITAR licences take away one of the strongest selling points of European kit, the "ITAR-free" label (see MBDA–CEA IRRIS, 20:30 edition of 17 November). The US keeps the power that matters: it decides who is "trusted", and items moved to Commerce stay under US jurisdiction and its extraterritorial reach, Entity List included. Read together with Colby, Washington is lowering its own export friction while asking Europe to drop its preference rules.

**What to watch.** Watch which governments make the General License list (whether EU states are included, and all or only some), the Federal Register texts, and the comment deadline of 30 November.

**Sources:** [State Department release, 17 Nov 2026](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesman/2026/11/department-of-state-completes-90-day-itar-reform-sprint-delivering-historic-reforms-to-strengthen-american-export-dominance), [IANS](https://ianslive.in/us-plans-faster-arms-export-approvals-new-license-route-for-trusted-partners--20261118051202), [post](https://x.com/i/status/2122817940517195975), [@UnderSecT](https://x.com/i/status/2122815878270177285)

## Arms sales: Albania cleared to buy HIMARS with US loans ($210m)

**The State Department approved a possible Foreign Military Sale (FMS) to Albania on 17 November: two M142 HIMARS launchers, six M31A2 GMLRS unitary pods and six M30A2 alternative-warhead pods (72 rockets), eight IFATDS fire-control systems, two HMMWVs, radios and support.**

The estimated cost is $210m, paid with US Foreign Military Financing **loan** funds. The principal contractor is Lockheed Martin (Grand Prairie, Texas), and no offset is known. Up to 15 US government and 15 contractor staff would travel to Albania about twice a year. Congress has the usual review period before a Letter of Offer and Acceptance.

**Why it matters for Europe.** This is another European NATO member buying a US system, paid for with US credit, which ties Albania to US munitions, fire-control software and support for decades. Europe has no fully home-grown rocket-artillery system in series production. The nearest options are hybrids (Rheinmetall–Lockheed Martin GMARS; KNDS Deutschland–Elbit EuroPULS on an Israeli design), a gap the EU's joint projects have not yet closed. Small buyers like Albania go where the financing is.

**Sources:** [State Department notification](https://www.state.gov/releases/bureau-of-political-military-affairs/2026/11/albania-m142-high-mobility-artillery-rocket-systems), [Reuters via Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/international/4008647-us-approves-potential-210-million-artilleryrocket-systemssale-to-albania), [post](https://x.com/i/status/2122832968162927001)

## US firms move into NATO space: HawkEye 360 wins a NATO SIGINT order via Luxembourg

**HawkEye 360 (US, NYSE: HAWK) said on 17 November that Luxembourg's Directorate of Defence chose NATO's new Global Data Marketplace for Space as the channel to buy its commercial radio-frequency (RF) intelligence for the NATO Space Theatre Component.**

The company points to its Luxembourg office and partnerships with Leonardo and ERA. NATO launched the marketplace the same day (NATO). *Why it matters:* the first marketplace order publicised by a supplier goes to a US firm, through a European government. European RF-geolocation operators exist (e.g. France's Unseenlabs), and CEAFE would expect a European-preference test here. [HawkEye 360 release](https://www.he360.com/hawkeye-360-announces-agreement-to-support-nato-space-operations/), [post](https://x.com/i/status/2122798183164055858).
- UK–US frigate. UK Defence Readiness and Industry Minister Luke Pollard lobbied in Washington for US production of a version of Babcock's Arrowhead 140 (the Type 31 design) to fill a US Navy capability gap. UK Defence in the US reposted an interview (@tomhfh) describing the pitch as a British-designed frigate "to be built in Florida by Americans". Earlier reporting points to licence production with HII, whose yards are in Virginia and Mississippi; the Florida claim is not confirmed by these sources. *Why it matters:* a European design sold to the US is welcome, but the deal model shows the asymmetry. The US requires its ships to be built in US yards with US jobs, while it asks Europe to buy US kit off the shelf. [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4806290/uk-special-relationship-joint-us-frigate-production/), [Defence War Room](https://defensewarroom.com/2026/10/26/babcock-arrowhead-140-frigate-us-navy/), [post](https://x.com/i/status/2122771463706943995).
