## Eutelsat wins up to $64.46m to supply OneWeb connectivity to the US armed forces

**The US arm of the French-led operator Eutelsat has a two-year contract with the US Space Force worth more than $60m, European Spaceflight reports.**

US procurement records put the ceiling at $64.46m, with $6.1m committed so far. The contract covers a shared pool of OneWeb low Earth orbit capacity, alternative positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) and technical support. It was awarded in June and announced on 17 November. According to the Space Force justification, the US military has already bought more than 500 OneWeb terminals.

*CEAFE:* Without a common industrial, technological and defence base, Europe will remain dependent on decisions taken in Washington or Beijing.

**Context:** Antecedentes: on 29 March 2023 @F_CEAFE shared an analysis on whether Eutelsat-OneWeb and the EU's sovereign connectivity constellation would "ever align" ([X, 2023](https://x.com/i/status/1641027201318387713)). The usual flow runs the other way, with European forces buying Starlink/Starshield services.

**Why it matters for Europe:** a constellation built up with European capital is now also serving US military programmes. Eutelsat does so through a US subsidiary, the FOCI-type arrangement Washington requires of foreign suppliers; the EU has no equivalent requirement for US firms. The contract also adds weight to OneWeb, and to IRIS², as European options for European armed forces.

**What to watch:** how much of the $64.46m ceiling is drawn down; OneWeb's role in IRIS²; further US orders for terminals.

**Sources:** [European Spaceflight](https://europeanspaceflight.com/eutelsat-wins-60-million-us-space-force-contract/), [Andrew Parsonson on X](https://x.com/i/status/2124881761071219129)

## HENSOLDT and THEON open a new night-vision plant in Wetzlar for the OCCAR programme

**HENSOLDT THEON NightVision GmbH, the joint venture of Germany's HENSOLDT Optronics and Greece's THEON, has opened a new production facility in Wetzlar, Germany.**

HENSOLDT describes it as a multi-million-euro investment. The plant raises output for the Night Vision programme managed by OCCAR, the European organisation for joint armament cooperation. The programme began in 2022 with MIKRON night-vision goggle sets for the German and Belgian armed forces. It is open to other European countries interested in joining.

*CEAFE:* Acting together is not a political ornament: it is the only way to reach a scale that others already have.

**Context:** Germany, Belgium and Greece are EU member states and NATO members. OCCAR manages cooperative programmes on behalf of its participating nations.

**Why it matters for Europe:** a German–Greek joint venture supplying several European armies through a joint procurement framework keeps both production and know-how inside Europe. It is the kind of intra-European procurement CEAFE considers positive.

**What to watch:** which other European countries join the programme; new orders placed through OCCAR.

**Sources:** [HENSOLDT](https://www.hensoldt.net/news/hensoldt-theon-nightvision-expands-production-capacity-in-germany-to-support-major-european-night-vision-programme), [HENSOLDT on X](https://x.com/i/status/2124888734927569215)

## Update: The UK signs NATO's STARLIFT launch initiative and backs SaxaVord with $39m

**The UK has signed NATO's STARLIFT initiative for a network of flexible satellite launch services and is backing it with $39m for SaxaVord Spaceport in Shetland, the UK Ministry of Defence announced.**

SaxaVord is the UK's fully licensed vertical orbital launch site. STARLIFT now has eleven nations. London says the announcement builds on the UK Space Strategy, which sets out more than £7.8bn for space. On the same day, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said it would be "unthinkable" to rejoin the EU without a new referendum.

**Context:** Antecedentes: on 17 November @F_CEAFE reported the launch of STARLIFT, with ten NATO Allies, including the UK, and the Republic of Korea committing to finance it ([X, 2026](https://x.com/i/status/2122769519185629490)). The UK is a NATO member and no longer an EU member state.

**Why it matters for Europe:** STARLIFT gives European spaceports such as SaxaVord, Andøya and Esrange a NATO role. London is placing its launch capacity in a NATO framework rather than a European one, while Europe is short of sovereign launch capacity (Ariane 6, Vega C).

**What to watch:** whether STARLIFT links to ESA or EU launch programmes; whether it serves European launchers (Isar, RFA, Orbex, PLD) or US ones.

**Sources:** [UK Government](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-reaches-for-stars-with-nato-space-launch-plan), [UK Defence in the US on X](https://x.com/i/status/2124925165196738974), [Politico Europe](https://www.politico.eu/article/rejoining-eu-without-referendum-unthinkable-andy-burnham/)

## European space round-up: IRIDE at Kourou, Ariane 6 rescheduled, Isar Aerospace in Canada

**The first batch of Italy's IRIDE Earth-observation satellites, including six synthetic aperture radar (SAR) satellites, has arrived at the European Spaceport in French Guiana for the final phase of preparations ahead of launch, Thales Alenia Space reports.**

**Ariane 6:** after a technical event on 23 November affecting the launcher's ground support systems, Arianespace says the necessary analyses and checks have been carried out. The VA271 launch, carrying 36 Amazon Leo satellites, is now scheduled for 24 November, in a window from 11:45 to 12:14 Madrid time. Arianespace says the launcher and satellites remain in stable and safe conditions.

**Isar Aerospace:** the German launcher company has started building a launch complex at Spaceport Nova Scotia (Maritime Launch), aiming for orbital launches from Canadian soil from 2028.

**Also:** ESA's Hera released imagery of Dimorphos casting a shadow on its parent asteroid Didymos; Leonardo has completed flight testing for AW09 certification.

**Why it matters for Europe:** IRIDE and Ariane 6 are pieces of European autonomy in Earth observation and access to space. The next Ariane 6 mission carries a US customer's constellation, and a German launcher is building capacity outside Europe.

**What to watch:** the Ariane 6 launch on 24 November; the IRIDE launch date.
