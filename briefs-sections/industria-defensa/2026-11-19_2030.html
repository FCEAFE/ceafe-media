Poland and Greece will buy radar satellites together with EU SAFE loans

On 19 November in Warsaw, the defence ministries of Poland and Greece signed a Memorandum of Understanding.

It sets up a framework to buy SAR (synthetic aperture radar) Earth-observation satellites together, using part of each country’s loan under the EU’s SAFE instrument, the Polish ministry says. The memorandum lets Poland’s Armament Agency add Greece to the MikroSAR contract it signed in May 2025 with a consortium of ICEYE Polska and the state-owned Wojskowe Zakłady Łączności Nr 1 (WZŁ-1). The ministry notes that it does not yet oblige either side to sign a framework or delivery contract. A Polish–Greek Defence Industry Day held alongside it brought together 84 companies (53 Polish, 31 Greek). Deputy Defence Minister Magdalena Sobkowiak-Czarnecka wrote: “This time the Greeks are buying our satellite system. Within SAFE, of course.”

Why it matters for Europe. This is the kind of joint purchase SAFE was designed for: two member states pooling demand for a European product instead of buying national systems or US imagery. It also shows that European space capacity can be built inside the EU, at a time when ICEYE itself is bidding €2.05bn for Avio S.p.A., the Vega maker. Joint orders like this one, not 27 separate national ones, give European space firms the scale to compete with US providers. Here, once again, Europeans buying together make a stronger market than any one of them alone.

What to watch. Whether Greece signs on to the MikroSAR contract itself, and how many satellites it would order.

The Netherlands and Germany order HENSOLDT’s Knifefish jammer in series production

HENSOLDT will supply its Knifefish electromagnetic-warfare system in series production to the armed forces of the Netherlands and Germany.

The Dutch materiel command COMMIT, also signing for Germany, and HENSOLDT Netherlands signed the call-off on 19 November at the NEDS fair in Rotterdam. It is the first option under a framework agreement concluded in June. Knifefish jams enemy radio links, including drone control and command radio. It will be fitted to GDELS’s Piranha 5 wheeled vehicle and works with HENSOLDT’s Sawfish reconnaissance system. On the same day, HENSOLDT signed an industrial participation agreement with the Dutch Ministry of Economic Affairs for the programme. No contract value was given.

Why it matters for Europe. One country buying for two, from a European supplier, for a European vehicle, is the common procurement Europe needs. Electronic warfare against drones is now a frontline need, and here it is met by a European product rather than a US one.

What to watch. NEDS also brought a Rheinmetall–Netherlands agreement on military vehicle technology (@RheinmetallAG https://x.com/i/status/2123428081478119539; the press-release link returned 404 when checked).

Kongsberg joins Ukraine’s Brave Prime industry programme

Norway’s Kongsberg signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Brave1, Ukraine’s government-backed defence-technology cluster, in Kyiv.

That makes it the third foreign defence company in Brave1’s Brave Prime initiative. The partnership will look at Kongsberg technologies that meet Ukraine’s needs and test them under Brave1’s “Test in Ukraine” framework. It will also match Kongsberg with Ukrainian firms to integrate their technologies and components, and identify areas for joint research and development. Kongsberg’s NASAMS air defence has been in use in Ukraine since 2022, and the company opened a permanent office in Kyiv in 2025.

Why it matters for Europe. Bringing Ukraine’s fast, battle-tested defence-tech sector together with European primes builds a European supply base, and keeps it from becoming a showroom for US firms.

Sources: kongsberg.com, · @kongsbergasa

France orders 72,000 HK416F rifles and receives its third Albatros patrol aircraft

France’s procurement agency DGA has placed a ten-year order with Heckler & Koch France for 72,000 HK416F rifles, notified on 2 November 2026.

The rifle is the core of France’s AIF individual weapons programme. The order also includes silencers and optical sights. More than 100,000 rifles have been delivered since 2017 to replace the FAMAS. Separately, the DGA delivered the third Falcon 2000 Albatros maritime surveillance aircraft from Dassault Aviation to the French Navy on 16 November. This meets the target of three deliveries in 2026, and two more will follow each year. The AVSIMAR programme covers 12 aircraft, which will replace eight Falcon 50M and five Falcon 200 Gardian.

Why it matters for Europe. Both are European supply chains: a German rifle design made by H&K’s French subsidiary, and a French business jet adapted for maritime patrol.

European exports: Argentina eyes Fincantieri corvettes, Malaysia KNDS light guns

Argentina’s defence minister signed a Letter of Intent in Rome to acquire up to three Fincantieri FCX20 corvettes, in full configuration with an integrated logistic support package, Fincantieri says.

Malaysia handed KNDS a letter of intent to buy 105LG light artillery systems, KNDS France reports. Neither statement gives a value or quantity beyond these details.

Why it matters for Europe. Exports spread the cost of European production lines. In both markets, Europeans compete with US, Turkish and Asian suppliers.

Sources: @Fincantieri, · @KNDS_France

GKN Aerospace and MBDA explore cooperation on missile and launcher structures

GKN Aerospace and MBDA have signed a Letter of Intent to explore working together on missile and launcher systems.

It combines MBDA’s missile-system expertise with GKN’s lightweight composite canisters and structures. GKN Aerospace is British (owned by Melrose), and MBDA is jointly owned by Airbus, BAE Systems and Leonardo.

Why it matters for Europe. Europe’s missile production is short of capacity, and canisters and structures are a bottleneck. Keeping that supply chain European keeps it free of ITAR.

Sources: @GKNAero
