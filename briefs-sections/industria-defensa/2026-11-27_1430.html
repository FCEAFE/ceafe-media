Poland signs with Palantir for the Maven Smart System (“Saturn”): a US AI targeting backbone for its army

Poland’s Ministry of National Defence signed an agreement on 27 November in Warsaw, with the defence minister present, with Palantir Technologies Poland to supply the Maven Smart System, called “Saturn” in Poland. The AI platform fuses data from drones, satellites, radars and reconnaissance for command, targeting and decision support. The ministry says the data stay on army infrastructure and under Polish control. The value and duration were not disclosed (AIPORT). Palantir said: “We are extremely proud to stand with Poland… Maven Smart System is universally recognized as the vanguard of next-generation AI warfare.” Polish critics (Nasze Dane, the Panoptykon Foundation and Digital Affairs Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski) asked for an independent audit. The Polish account @zofia_malisz claims that Germany, France and Denmark have already dropped Palantir and that the UK is limiting it (her claim, not confirmed by official sources).

Background (CEAFE archive): the contract follows a December 2024 cyber memorandum and an October 2025 letter of intent, when Palantir said it was “committed to Poland” (https://x.com/i/status/1982921755401519177). On 16 November CEAFE shared Reuters’ report that Europe is hunting for Palantir alternatives as militaries seek to limit US dependence (https://x.com/i/status/2122265332274319486).

Why it matters for Europe: the largest EU army on NATO’s eastern flank puts its data-fusion and targeting layer on a US proprietary platform, a deep software and data dependence subject to US law. European alternatives exist, such as Helsing (Germany) and the command-and-control and data platforms of Thales and Airbus Defence and Space. The US would not let a foreign firm run the Pentagon’s targeting cloud.

What to watch: whether the value is published; the audit demanded by critics; SIPRI’s new report “Mapping the Military Artificial Intelligence Industry”.

Thales enters exclusive talks to take control of French photonics firm Lumibird

Thales (France) has entered exclusive negotiations to acquire a 51.6% controlling stake in Lumibird (France), a European laser and photonics specialist, aiming to “lay the foundations of a European photonics platform”. The offer values the shares at €30.10, including an €11.34 exceptional distribution linked to the sale of Lumibird’s Medical division (€18.76 in cash excluding the distribution; earn-outs could take the total to €32.66), according to Il Sole 24 Ore and the companies’ release. Lumibird shares jumped about 17% (Investing.com).

Background: Lumibird’s technologies (rangefinders, designators, lidar and building blocks for directed-energy systems) are part of the counter-drone and laser race covered on 26 November (US–Israel laser trial, 14:30 edition).

Why it matters for Europe: a European buyer consolidating a strategic European laser and photonics supplier keeps the technology in Europe. It is not a US takeover; CEAFE sees intra-European consolidation as positive.

What to watch: works-council consultation and regulatory approvals; whether the offer extends to the remaining shareholders.

France launches its counter-drone pact with industry and receives its first sovereign GOBI Zephyr interceptors

Junior armed forces minister Alice Rufo launched, with the DGA procurement agency and the GICAT industry association, the counter-drone strand of France’s “drone pact” with industry, open to start-ups and primes alike and running to summer 2027, with continuous testing through the CeRLAD centre (Defense News, GICAT). Rufo called the Ukraine and Middle East wars a “quite brutal reality check” and said French forces urgently need counter-drone capabilities. In parallel, the DGA received the first GOBI Zephyr interceptor drones built by French start-up Harmattan AI, two months after ordering them: kinetic hit-to-kill without an explosive charge, a 10 km radius and 350 km/h, day and night, launched after human validation.

Background (CEAFE archive): in January CEAFE shared Dassault Aviation’s announcement that it had led Harmattan AI’s $200 million Series B round (https://x.com/i/status/2010694974967320747).

Why it matters for Europe: this is fast, sovereign European counter-drone procurement, from order to delivery in two months, and it contrasts with the purchase of US software announced in Warsaw the same day.

What to watch: the first projects selected under the pact; follow-on orders of GOBI Zephyr.

OCCAR orders a third Vulcano-class logistic ship for the Italian Navy from Fincantieri and Leonardo

OCCAR, the European organisation for joint armament cooperation, has ordered a third Vulcano-class logistic support ship, Prometeo (A5337), for the Italian Navy from Fincantieri and Leonardo, for delivery by 2030 (@defpostmedia). In other European programmes, Romania’s new corvettes will be designated MPV 96, enlarged versions of Bulgaria’s MMPV 90 class, and BAE Systems will supply CV90 MkIIIC simulators to Denmark for $90 million, built with VRM in Slovakia.

Background (CEAFE archive): CEAFE has called for European naval consolidation since November 2019, citing Fincantieri, Navantia and Damen alongside a possible “naval Airbus” (https://x.com/i/status/1195138944544976897).

Why it matters for Europe: the OCCAR order and the Romania–Bulgaria design link are intra-European procurement, which CEAFE sees as positive.

What to watch: further OCCAR naval orders; the Romanian corvette contract.
