## Update: five nations sign the €5.5 billion CV90 joint procurement, Europe's largest common land-vehicle buy

Finland, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway and Sweden signed contracts in Stockholm on 27 November. The deal is worth €5.5 billion ($6 billion). It covers more than 400 CV9035 MkIV "Nordic Edition" infantry fighting vehicles from BAE Systems Hägglunds, based in Örnsköldsvik, Sweden. Each country signs its own purchase order under one framework agreement. That agreement fixes a common configuration. BAE Systems calls it "a world first". National releases give Finland 160 vehicles, Lithuania 100, Norway 70 and Sweden 60. The sources read here do not give the Dutch number. Production will be spread across suppliers in the five countries, with technology transfer and security of supply. Lithuania is a new CV90 user. Hägglunds is based in Sweden but owned by the UK's BAE Systems.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** the 26 November 20:30 edition reported Finland's order for 160 vehicles, which CEAFE shared from @DefenceFinland (https://x.com/i/status/2125940016216158499). In December 2024 BAE Systems won $2.5 billion in CV90 contracts with Denmark and Sweden (https://x.com/i/status/1868598853655765379).

**Why it matters for Europe:** five states pool their demand behind one European-built vehicle, with one configuration and shared logistics. None of them could reach this scale alone. This is the model CEAFE argues for: joint buying gives small states weight in front of large suppliers and keeps production and know-how in Europe.

**What to watch:** the Dutch share of the order; delivery dates; whether other countries join the framework.

## Kubilius in Vilnius: a European defence industry without "dangerous dependencies"

EU Commissioner for Defence and Space Andrius Kubilius spoke at BusinessEurope's COPRES meeting in Vilnius. He gave industry three messages. First, the EU defence industry must be free of dangerous dependencies in its supply chains. Second, EU armed forces need enough fuel from EU refineries. Third, the Commission will work towards an integrated EU defence market.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** on 20 October CEAFE commented on an earlier Kubilius post that cutting non-NATO suppliers means more dependence on the US: in weapons systems, access to space, minerals, energy, chips and digital services (https://x.com/i/status/2112508233541624302). The 08:30 edition reported that Paris and Berlin are split over "made in Europe" rules in the next EU defence budget.

**Why it matters for Europe:** dependence is not only on China. It is also on the US, for weapons, software and space. An integrated market is the only way for small member states to have a defence industry of sufficient scale. The test is whether "made in Europe" survives in the next EU budget.

**What to watch:** Commission proposals on the integrated defence market; the "made in Europe" clause in the budget talks.

Sources: @KubiliusA https://x.com/KubiliusA/status/2126315335011934356.

## Nearly all of ArianeGroup's Bremen workforce joins a 24-hour strike

Nearly 650 ArianeGroup employees in Bremen joined a 24-hour warning strike called by the IG Metall union on 24 November, according to European Spaceflight. Bremen builds the upper stages of Ariane 6. The strike was part of action by about 39,000 workers at 22 sites in northern Germany. ArianeGroup said the delivery schedule of Ariane 6 upper stages was not affected and that production was "adapted". On 26 November IG Metall reached a pay deal: +2% from May 2027 and +2.3% from April 2028, with provisions on investment and job security.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** Ariane 6 made its first launch on 9 July 2024 (ESA, https://x.com/i/status/1798596621871317087). In May 2024 CEAFE shared a report that the French space commander feared Ariane 6 delays could hold up a military patrol satellite (https://x.com/i/status/1791098980178252249).

**Why it matters for Europe:** Europe's only heavy launcher depends on a few single sites. Any stop there affects Europe's independent access to space, against the scale of SpaceX.

**What to watch:** upper-stage deliveries and the Ariane 6 launch cadence in the coming months.

Sources: European Spaceflight https://europeanspaceflight.com/hundreds-of-arianegroup-workers-strike-in-bremen/, @AndrewParsonson https://x.com/AndrewParsonson/status/2126324094043865136.

## Sweden and ICEYE sign a new agreement on sovereign space capability

The Finnish satellite company ICEYE says it has signed a new agreement with Sweden's Defence Materiel Administration (FMV). It was signed by Brigadier General Carl-Fredrik Edström and ICEYE's Seppo Aaltonen. ICEYE says it "further strengthens Sweden's sovereign capability in space". The post gives no scope or value. It builds on a January 2026 contract under which the Swedish Armed Forces own and operate ICEYE radar (SAR, synthetic aperture radar) satellites. That contract was expanded in August.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** in May CEAFE shared ICEYE's post that Finland, Sweden, Poland and the Netherlands are building sovereign surveillance systems designed to work together (https://x.com/i/status/2056512636095795334). On 25 November CEAFE shared ICEYE's visit by NATO's HALO coalition in Finland (https://x.com/i/status/2125479902506066313).

**Why it matters for Europe:** a Finnish supplier gives a Swedish customer its own radar imaging from space. Europe gains observation capacity that does not depend on US commercial providers.

**What to watch:** the scope and value of the agreement; further orders from Nordic and Baltic states.

Sources: @iceye_global https://x.com/iceye_global/status/2126330329585570005, ICEYE https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-to-deliver-sovereign-space-based-intelligence-capabilities-to-the-swedish-armed-forces.

## Ukraine's Fire Point: Stubb praises its output and the CEO wants guarantees "written in steel"

The Ukrainian drone and missile maker Fire Point quotes Finnish President Alexander Stubb after his visit to its plants. According to the company, Stubb said Ukraine went from "zero drones" four and a half years ago to "a capacity to produce 10 million drones" this year. Fire Point's CEO, Iryna Terekh, told Web Summit that security guarantees "should be written in steel... not in any sort of paper or ink". Fire Point also claims that Ukrainian strikes on AVT distillation units knocked out over 60% of Russia's refining capacity in one month. This is the company's claim and has not been verified.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** on 28 October CEAFE shared a post recalling that in 2024 President Zelenskyy said Ukraine, through Fire Point, would produce 3,000 cruise missiles by the end of 2025 (https://x.com/i/status/2115399051977834979).

**Why it matters for Europe:** Ukraine's industry has reached a scale of drone production that Europe needs. Integrating it into the European defence base would add capacity that no single member state has.

**What to watch:** joint production deals between Fire Point and EU states; independent data on the refinery strikes.

Sources: @FirePointUA https://x.com/FirePointUA/status/2126290241631748215, https://x.com/FirePointUA/status/2126326317587087639, https://x.com/FirePointUA/status/2126369886150578581.

## The UK orders US-made REMUS 620 underwater drones for Royal Navy minehunting

The UK has ordered REMUS 620 unmanned underwater vehicles from the US company HII for Royal Navy minehunting, according to Navy Recognition. No value was given in the source.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** in January 2024 CEAFE shared a UK Defence Journal report that the Royal Navy would buy more REMUS 300 drones from HII (https://x.com/i/status/1748851009600860469).

**Why it matters for Europe:** another European naval purchase goes to a US supplier. Each national order of this kind adds to dependence on US technology and support.

**What to watch:** numbers, value and delivery dates.

Sources: Navy Recognition https://x.com/i/status/2126297452802429056.
