Germany’s record €139bn 2027 defence budget clears committee, with 16 new Airbus A400Ms

The Bundestag budget committee approved Germany’s 2027 defence budget and the Bundeswehr special-fund plan in an overnight session ending on 13 November: more than €139bn in total, of which about €109.7bn is the regular defence budget, €31.4bn more than in 2026.

The amendments allow the purchase of 16 new Airbus A400M airlifters in tactical configuration (self-protection, DIRCM, Link 16) to replace the 16 earliest German A400Ms, which are configured only for logistics in permissive airspace; the older aircraft could be sold to partners. The budget also adds more frigates, more communications satellites and funding for a space-based sensor network, according to Aviation Week. The full Bundestag votes from 24 to 27 November and the Bundesrat on 18 December; no price has been given for the aircraft.

Why it matters for Europe. This is the kind of purchase CEAFE wants: a European aircraft, built by a multinational European programme (final assembly in Seville, Spain), bought by Europe’s largest defence budget. It also gives the A400M line new work as its backlog shrinks (139 of 178 delivered by March, per AFP via defence-blog). One caveat: Germany’s existing tactical A400Ms use the Israeli Elbit J-MUSIC DIRCM; a European self-protection suite would keep the whole value chain in Europe.

What to watch. Bundestag plenary vote (24–27 Nov); contract with Airbus and its value; which partner buys the 16 older aircraft.

Morocco reported close to French Scorpène submarines, with Rafale F4s and A330 MRTTs also in talks

Moroccan and Spanish outlets reported on 16 November that Rabat is in the final phase of buying Scorpène submarines from Naval Group, citing a French National Assembly foreign-affairs committee report (October 2025) that said Morocco was “in the final phase of an agreement” for the boats.

El Español says the deal covers two submarines, after Rabat set aside offers from Germany, Russia, Portugal and Greece, and includes technology transfer and a maintenance facility in Casablanca; Médias24 says the signature could come during King Mohammed VI’s visit to Paris. A post reposted by @F_CEAFE describes a wider package: 12–18 Dassault Rafale F4s, 2–3 Scorpènes, up to four Airbus A330 MRTT tankers and Airbus H125/H145 helicopters. None of this is officially confirmed by Paris, Rabat, Dassault or Naval Group; the Rafale talks were first reported in July 2026 (deliveries 2030–31 mentioned).

Why it matters for Europe. A package of this size would be one of Europe’s biggest export wins of the year, all of it European (French and Airbus) hardware in a market where the US F-16 and other non-European suppliers compete. For Spain it would be a significant change on its southern flank: a first Moroccan submarine force operating around the Strait of Gibraltar.

What to watch. Date of the royal visit to Paris and any signature; reaction in Spain and Algeria.

Italy will build its own naval missile launcher for the MBDA Stratus LO cruise missile

Italy’s 2026–2028 multi-year defence plan (Documento Programmatico Pluriennale, sent to Parliament on 10 November and published on 15 November) formalises the development of a national vertical launch system, the “lanciatore polivalente nazionale”, for the new FREMM EVO frigates and the future DDX destroyers.

It must fire the Stratus LO, the stealthy land-attack and anti-ship cruise missile that MBDA is developing for France, the UK and Italy; Rivista Italiana Difesa reports an eight-cell module with cells larger than the French SYLVER’s, able to fire the Aster 30 and later the Aquila anti-hypersonic interceptor. The deep-strike programme keeps its €260m total but brings funding forward (€71.2m in 2026, €170.2m in 2027). The plan also funds two FREMM EVO frigates from Orizzonte Sistemi Navali (Fincantieri–Leonardo). No manufacturer is named for the launcher.

Why it matters for Europe. Stratus LO is a genuine three-nation European missile, and putting it on Italian ships instead of buying US Mk 41 launchers and Tomahawks keeps the strike capability sovereign. But a new Italian launcher next to the French SYLVER is another national duplicate inside Europe; a joint Franco-Italian launcher would give MBDA’s missiles one common home.

What to watch. Choice of launcher maker (likely Leonardo or MBDA Italia); the DPP’s other lines, including GCAP (see Italy’s GCAP spending, 16 Nov 07:30 brief).

UK defends its use of SpaceX’s Starshield as Europe debates dependence on Musk

UK defence minister Luke Pollard told Parliament that “Starlink is not used for UK military operations” but that the Ministry of Defence does use SpaceX’s military Starshield service, alongside military and commercial satcom, to provide “operational resilience through diversity”.

In answers published on 16 November he said the MoD has “a substantial number” of Starshield terminals but would not give the figure or list their uses, citing national security. The questions came from Liberal Democrat MPs after restrictions were placed on Ukraine’s access to SpaceX services.

Why it matters for Europe. The UK, Europe’s leading military space power alongside France, now depends in part on a US private company whose service to Ukraine has been restricted. That is the kill-switch risk CEAFE warns about, applied to communications. European alternatives exist or are being built: Eutelsat OneWeb, the UK’s own Skynet, and the EU’s IRIS² constellation.

What to watch. Ukraine’s Starshield restrictions (Conflicts section); IRIS² timetable.

US Senate proposes a $1.1 trillion defence bill, with $47bn for munitions and $9.4bn for Golden Dome

Senate Appropriations Republicans released a $1.072 trillion fiscal 2027 defence bill on 16 November (military construction excluded), short of the $1.5 trillion the administration wants.

They moved nearly $69bn of the White House’s stalled $350bn reconciliation request into regular appropriations: $9.4bn for Golden Dome missile defence, $3bn for the new Defense Autonomous Warfare Group and $47.2bn for munitions, $40.6bn of it for multiyear deals such as AMRAAM, PAC-3 MSE and PrSM. Appropriators add 25 F-35s (five A, five B, 15 C; +$4.1bn). The bill also has $750m for the Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative, about $175m for the Baltic Security Initiative and about $1bn for a First Island Chain initiative (Philippines, Taiwan). Separately, the Pentagon failed its ninth annual audit and now aims for a clean opinion by 2028.

Why it matters for Europe. US munitions multiyear contracts (PAC-3 MSE, AMRAAM) fund the same production lines Europeans queue for, so US orders come first. With $750m for Ukraine and $175m for the Baltics, the US contribution to Europe’s security is now small next to the EU’s own: the Commission paid €1.71bn to Ukraine on 16 November alone.

What to watch. House–Senate negotiation before 11 Dec; fate of the $350bn reconciliation package.

Update: Vulcan failure tightens the US launch squeeze; Europe’s space week opens in Bremen

Breaking Defense reports that the Vulcan failure of 15 November is likely to ground the rocket for months, leaving SpaceX’s Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy as the only US rockets certified for critical national-security payloads while Blue Origin’s New Glenn remains grounded; six Vulcan missions were already moved to SpaceX by 1 October.

If the BE-4 engine is involved, New Glenn is also affected. SpaceX stopped taking commercial Falcon orders for after 2028. Meanwhile Arianespace promoted the next Ariane 64 launch for Amazon Leo (VA271/LE-04), and Space Tech Expo Europe opens in Bremen today (17–19 November), with ESA’s SME office present. US firms are there too: Redwire is showing its Hammerhead LEO platform built in Kruibeke, Belgium, by its Belgian subsidiary (the former QinetiQ Space).

Why it matters for Europe. A US launch shortage makes Ariane 6 more valuable, even to American customers like Amazon. Europe should make sure its launch capacity serves European sovereign missions first and keeps growing, rather than filling US gaps.

What to watch. ULA’s failure investigation; VA271 launch date; EU SST webinar on space surveillance on 19 Nov (https://x.com/i/status/2122396785444602251).
