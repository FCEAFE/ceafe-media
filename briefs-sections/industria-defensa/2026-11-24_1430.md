## Airbus Manching works council rejects GCAP and demands a German sixth-generation fighter

The works council at Airbus Defence and Space's Manching site (Bavaria) has "firmly rejected" Germany joining the Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP) led by the UK, Italy and Japan, calling it "doomed to failure" for Germany because work shares are already allocated and Berlin would have little influence. It urges the federal government to launch a national sixth-generation fighter programme now, open to later partners, with a prototype first flight in 2032 and entry into service before 2040 (FlightGlobal, Aviation Week, Focus, 24 Nov). "We must not repeat the political mistakes made with France," the council says, in reference to the stalled Franco-German-Spanish FCAS.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** the German debate is not new. In November 2023 @F_CEAFE shared a Times report that Germany might leave FCAS (with France and Spain) for the British-Italian-Japanese Tempest/GCAP, and said it preferred Italy joining the European FCAS; in January 2025 the Airbus CEO said Europe's two fighter programmes could combine (Reuters); in January 2026 Italy's Crosetto called British secrecy on GCAP "madness" (Defense News).

*CEAFE:* The lag is not inevitable: it is the result of acting separately on a board that is already continental or global.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Europe now risks three separate combat-air efforts (GCAP, a rump FCAS/SCAF and a possible German national programme), while the US F-47 advances as one programme. No European state can fund a sixth-generation fighter alone at US scale; fragmentation is the strongest argument for a single European programme, or at least a common core of engines, sensors and combat cloud. Spain, a FCAS partner through Indra, is directly exposed.

**Key facts:** prototype first flight 2032 and service entry before 2040 (council's proposal); GCAP partners UK, Italy, Japan.

## Germany picks Israel's Elbit for the F-128 frigates' electronic warfare

TKMS has awarded Elbit Systems Deutschland a five-year contract to supply Aqua Marine naval electronic-warfare suites for the German Navy's four F-128 frigates. The modular system provides radar electronic support measures and countermeasures for threat detection, self-protection and electronic attack. No value was disclosed (Elbit on X; Naval Today, hartpunkt, EDR, 24 Nov).

**Why it matters for Europe:** another core sensor of a European warship goes to a non-European supplier, even if through its German subsidiary. European alternatives exist: HENSOLDT (Germany), Elettronica (Italy), Indra (Spain) and Thales (France) all build naval EW suites. Each such choice weakens the European base that the EDIP and SAFE instruments are meant to build.

## Ariane 6 flies its tenth mission, with 36 satellites for Amazon Leo

Ariane 64 flight VA271 (Amazon Leo LE-04) lifted off from Kourou at 11:14 UTC (12:14 Madrid) on 24 November, a day late after a ground-support-system problem on 23 November, carrying 36 Amazon Leo satellites to low Earth orbit (Arianespace, ESA, Bloomberg on X; Space.com). Satellite separation over about two hours was still to be confirmed at writing. It is Ariane 6's fifth flight of 2026 in our launch log.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the launch shows Ariane 6 in a regular commercial cadence, but its biggest customer is a US broadband constellation that competes with Europe's own IRIS² and Eutelsat OneWeb. Launch income helps the European launcher; the strategic asset in orbit is American.

## Kongsberg delivers DESNA, a Norwegian–Ukrainian air-defence system, to Ukraine

Kongsberg says it has delivered DESNA, developed with Norwegian and Ukrainian authorities and Ukrainian industry. It links Ukrainian-made air-defence missiles and launchers to Kongsberg's fire command and control centre, to protect people and infrastructure against drones and other aerial threats. It complements NASAMS, in Ukraine since 2022, and uses missiles that can be produced in large numbers at lower unit cost (Kongsberg, 24 Nov).

**Why it matters for Europe:** a European C2 backbone joined to cheap Ukrainian interceptors is the kind of co-production that reduces dependence on scarce US interceptors (see item 5).

## Pentagon in talks with GM and Ford to mass-produce drones

The Wall Street Journal reports that the Pentagon is in early talks with General Motors, Ford and other manufacturers to use their factories and supply chains to scale up military drone production; automakers would mainly supply components (motors, actuators, engines, sensors, lidar) to established drone makers, and GM is considering drone motors. No agreement is final (WSJ on X; Anadolu, US News, 24 Nov).

**Why it matters for Europe:** the US is mobilising its civil industrial base for mass. Europe's car industry, now shedding jobs, is an obvious candidate for the same move.

**Sources:** https://x.com/i/status/2125182412267118955 (WSJ, paywalled); https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/pentagon-in-talks-with-general-motors-ford-to-boost-military-drone-production-report/4135749

## JAXA reports a data breach in its Earth-observation distribution system

JAXA says attackers breached its public Earth Observation Data Distribution System on 18 November through a command-injection flaw, installed a backdoor and stole the account names and encrypted passwords of about 2,000 users. The system was shut down that day; JAXA found no sign the satellite data were accessed and has set no date for restoration (JAXA press release, 24 Nov; Nikkei, IT Post).
