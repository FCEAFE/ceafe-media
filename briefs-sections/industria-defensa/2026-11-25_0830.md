## Poland and the US sign a defence R&D agreement after 17 years of talks

Polish Deputy Defence Minister Cezary Tomczyk and US Under Secretary of War for Research and Engineering Emil Michael signed a Research, Development, Test and Evaluation (RDT&E) agreement at the Pentagon on 24 November (Polish MoD, Anadolu Agency). It ends 17 years of negotiations and sets a framework for joint research projects, technology development and exchange of technical data. Polish universities, research institutes and companies can now join US R&D, testing and evaluation programmes; the first pilot area is high-energy materials and ammunition (Polish MoD). During the same visit Polish officials discussed with Lockheed Martin faster delivery of F-35 simulators, full operational capability of Polish F-35s, a European service centre in Poland for PAC-3 Patriot missiles and technology transfer for HIMARS.

This is not about diminishing this country, but about measuring its real weight against those who already operate on a continental or global scale.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Poland, the largest buyer of US equipment in the EU, is anchoring its defence research in a bilateral framework with Washington rather than in European ones (EDF, EDA, PESCO). Early-stage joint research shapes future requirements, so it tends to lock in US standards and suppliers, and anything developed together falls under US export rules (ITAR) that Europe does not mirror. A PAC-3 service centre in Poland would be a US-controlled node; Europe's own alternative for ground-based air defence is the SAMP/T NG (Eurosam: MBDA and Thales), and Poland is already co-developing CAMM-MR with MBDA UK (see the 24 Nov 20:30 brief).

**Key facts:** signed 24 Nov 2026 at the Pentagon; signatories Tomczyk (PL) and Emil Michael (US); first pilot area: high-energy materials and ammunition.

**What to watch:** first joint projects named; whether the PAC-3 service centre and HIMARS technology transfer turn into contracts with PGZ.

## Raytheon moves the US Navy's nuclear sea-launched cruise missile (SLCM-N) into development

Raytheon (RTX) has been awarded an Other Transaction Authority agreement by the US Navy to move the Nuclear-Armed Sea-Launched Cruise Missile (SLCM-N) from concept work into a development phase that will mature the design, integrate subsystems and prepare prototype demonstrations (RTX release, 24 Nov). Contributors are Anduril Rocket Motor Systems, Divergent (which says it builds the airframe) and Williams International. No value was disclosed.

**Why it matters for Europe:** SLCM-N adds a non-strategic US nuclear option at sea, relevant to extended deterrence in Europe, at a time when France and Sweden held their first joint nuclear-deterrence exercise (Defense News). The supply chain also shows how far US start-ups (Anduril, Divergent) are now inside top-tier strategic programmes.

**Sources:** https://x.com/i/status/2125390434004500859 ; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rtxs-raytheon-selected-to-advance-development-of-new-submarine-launched-missile-for-us-navy-302948503.html

## Korea's KAI targets the UK Hawk replacement with the T-50

Korea Aerospace Industries has started marketing the T-50 (offered as "TF-50") in the UK as a replacement for the BAE Systems Hawk used by the RAF, with adverts in AirForces Monthly; Lockheed Martin is named only as development partner and KAI says it is exploring partnerships with UK companies (Aviation Week, 23 Nov). The UK Jet Training System competition is still being defined.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the end of the Hawk leaves Europe's first jet-trainer exporter without a successor in its home market. A Korean–US trainer would be a non-European purchase; the European option already in service with Italy, Poland and Greece is Leonardo's M-346. CEAFE comment: a market fragmented country by country lets outside suppliers pick off each requirement one at a time.

**Sources:** https://x.com/i/status/2125372394894155941 ; https://aviationweek.com/defense/light-attack-advanced-training/kai-begins-uk-t-50-marketing-push

## Boeing: 15 more KC-46A tankers for $2.82bn; Malaysia Airlines chooses the 787

The US Air Force added $2.82bn to Boeing's contract for 15 KC-46A Lot 13 aircraft plus support, subscriptions and licences, with work in Seattle due by 30 June 2030 (MarketWatch, DefenseBook; Boeing on X). Separately, Malaysia Aviation Group picked the Boeing 787 Dreamliner to expand its wide-body fleet, breaking Airbus' hold on its long-haul line-up (Bloomberg on X; article not fetched).

**Why it matters for Europe:** the KC-46 is the US tanker that beat the Airbus A330 MRTT; the MRTT remains Europe's tanker (Multinational MRTT Fleet). Losing a long-haul customer in Asia is a commercial blow for Airbus at a moment when Boeing is recovering.
