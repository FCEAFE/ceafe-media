German industry sets up a joint trading house for critical raw materials, with Rheinmetall and BMW

Forty-five German companies from the automotive, electrical and defence industries, among them Rheinmetall and BMW, back a planned German Raw Materials Trading House (Deutsches Rohstoffhandelshaus, DRH). It will pool demand, buy and trade critical materials jointly, build strategic reserves and may take stakes in supply chains, while each company keeps its own purchasing. The DRH is the core of a new “Zukunftsmodell Rohstoffsicherung” (raw-materials security model) presented by industry and the federal government on 23 November; the pre-founding phase runs until mid-2027, when it is due to be formally established (Handelsblatt, VDA, 23 Nov; Bloomberg on X).

Why it matters for Europe: defence production (rare-earth magnets, germanium, gallium, tungsten) depends on supply chains China dominates and has restricted. A national pool is a step forward, but a German-only buyer competes with other Europeans for the same tonnes; the EU’s RESourceEU and Critical Raw Materials Act joint-purchasing tools carry more weight if the big national champions buy through them. Here again, size decides: in raw materials no single member state can bargain with Beijing on equal terms.

  • 45 companies; formal founding planned mid-2027 (Handelsblatt, 23 Nov).

Germany’s Aerodata flies the AeroForce X, an ITAR-free MALE drone prototype

Aerodata AG flew the AeroForce X prototype (registration D-IAFX) for the first time on 14 November from Strausberg near Berlin, with a test pilot on board to check basic handling; all test objectives were met, the company says. The twin-engine, twin-boom aircraft weighs 5 tonnes, carries 1.3 tonnes of payload and has up to 40 hours’ endurance. Company officials told Aviation Week it will be 100% German, engines, mission suite and communications included, and therefore free of ITAR (Aviation Week, 16 Nov; posted 24 Nov).

Context: Antecedentes: on 26 March 2022 @F_CEAFE noted the concerns raised about the European sovereignty of the Eurodrone because its engine comes from Avio Aero (a company of the US group GE, not the Italian rocket maker Avio S.p.A.), and that the company stressed the engine is not subject to ITAR, so no US approval would be needed (X, 2022).

Why it matters for Europe: European forces fly mostly US MQ-9 Reapers or Israeli Herons, with US export control over use and upgrades. A national, ITAR-free MALE is a sovereign option alongside the late Eurodrone (Airbus–Dassault–Leonardo); the risk is fragmentation if each country builds its own. Aviation Week understands it is powered by two Continental CD-300 Jet-A piston engines.

Update: BepiColombo safely in orbit around Mercury

ESA says BepiColombo is safe and in a stable orbit around Mercury after an eight-year journey, even though a software issue cut its orbit-insertion manoeuvre short (the thruster glitch reported in the 14:30 brief of 23 November). JAXA says the separation of its Mio orbiter remains scheduled for 10 December 2026 (Japan time), after nine flybys since the 2018 launch.

Context: Antecedentes: BepiColombo is a joint ESA–JAXA mission whose European spacecraft was built by Airbus; @F_CEAFE has followed it since its Earth fly-by in April 2020 (X, 2020).

Why it matters for Europe: Europe’s largest planetary mission, run with Japan, is back on track; a sign of what ESA achieves through partnership with non-US agencies.

China’s Tiangong will host its first foreign astronaut, a Pakistani air force pilot

China will launch Shenzhou-24 on 25 November at 08:18 local time (01:18 Madrid) with commander Tang Hongbo, Wu Yi and Pakistani payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, who will be the first foreign astronaut on the Chinese space station (Reuters; China Manned Space Agency via Xinhua and Global Times, Chinese state media). The mission includes four China–Pakistan joint experiments, according to Global Times. China will also rehearse a Long March-10A launch with the Mengzhou crewed spacecraft at Wenchang.

CEAFE: We see once again how a power with global reach operates at a scale that no European state can match on its own.

Context: Antecedentes: @F_CEAFE has followed China’s Tiangong station since the Shenzhou-13 crew arrived in October 2021 (X, 2021); CEAFE has argued that Europeans should take part in Tiangong, a plan ESA dropped.

Why it matters for Europe: ESA astronauts had trained with Chinese crews and planned to fly to Tiangong, but ESA dropped the plan. Today the first foreign seat on the only station not led by the US goes to Pakistan. Choosing partners in space, as on Earth, is a test of strategic autonomy. CEAFE argues that Europe should cooperate with every major space power on its own terms, rather than give up options to stay in line with a single partner.

What to watch: the launch on 25 November and docking; China’s offer of Tiangong seats to other countries; ESA’s next decisions on human spaceflight partners.

Sources: Reuters, Reuters (X), Xinhua, Xinhua (2), Global Times, Global Times (2), Chinese MFA spokesperson
