EDA: only 24% of EU defence procurement was collaborative in 2025 — a joint-buying centre is coming

The European Defence Agency says only 24% of defence equipment procurement was collaborative in 2025 and that more than 80% of EU defence procurement is still run nationally. It will set up a collaborative defence procurement centre for off-the-shelf equipment “in the coming months”. Citing a European Parliament Research Service (EPRS) study, the EDA says €2.8 billion could be saved if collaborative procurement rose to 30%, which it equates to 28 Eurofighters or 100 Leopard 2A8 tanks. Why it matters for Europe: the EDA’s own figures show how fragmented European buying still is. The five-nation CV90 order signed on 27 Nov (see Ongoing threads) shows what pooled demand behind a European product can achieve; a standing EDA centre could make that the rule rather than the exception. Sources: https://x.com/EUDefenceAgency/status/2126586900601610693 · https://eda.europa.eu/news-and-events/spotlight/spotlight-of-the-month/buying-military-equipment-together

Poland: Grupa WB licenses Łukasiewicz gas-generator technology for extended-range 155 mm shells

Grupa WB, Poland’s largest private defence company, signed an agreement with the state Łukasiewicz Research Network (Institute of Industrial Organic Chemistry) to commercialise gas generators for artillery shells (signed 25 Nov, publicised on X on 27 Nov evening). A base-bleed gas generator reduces drag and increases the range of 155 mm rounds. WB says Poland will join the small group of states able to make extended-range 155 mm ammunition, a product only a few European firms offer. Rzeczpospolita puts the deal at PLN 90 million; Łukasiewicz calls it possibly one of the biggest commercialisation successes of Polish science. Why it matters for Europe: national research feeding European ammunition production — the bottleneck Europe has struggled with since 2022. The next step is to make such capabilities available across the EU’s joint ammunition effort rather than only national programmes. Poland: another Regina battalion fire module with Krab howitzers delivered

Grupa WB said on 28 Nov that another battalion-level Regina fire module has been delivered to the Polish Armed Forces. The module is a self-propelled unit combining reconnaissance, command and control, fires and logistics, with Krab 155 mm self-propelled howitzers from Huta Stalowa Wola (HSW, PGZ group), Radmor radios, the Fonet digital communication platform and the Topaz fire-control system (WB). WB presents it as “technological sovereignty in practice”. (The post does not give the number of modules delivered to date.) Why it matters for Europe: a domestic, European artillery chain; it contrasts with Poland’s large purchases of US and Korean systems, for which European alternatives exist. Source: https://x.com/WBGroup_PL/status/2126629348547481874

Space: HyImpulse hot-fires its SL1 hybrid motor with thrust vector control

German launch start-up HyImpulse completed the first hot-fire of its 90 kN HyPLOX-90 hybrid motor (paraffin and liquid oxygen) fitted with thrust vector control, on 16 Nov at a new site in the “Mediterranean region” (announced 27 Nov). The motor powers the three-stage SL1, designed for up to 600 kg to low Earth orbit. HyImpulse declined to give European Spaceflight an updated date for the first flight (its last stated target was 2027). Why it matters for Europe: one more step for Europe’s micro-launcher field (with Isar, RFA, PLD and others); Europe still lacks operational small launchers while US firms dominate the market. Sources: https://x.com/AndrewParsonson/status/2126568788120240447 · https://europeanspaceflight.com/hyimpulse-completes-first-hot-fire-test-with-sl1-thrust-vector-control-system/

Leonardo and the UK: Scottish Secretary meets CEO Mariani in Italy

UK Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander met Leonardo Group CEO and GM Lorenzo Mariani in Italy to discuss Scotland’s role in UK–Italy cooperation, the UK Government in Scotland said (reposted by Leonardo). Leonardo employs over 2,500 people in Edinburgh and more than 10,000 in the UK. Why it matters for Europe: an Italian group as a major UK defence employer — European industrial integration that crosses the EU’s border, as in GCAP (UK, Italy, Japan). Source: https://x.com/Leonardo_live/status/2126613485987483888

Shield AI sells “sovereign” autonomy to South Korea

US firm Shield AI is pitching its Hivemind software to South Korea at K-DEX 2026 as “sovereign mission autonomy” for Korean ground forces, powering its X-BAT autonomous VTOL fighter and V-BAT drone. Why it matters for Europe: US vendors now market US-owned software as “sovereignty”, as Palantir did with Poland’s Maven deal (27 Nov). Software under US export control is not sovereign for the buyer; Europe has its own autonomy and AI firms (e.g. Helsing, Quantum Systems). Source: https://x.com/shieldaitech/status/2126435946497286343
