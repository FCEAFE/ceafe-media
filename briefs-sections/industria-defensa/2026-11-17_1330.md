## ICEYE bids €2.05bn for Avio S.p.A., the Vega rocket maker, to build a European space champion

**Finland's ICEYE, a satellite builder and operator, launched on 17 November an all-cash tender offer for all the shares of Avio S.p.A., the Italian launcher company based in Colleferro that builds the Vega rocket.**

Avio S.p.A. is not Avio Aero, the aero-engine maker owned by GE Aerospace. According to European Spaceflight, the offer is made through ICEYE's Italian subsidiary Iris 2 (unrelated to the EU's IRIS² constellation) at €41 per share, a 21.3% premium over Avio's closing price on 16 November, valuing the company at about €2.05 billion. ICEYE plans to delist Avio from the Milan stock exchange and to invest a further €1 billion in Italy over five years, with up to 400 jobs. "Governments need control of their critical space infrastructure, and Europe needs it most," said ICEYE chief executive Rafal Modrzewski. Avio's board said it would assess the offer. European Spaceflight reports that the deal needs regulatory clearances involving Italy, France, the UK and the United States, as well as approval under the EU's Foreign Subsidies Regulation.

**Why it matters for Europe.** This is an intra-European takeover in a strategic sector, of the kind CEAFE supports: a satellite company and a launcher maker joined under European ownership, at a time when Europe's launch capacity is far behind that of the United States. The fact that clearances are reported to involve Washington shows how far US rules reach into European deals.

**What to watch.** The Avio board's response, the Italian government's position under its golden-power rules, and the reaction of Avio's main shareholders.

## Israel's Elbit Systems reports a record $33bn backlog

**Elbit Systems reported its third-quarter 2026 results on 17 November, with a record backlog of $33 billion, revenues of $2.3 billion and 17% revenue growth, according to the company.**

Elbit also reported GAAP net earnings per share of $3.70 and non-GAAP EPS of $4.36, and said EPS grew by more than 30%.

**Why it matters for Europe.** European governments are among the buyers of Israeli systems; the 07:30 edition reported that Prague is looking at Israel's Arrow 3 and Barak. Every such purchase is money that does not go to the European defence industrial base.

**What to watch.** How much of Elbit's backlog comes from European customers, which the company breaks down in its quarterly report.

## MBDA and France's CEA present IRRIS, an "ITAR-free" infrared test system

**MBDA and France's Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission (CEA) presented IRRIS on 17 November, describing it as "a new sovereign and ITAR-free capability" for realistic infrared system testing.**

ITAR is the US regulation on the export of defence articles; equipment that contains US-controlled parts needs Washington's approval before it can be re-exported. The companies say IRRIS was developed jointly by their teams.

**Why it matters for Europe.** "ITAR-free" means that the US cannot block the export of systems tested with it. It is a small but concrete example of European technological sovereignty.

**What to watch.** Whether IRRIS is offered to other European missile and sensor makers.
