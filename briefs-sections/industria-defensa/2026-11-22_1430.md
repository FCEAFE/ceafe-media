## US buyers take control of European defence suppliers: Red Cat bids for Steyr Motors, Fairbanks Morse buys Truflo Marine, NUBURU takes Tekne

**Three specialist European defence suppliers, in Austria, the United Kingdom and Italy, have come or are coming under US ownership since mid-October, just as Europe raises its defence spending.**

(1) **Red Cat Holdings** (US) has made a cash offer for **Steyr Motors AG** (Austria), which makes high-performance engines for special military vehicles and boats and auxiliary power units for battle tanks: €39.00 per share, about €205 million in total, a 28.9% premium on the 16 October close. The Steyr Motors management and supervisory boards recommended acceptance on 2 November; the acceptance period runs until 18 December 2026, and the offer is conditional on foreign direct investment approvals in Austria, Australia, Italy and the UK. (2) **Fairbanks Morse Defense** (US), owned by the US private-equity firm Arcline Investment Management, completed the acquisition of **Truflo Marine** (Birmingham, UK) on 9 November: its naval valves are in service with more than 37 allied navies, and it has about 270 employees. (3) **NUBURU** (US) completed the purchase of a 70% controlling stake in **Tekne S.p.A.** (Italy), a maker of electronic warfare systems, special vehicles and defence mobility, on 15 October, after Italy's Golden Power clearance of 5 August; it paid a fixed €5.2 million for the additional 67.1%, plus an earn-out capped at €20 million.

**Context:** under US ownership, these companies' future exports and many supply decisions can fall under US export-control law: the ITAR rules for defence articles and the EAR for dual-use items. The deals come as Washington asks Europeans not to exclude US firms from their defence market (see World, Colby's "NATO 3.0"). In the Tekne case the Italian government authorised the sale under its Golden Power; in the Steyr case the decision lies with the Austrian, Italian, UK and Australian screening authorities, not with the EU.

**Why it matters for Europe:** specialists in engines, naval components and electronic warfare are passing under US control while European orders grow, so part of the value and of the control over supply moves across the Atlantic. Each sale is a small step in European deindustrialisation and loss of sovereignty, decided state by state. The contrast in scale makes clear that national strategies, however ambitious, are not enough against competitors of a different order of magnitude.

**What to watch:** the acceptance level of the Steyr Motors offer and the screening decisions before 18 December; whether the UK reviewed the Truflo deal under its National Security and Investment Act.

## Türkiye's Fidan questions why only Ankara was sanctioned under CAATSA for buying the S-400

**Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan asked on 21 November why Washington spared "tons of countries that bought Russian defense systems" from the CAATSA sanctions it imposed on Türkiye over the Russian S-400, and blamed Israeli and Greek lobbies in Washington for blocking relief (TGRT Haber interview, via Anadolu).**

He said there is political will in Ankara and Washington to settle their defence disputes, that US delays on "some long-stalled ammunition purchases" have been lifted, but that the administration's room for manoeuvre is "very limited" on matters set by law.

**Context:** the US sanctioned China's Equipment Development Department in 2018 over Su-35 and S-400 purchases, and Türkiye's defence-industry agency in December 2020; India, which also bought the S-400, has not been sanctioned, because successive administrations chose not to enforce CAATSA against it (Foreign Policy). Türkiye was removed from the F-35 programme in 2019, and the FY2020 NDAA bars F-35 transfers unless Türkiye no longer possesses the S-400 (Congressional Research Service).

**Why it matters for Europe:** CAATSA is a secondary-sanctions law: US law and Congress decide which allies may buy which weapons, and access to US systems and their support can depend on political decisions in Washington for the decades of a system's life.

**What to watch:** any US move on CAATSA relief for Türkiye; Ankara's choice between a return to the F-35 and more Eurofighters.

## NATO's sovereignty dilemma: armed forces depend on private tech, AI and networks such as Starshield

**At the NATO Edge conference in Türkiye this week, NATO officials and defence experts discussed how armed forces can use artificial intelligence and high technology without losing control over sensitive data and capabilities, France 24 reports.**

The report sets out the tension between technology firms "controlled by private capital demanding profits, independent from any political control" and military leaders who answer to political authority, and cites SpaceX's Starlink network and its military counterpart Starshield, on which Ukraine depends, as the example. Separately, SpaceNews reports that the US Space Force is speeding up the Space Data Network Backbone, a military satellite data network built under a $2.29 billion SpaceX contract, with three launches in two weeks.

**Context:** US Senate Democrats plan to investigate the cuts to Ukraine's Starshield access (see the 21 November 20:30 edition).

**Why it matters for Europe:** connectivity, cloud and AI are becoming as decisive as munitions, and most of the private suppliers are American; whoever runs the network can restrict it. Europe's own secure-connectivity constellation, IRIS², is still years from service.

**What to watch:** the Senate inquiry into Starshield; the IRIS² timetable.

## Roketsan's ORKA torpedo fired from a Baykar Bayraktar AKINCI drone

**Baykar and Roketsan reported on 22 November that Roketsan's ORKA next-generation lightweight torpedo was successfully fired from a Bayraktar AKINCI unmanned aircraft.**

According to Anadolu, the AKINCI took off from Çorlu (Tekirdağ) and released the torpedo into the Gulf of Saros; ORKA uses active and passive sonar and can be guided internally or externally via fibre optics. Turkish outlets describe it as the first release of the torpedo from an unmanned aircraft.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Türkiye keeps pairing domestic weapons with domestic platforms, which gives its industry an anti-submarine export package that competes with European suppliers.

**What to watch:** qualification and export offers of the AKINCI–ORKA combination.

**Sources:** [Selçuk Bayraktar](https://x.com/i/status/2124398186689069474), [Baykar](https://x.com/i/status/2124397625055060339), [Roketsan](https://x.com/i/status/2124401006297403459), [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/tr/bilim-teknoloji/milli-torpido-orka-bayraktar-akinci-ile-denizle-bulustu/4133381)

## Ariane 6's tenth flight will carry 36 Amazon Leo satellites

**Arianespace has published the launch kit for flight VA271, Ariane 6's tenth: an Ariane 64 with four P160C boosters will carry 36 Amazon Leo satellites from Kourou, French Guiana, with launch scheduled for 23 November.**

**Context:** in the same window SpaceX scheduled the launch of the US National Reconnaissance Office's NROL-136 mission from Vandenberg on 22 November. Separately, MBDA opened the Anglo-French Complex Weapons Innovation & Technology Partnership call for French and British SMEs, start-ups and academia, with applications due by 31 January 2027.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Europe's heavy launcher is again flying to deploy a US commercial constellation, Amazon's competitor to Starlink, while Europe's own IRIS² is not yet in orbit.

**What to watch:** the VA271 launch on 23 November.
