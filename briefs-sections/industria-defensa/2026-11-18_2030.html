Avio’s American arm wins Tomahawk motor work through a US-governed subsidiary

Raytheon (RTX) has awarded Ursa Major and Avio USA a joint contract to design, develop and qualify a new solid rocket motor booster for the US Navy’s Tomahawk cruise missile, both companies said on 18 November, so that Raytheon can dual-source the booster. Avio USA (Arlington, Virginia) is a subsidiary of Avio S.p.A. (Colleferro, Italy; Vega launcher and solid motors), not of Avio Aero (GE Aerospace’s engine business). Its press note says it is “structured to operate in compliance with U.S. security and export-control regulations” and is “governed by a U.S.-based board of directors”; its CEO is James Syring, a retired US Navy vice admiral and former director of the Missile Defense Agency. The company broke ground in September on a solid rocket motor plant in southern Virginia. No contract value was disclosed.

Why it matters for Europe. This is the other side of ICEYE’s bid for Avio S.p.A. (13:30 edition). An Italian propulsion firm can work on a core US weapon only through a US-governed subsidiary, the foreign-ownership ring-fence Washington demands, which is why the ICEYE offer needs CFIUS clearance and a DCSA notification. Europe has no equivalent rule forcing US-owned defence units in Europe under a European board, and European know-how here adds to US production capacity.

What to watch. Whether the Tomahawk work weighs in Rome’s golden-power review and CFIUS’s review of the ICEYE bid; Leonardo’s decision on its 18.04%.

Sources: Ursa Major press release, @ursamajortech (post).

Patriot’s French bottleneck: the PAC-3 MSE ramp-up depends on an ArianeGroup fabric

According to a Wall Street Journal investigation, Raycarb C2B is the only carbonised rayon approved for the nozzle of the PAC-3 MSE interceptor’s rocket motor. ArianeGroup, the Airbus–Safran joint venture, makes it near Bordeaux. The fibre comes from a Czech factory owned by a Thailand-based group, is woven in France and carbonised by ArianeGroup, then sold to Park Aerospace in Kansas and passed to L3Harris for the motors that go to Lockheed Martin’s Patriot line. A new ArianeGroup line is due in 2028 but will not meet US demand; a preliminary deal of 18 July 2026 foresees an ArianeGroup plant in the United States, with $25 million in advance payments from Park, unlikely before 2030 (Aerotime; Interfax-Ukraine). More fabric for the US would mean less for other priority clients, including the French armed forces (Interfax-Ukraine, citing the WSJ). The target is about 2,000 PAC-3 MSE a year, up from 620 delivered in 2025.

Why it matters for Europe. The best-known symbol of Europe’s dependence on US air defence runs on a European chokepoint, which gives Europe leverage just as the Pentagon insists that European markets stay open to US firms. A US plant would move that capability across the Atlantic. The European alternative to Patriot exists: SAMP/T NG by Eurosam (MBDA and Thales).

Technological and defence dependence is not abstract: it shows up in the number of companies, in spending and in the presence on the ground.

What to watch. Whether France sets conditions on the US plant; L3Harris’s qualification of a second rayon source.

Sources: WSJ (post), Aerotime, Interfax-Ukraine, Hindustan Times (WSJ reprint).

MBDA Deutschland and ATMOS Space Cargo study “Precision Orbital Deep Strike”

ATMOS Space Cargo (Lichtenau, Germany; sites in Strasbourg and Kraków) and MBDA Deutschland signed a cooperation agreement on 18 November to design, develop and demonstrate a “Precision Orbital Deep Strike” (PODS) capability, combining military payloads with ATMOS’s orbital transport and re-entry technology. The initial scope covers architecture, feasibility, payload integration and possible demonstrations; no budget, timetable or specifications were disclosed. ATMOS says PODS is separate from its civilian PHOENIX capsules and that deployment would depend on government decisions and applicable law. Opex360 notes the vulnerabilities: a predictable low-orbit trajectory, exposure to anti-satellite weapons and escalation risk.

Why it matters for Europe. A German start-up and a European champion are entering a field shaped so far by the US and China, in principle a sovereign capability. But conventional strike from orbit raises arms-control questions Europe has not debated: anything that shortens warning times between NATO and Russia increases the risk of miscalculation, including nuclear.

What to watch. Whether the Bundeswehr or the European Defence Fund funds a demonstrator; reactions under the Outer Space Treaty.

Sources: ATMOS Space Cargo, @MBDADeutschland (post), Resilience Media, hartpunkt, Opex360.

HENSOLDT hands Spexer radar series production to car-parts maker ZF

HENSOLDT and ZF Friedrichshafen signed a cooperation agreement on 18 November at Germany’s Economy Ministry, with Minister Katherina Reiche present. ZF will set up scalable series production of Spexer counter-drone radars and an automated test system, making the radars and delivering them directly to customers, while all intellectual property stays with HENSOLDT. The Spexer 2000 is part of Rheinmetall’s Skyranger 30 and of the Bundeswehr’s short-range air defence programme (NNbS). CEO Oliver Dörre called it “a key element in reliably meeting the structurally rising demand in the long term”.

Why it matters for Europe. A European car supplier (about 153,000 staff, €38.8 billion in 2025 sales) moves into defence electronics, keeping industrial jobs and adding capacity with IP kept in Europe: a model for a car sector under pressure from energy costs.

What to watch. Volumes and site; other automotive conversions.

Sources: HENSOLDT, @hensoldt (post).

Dutch marines’ “Kaaiman”: a US JLTV chosen over a European bid

Oshkosh Defense said it hosted the Netherlands Ministry of Defence this week to view the prototype of the Dutch Expeditionary Patrol Vehicle (DXPV) “Kaaiman”, built on its JLTV line. The Netherlands ordered 150 vehicles for the Korps Mariniers in June 2025, with deliveries from 2028. Defensie’s own magazine says the tender ended with one European and one American finalist, and that the European bid “was not sufficient on a number of critical points”.

Why it matters for Europe. Another European fleet goes to a US platform. European light tactical vehicles exist, such as the Rheinmetall/Mercedes-Benz Caracal already in Dutch and German service; the requirement gap cited (fording to 1.5 m, salt-water corrosion) is one European industry could close through joint EDF/EDIP-funded development. Each such purchase means lost jobs and R&D and dependence on US export permission.

What to watch. Prototype trials in 2027 and deliveries from 2028.

Sources: @OshkoshDefense (post), Oshkosh Defense, Defensie, Defense News.

Round-up: TEKEVER’s French factory, a US radar order and Vulcan’s failure cause

TEKEVER (Portugal) in France: the DGA recapped the new TEKEVER factory at Fontanes near Cahors, inaugurated on 13 November: a €100 million plant and a €43 million contract for 15 AR3 tactical drone systems, first deliveries by year-end; TEKEVER is in exclusive negotiations for deep-support drones. A European supplier building in another EU state, with French payloads. Navantia (Spain) held an industry day in Copenhagen with more than 40 Danish companies. Leidos won a $40.8 million US Marine Corps award for 14 Medium Range Air Defense Radars. Shield AI plans a 420,000 sq ft factory in Des Moines. ULA says its first data review points to “inadequate fuel supply from the vehicle to the BE-4 engines” as the cause of low booster thrust on the 15 November Vulcan launch. No new US arms-sale notification since Albania’s HIMARS (17 November); next launches: Falcon 9 USSF-317 (19 November) and Ariane 64 VA271 (23 November).

Sources: @DGA (post), TEKEVER, @NavantiaOficial (post), @LeidosInc (post), @shieldaitech (post), ULA, US State Department.
