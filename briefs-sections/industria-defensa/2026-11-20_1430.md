## MBDA Deutschland and ATMOS to study precision strike from space

**German in-space logistics start-up ATMOS Space Cargo has signed a cooperation agreement with MBDA Deutschland to explore a capability for precision strikes against ground targets from space, using reentry vehicles (SpaceNews, 19 Nov; Defense News).**

According to SpaceNews' Andrew Parsonson, it is an industry-led initiative, not a response to an existing government programme, betting that nations will seek such capabilities in the coming years.

**Why it matters for Europe.** A European-owned missile maker and a European start-up are moving into a capability until recently seen as taboo, without waiting for a US-led framework. It is an intra-European initiative; whether governments fund it will show if Europe wants sovereign options in space-based strike or leaves the field to the US and China.

**Sources:** [spacenews.com](https://spacenews.com/atmos-and-mbda-to-explore-space-based-precision-strike-system/), [defensenews.com](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/)

## UK minister: the US should build a British frigate design in America

**In an op-ed in Breaking Defense (18 Nov), Luke Pollard, the UK's Minister for Defence Readiness and Industry, argues that the US Navy should procure a UK frigate design and produce it in the United States, as Washington rebuilds its shipbuilding base.**

The British Embassy's defence account in Washington pushed the piece on the US list today.

**Why it matters for Europe.** Linked to the 08:30 item on the US Navy nominee's idea of a foreign-built shipyard on US soil: European designs are welcome in the US only if the work, jobs and yards move to America, while Washington opposes any European preference in Europe's own market. The asymmetry (US content rules, ITAR) runs one way.

**Sources:** [breakingdefense.com](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/britain-has-the-frigate-the-us-needs-and-americans-should-build-it-uk-minister/)

## Europe still seeks an "indispensable" role in the revised Artemis programme

**Nearly eight months after NASA made major changes to Artemis, Europe is still working out how to adapt and remain "indispensable", SpaceNews reports from Bremen (20 Nov).**

ESA is considering upgrading or augmenting the European Service Module so Orion can enter low lunar orbit.

**Why it matters for Europe.** Europe's place in lunar exploration depends on US programme decisions it does not control. Artemis is a US-led bloc; CEAFE's line is that Europe should keep options open with other partners rather than tie its space future to one ally's changing plans.

**Sources:** [spacenews.com](https://spacenews.com/europe-seeks-indispensable-new-roles-in-artemis/)

## ESA selects Plasma Observatory; Kongsberg wins LISA transponders

**ESA member states selected Plasma Observatory, seven coordinated spacecraft to track how solar energy moves through Earth's magnetic shield, as the agency's next medium-class mission (ESA, 19 Nov).**

Separately, Kongsberg (Norway) signed a contract with Antwerp Space (Belgium) to deliver X-band deep-space transponders for ESA's LISA gravitational-wave mission, ESA's third Large-class mission; value undisclosed (Kongsberg, 19 Nov).

**Why it matters for Europe.** Intra-European space supply chains (Norwegian and Belgian suppliers on an ESA mission) — the cooperative model that gives Europe scale.

**Sources:** [esa.int](https://esa.int/articles/seven-spacecraft-observatory-to-study-plasma-universe), [kongsberg.com](https://www.kongsberg.com/news/news-archive/2026/kongsberg-awarded-contract-to-support-esas-lisa-mission-with-deep-space-transponders/)

## China delivers a Y-9E military transport aircraft to Kazakhstan

**Kazakhstan's Defence Ministry announced on Thursday that a Chinese Y-9E military transport aircraft has arrived in Almaty and joined the fleet of the Kazakh Armed Forces' military transport aviation (Global Times, Chinese state media).**

Global Times stressed the aircraft's "mature technology". The post was reposted by @F_CEAFE. Kazakhstan is a member of the Russian-led Collective Security Treaty Organization (CSTO).

**Why it matters for Europe.** China is no longer only a buyer of Western and Russian technology: it is becoming an arms exporter and security provider in Central Asia, Russia's traditional backyard, and across the markets where European industry competes. The same day, US lawmakers pushed to deny certification to Chinese-made aircraft (see Economy).

**What to watch.** Further Chinese deliveries to Central Asian armed forces; Russia's reaction.
