BAE Systems plans to cut up to 1,070 UK jobs while its order book hits £33 billion

BAE Systems told staff on 18 November that it is consulting on cutting up to 1,070 roles, about 940 of them in its air business in north-west England (Warton and Samlesbury in Lancashire) and the rest at its head office, Aviation Week and local media report.

The company says it wants to be “efficient, agile and affordable” and aims to make the cuts through voluntary redundancies; shop-floor manufacturing, aircraft maintenance at RAF bases and early-career trainees are excluded. The move comes six days after a market update citing £33 billion of orders so far in 2026 and an expected 10–12% rise in underlying operating profit. The Unite union, which first disclosed the plan, blamed the government for “not making critical decisions” and called for new Eurofighter Typhoon and Collaborative Combat Aircraft contracts. The Ministry of Defence pointed to a £708 million GCAP contract extension for BAE and the £8 billion Typhoon sale to Turkey.

Why it matters for Europe. BAE’s air sector builds Eurofighter Typhoon, assembles the GCAP demonstrator and makes parts for the US F-35. Cutting engineering and support staff at the heart of Europe’s combat-air base, while governments say they are rearming, shows that budgets alone do not keep capacity: firm, multi-year orders do. Every European order for a US fighter is an order not placed for Typhoon, Rafale, Gripen or, later, GCAP and FCAS. Without common European demand, Europe’s own industrial base will keep shrinking even in a boom.

What to watch. UK decisions on new Typhoon orders and a UK collaborative combat aircraft; GCAP funding.

The Netherlands joins the European CAVS armoured-vehicle programme

The Netherlands signed the Technical Arrangement of the Common Armoured Vehicle System (CAVS) programme on 19 November at the NEDS 2026 defence fair in Rotterdam, with State Secretary for Defence Derk Boswijk signing.

It is the first step towards becoming the eighth participating nation, after Finland, Latvia, Sweden, Germany, Denmark, Norway and the United Kingdom. Finland’s Patria leads development of the 6x6 vehicle; within CAVS it has orders for nearly 2,000 vehicles including options and has delivered nearly 500, with much of the production done by participating nations’ own industries. The programme received €60 million from the EU’s EDIRPA joint-procurement instrument. Dutch parliamentary answers name the Patria 6x6 as a possible candidate to replace CBRN reconnaissance vehicles; numbers and costs are not yet set.

Background: CEAFE has followed CAVS since it began in 2020 as a Finnish-led programme around the Patria 6x6; Sweden joined in 2021, Germany became a full member in January 2025 and Denmark became the fifth nation in April 2025, ordering 129 vehicles through it. #BuyEuropean in practice.

Why it matters for Europe. This is the model CEAFE supports: European states buying one European vehicle together, sharing requirements, spreading production across national industries and securing supply. CAVS, with EU co-funding, now spans the Nordic and Baltic states, Germany, the UK and soon the Netherlands. No EU member state is big enough alone; pooled demand is how Europe gets scale.

What to watch. The Dutch order (numbers, CBRN variant) and whether other states join.

ESA signs €102 million with OHB for the ClearSpace-1 debris-removal mission

ESA awarded the German satellite maker OHB a €102 million contract to build ClearSpace-1, a spacecraft that will capture and de-orbit ESA’s Proba-1 satellite after it retires, European Spaceflight reports.

The contract was signed on 17 November at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen and opens the implementation phase; launch is planned for 2030. OHB took over as prime in 2024, with the Swiss start-up ClearSpace keeping close-proximity and capture operations, after the original target, a Vega adapter, was hit by debris in 2023. The launch, first booked on Vega C, is now planned on a dedicated microlauncher; an OHB slide from July appears to show RFA ONE, built by Rocket Factory Augsburg, in which OHB holds 56.8%, though neither ESA nor OHB has confirmed it.

Background: ESA picked the Swiss start-up ClearSpace for the first debris-removal mission in 2020 and, as CEAFE noted in April 2024, handed mission leadership to Germany’s OHB.

Why it matters for Europe. Active debris removal and in-orbit servicing are part of space security, and the same capture technology underpins rendezvous and inspection in orbit. Keeping it in a European industrial chain, possibly launched by a European microlauncher, builds sovereign know-how in a field the US and China are moving fast in.

What to watch. Confirmation of the launcher; Space Tech Expo Bremen coverage (2026-11-17_0730.md).

Sources: @AndrewParsonson, 19 Nov 2026, @F_CEAFE

Ariane 6 to launch 36 Amazon Leo satellites on 23 November

ESA announced that Ariane 6 will lift off from Europe’s Spaceport in Kourou on 23 November carrying 36 Amazon Leo satellites, with the broadcast from 12:39 CET.

ESA also said its member states will meet in Rome on 15 December “to shape Europe’s future in space exploration”; Director General Josef Aschbacher wrote that “the world is not waiting for Europe” and that Europe has “a choice to make”.

Why it matters for Europe. A European heavy launcher earning money from a US mega-constellation helps Ariane 6’s cadence and finances. But it also means Europe’s rocket is helping a US competitor deploy its broadband network while the EU’s own sovereign constellation, IRIS², is still years from service. The 15 December meeting in Rome is where member states decide how ambitious Europe will be in exploration.

What to watch. Launch outcome (to be logged in the launches tracker); ESA meeting in Rome.

Sources: @esa, livestream, @esa, quoting @AschbacherJosef

European Parliament’s industry committee adopts its position on the EU Space Act

Defence and Space Commissioner Andrius Kubilius congratulated the European Parliament’s industry committee (ITRE) on adopting its position on the EU Space Act, thanking rapporteur Elena Donazzan.

He wrote that as the EU scales up its ambitions in space “it needs to have a functional space single market” and that he looks forward to the Council’s position and the start of trilogues. The committee text itself was not checked in this run.

Why it matters for Europe. A single set of EU rules on safety, resilience and sustainability for space activities would replace 27 national regimes, a precondition for European firms to scale. It also matters for non-EU operators serving the EU market, including US constellations.

What to watch. Plenary vote and Council general approach.

Sources: @KubiliusA

EDF-funded Euroguard semi-autonomous vessel launched in Estonia

Commission official Florian Arbault (DG DEFIS) said he represented the Commission, alongside Estonia’s President Ülle Madise, at the launch of the Euroguard ship.

Euroguard, a European Defence Fund project, brings together 29 partners from 10 countries to develop a versatile semi-autonomous vessel. Further details were not checked in this run.

Why it matters for Europe. A multinational, EU-funded naval drone is the kind of joint programme that can give Europe its own answer to US uncrewed-vessel offers.

Sources: @ArbaultF
