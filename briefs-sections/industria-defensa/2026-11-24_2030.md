## US private-equity firm Cerberus buys Airbus's US satellite business

Cerberus Capital Management (United States) announced on 24 November that it has agreed to acquire the Space Systems business of Airbus U.S. Space & Defense, the American arm of Airbus, with closing expected by the end of the year (Breaking Defense, SpaceNews). The unit builds satellites at a factory of about 200,000 square feet in Merritt Island, Florida, next to the Kennedy Space Center; SpaceNews notes that the deal brings a factory built for OneWeb's broadband constellation under American ownership. No price was disclosed, and Airbus does not break out the revenue of its US arm.

Airbus says its US subsidiary will "double down" on autonomous aircraft production; Cerberus says it will invest in the unit's engineering and products to meet demand for "U.S. spacecraft production capacity". **Background (CEAFE archive):** in September 2026 @F_CEAFE shared the news that Eutelsat had ordered 229 additional low-Earth-orbit OneWeb satellites from Airbus, on top of the 440 Airbus-built satellites already in the constellation.

*CEAFE:* Technological and defence dependence is not abstract: it shows up in the number of companies, in spending and in the presence on the ground.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the factory that mass-produced the satellites of OneWeb, now part of Europe's Eutelsat, passes to a US financial investor just as Europe is trying to build IRIS² and its own sovereign constellations. A European prime withdraws from US satellite manufacturing. The asymmetry is plain: a European-owned plant on US soil works under US control rules (CFIUS review, FOCI mitigation, ITAR), which limit what Airbus can share with Europe; no equivalent common European regime protects Europe's own assets when US capital buys in.

**What to watch:** the CFIUS review; who will build Eutelsat OneWeb's replacement satellites; Airbus's plans for its US drone business.

## Poland and the UK sign agreement to develop the CAMM-MR air-defence missile

Poland and the United Kingdom signed an intergovernmental agreement on 24 November that opens the way to the joint development of CAMM-MR, a medium-range air-defence missile meant to reach beyond 100 km, according to Poland's Armament Agency. Secretary of State Magdalena Sobkowiak-Czarnecka signed for Poland and defence procurement minister Luke Pollard for the UK; MBDA, its Polish arm and the state group PGZ are on the industry side. The agreement does not fund the missile: it sets the "legal and financial conditions" for the detailed design contract, to be followed by an executive agreement and later a supply contract, timed with the end of deliveries of Poland's three Miecznik frigates. No dates or cost were given. The agency calls it the first international project to develop a missile for Polish air defence.

@F_CEAFE quoted the agency's post: "Why can't Poland buy 🇪🇺 weapons? 🇺🇸🇰🇷🇬🇧... Should we, the rest of the EU partners, keep funding them? #Polexit". **Background (CEAFE archive):** in April 2022 @F_CEAFE shared the Polish defence ministry's announcement that Poland would acquire elements of MBDA's CAMM system and integrate them with Polish components.

**Why it matters for Europe:** MBDA is a pan-European group, but this programme is run with the UK, now outside the Union, and sits alongside Poland's large purchases of US and South Korean equipment. CEAFE's point is the double standard: an EU member state that receives EU cohesion and defence funds, and benefits from the European-preference rules of EU instruments, while equipping itself largely outside the Union.

**What to watch:** the executive agreement and its work share; whether CAMM-MR is put forward for EU SAFE loans or EDIP funding, which favour EU content.

## MI5 warns British defence executives of Russian proxy arson and sabotage

MI5, the UK's domestic security service, has warned British defence executives that they face a growing threat of arson and other sabotage carried out by proxies working for Russia, Bloomberg reported on 24 November; AP reports that firms aiding Ukraine have been told to brace for sabotage attempts. The full articles could not be checked.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Europe's defence industrial base, which is ramping up production for Ukraine and for rearmament, is itself a target. Protecting plants and staff becomes a cost of production and an argument for common European industrial-security standards.

**What to watch:** whether other European governments issue similar warnings to their defence suppliers.

## EU money for defence: EUROGUARD vessel and €68m proposed for space defence R&T

The European Commission (DG DEFIS) says the EUROGUARD project has launched a 45-metre semi-autonomous vessel for sea surveillance and the protection of critical infrastructure, including undersea cables, with €65m from the European Defence Fund. Separately, the European Defence Agency says EU member states proposed €68m for space defence research and technology at its CapTech Space meeting in Italy; projects include ASSAI (AI to protect satellites) and LEO2VLEO (three satellites in very low Earth orbit).

**Why it matters for Europe:** common funding buys capabilities that no EU member state would build alone, from seabed protection after the Baltic cable incidents to satellite defence. The sums remain small next to US programmes.

**What to watch:** EDA's selection of the space projects and the vessel's trials.

**Sources:** https://x.com/i/status/2125288344859062384, https://link.europa.eu/tynmMH, https://x.com/i/status/2125259241438994624

## A US launcher builds its pad in Sweden; Canada joins the NATO Innovation Fund

Firefly Aerospace (US) says construction of Launch Complex 3 at Esrange Space Center in Sweden is "well underway" in partnership with Sweden's SSC. Separately, Defense One reports from Halifax that Canada has joined the NATO Innovation Fund while promising "business as usual" with the US, a move Defense One describes as a reaction to dwindling cooperation from the Trump administration.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Esrange is one of Europe's few continental launch sites; giving a pad to a US launcher competes with Europe's own small launchers (Isar Aerospace, RFA, PLD Space, Orbex). Canada's step, by contrast, shows a NATO member diversifying towards European and multinational instruments.

**What to watch:** the first Firefly launch date from Esrange; Canada's first investments through the fund.
