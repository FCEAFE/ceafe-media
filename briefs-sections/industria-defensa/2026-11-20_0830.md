## US lawmakers want Airbus to lose US defence contracts over its China investments

**The House Select Committee on China released a report on 19 November recommending that the US government refuse FAA certification of Chinese-made aircraft and pull government contracts from aerospace firms, Airbus included, that do not halt investments in China's aerospace industrial base (Reuters; committee).**

The committee says Western firms "inadvertently participated" in China's military-civil fusion strategy and calls for tighter export controls and investment restrictions in the sector. It singles out Airbus's Tianjin A320 final assembly lines, whose wings come from Xi'an Aircraft Industry Group, which also builds Chinese military aircraft; chairman John Moolenaar has also accused the French government of controlling what Airbus may disclose (AeroTime). The report also names US firms — Honeywell, GE Aerospace and Collins — as having obtained licences for controlled transfers involving Chinese military end users (committee). These are recommendations, not law.

**Why it matters for Europe.** This is extraterritorial pressure on Europe's flagship aerospace group: a US congressional body proposing to use US defence contracts as leverage over a European company's commercial strategy, while US suppliers named in the same report obtained US licences for their own China transfers. It is a reminder that Europe has no equivalent leverage over US firms, and that dependence on the US defence market exposes European primes to US policy. Watch for a European (Commission/French government) response.

**What to watch.** Whether any recommendation moves into the NDAA or executive action; Airbus and Paris reactions.

## Spain approves over €6.4bn in zero-interest loans for defence modernisation programmes

**Spain's Council of Ministers approved a royal decree regulating zero-interest, multi-year loans from the Ministry of Industry to finance the armed forces' Special Modernisation Programmes (PEM).**

Spanish press reports the package at more than €6.4bn (Europa Press, Infodefensa, El Español, elEconomista); one outlet and a secondary brief give €6.9bn, so the total is unconfirmed. Beneficiaries named include Indra, GMV, Airbus, EM&E (Escribano) and the Spanish Eurofighter consortium, for programmes including PNT/Galileo systems, helicopters, digitalisation of the UME, counter-drone systems and air-platform upgrades.

**Why it matters for Europe.** Positive: national money flowing to European-built programmes (Eurofighter, Galileo-based PNT, Airbus helicopters) rather than off-the-shelf non-European purchases.

**What to watch.** The BOE text and the per-programme breakdown.

## Leonardo DRS wins THAAD seeker work and plans to quadruple seeker output

**Leonardo DRS, the US subsidiary majority-owned by Italy's Leonardo, said on 19 November it has a contract from BAE Systems to develop a next-generation infrared focal plane array for the THAAD interceptor.**

Work is under way at its 200,000 sq ft Dallas foundry, which DRS is expanding to support the programme's aim of raising seeker production to about four times historical levels. No value was given.

**Why it matters for Europe.** A European group growing inside the US missile-defence supply chain — but through a US-listed subsidiary operating under US control rules, so the technology stays American. Europe has no comparable domestic demand pull for its own interceptor seekers.

**What to watch.** Contract value; THAAD production ramp after the Iran war drawdown.

## Navy under-secretary nominee proposes a foreign-built shipyard in the US for frigates

**At his Senate confirmation hearing on 19 November, William Toti, nominee for Under Secretary of the Navy, proposed setting up a new "world-class" shipyard in the US, built by a foreign shipbuilder, focused on frigates and large surface combatants (Breaking Defense).**

Foreign shipbuilding is a sticking point between the administration, which wants some Navy vessels built overseas (the "Finland model"), and Congress, which wants to limit that.

**Why it matters for Europe.** European yards (Fincantieri already owns Marinette Marine) and Asian ones are the candidates; the US model is to bring foreign capacity onshore under US control, the reverse of Europe's habit of buying finished platforms abroad.

**Sources:** [breakingdefense.com](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/toti-pushes-new-foreign-built-shipyard-in-the-us-to-focus-on-frigates-large-surface-combatants/), [· @BreakingDefense](https://x.com/i/status/2123524406949372274)

## Senate Republicans shelve a $350bn defence top-up as Iran-war costs and munitions gaps grow

**Senate Republicans are suspending their effort to pass $350bn in additional military spending, as momentum fades to pay for the Iran war and other priorities before the GOP loses control of Congress in January (WSJ).**

The same night, Al Jazeera reported that the GAO found the Pentagon lacks an overarching plan for persistent munitions shortages, and Xinhua reported a Pentagon official telling lawmakers the Iran war had cost $48.3bn as of 13 November, up about 27% from $38bn.

**Why it matters for Europe.** US munitions shortages and a stalled top-up mean slower deliveries to European FMS customers (Patriot, THAAD, GMLRS) — an argument for European production capacity.

## Update: Vulcan's failure tightens the Space Force launch bottleneck

**Air & Space Forces Magazine reports that ULA's 15 November Vulcan Centaur failure, which destroyed 40 Amazon Leo satellites, tightens the Space Force's launch bottleneck; ULA blamed "inadequate fuel supply from the vehicle to the BE-4 engines".**

Blue Origin's New Glenn has not flown since May after a pad explosion, leaving SpaceX with a near monopoly on heavy lift. SpaceX separately said Starship Flight 15's Super Heavy booster performed a safe abort and splashed down off Starbase instead of being caught.

**Why it matters for Europe.** US launch concentration on one private supplier contrasts with Europe's need to keep Ariane 6 and Vega C flying; Ariane 6 carries Amazon Leo satellites on 23 Nov.
