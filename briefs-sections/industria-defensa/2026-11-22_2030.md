## German army tests Helsing and Stark loitering munitions and Helsing's Clarity AI tool in Lithuania

**Germany's army began its first tactical tests of loitering munitions on 22 November at the Rudninkai training ground in Lithuania, near the Belarusian border, where army chief Lt-Gen Christian Freuding watched munitions made by the German companies Helsing and Stark, fitted with inert warheads, hit main battle tank targets (Reuters).**

The tests are part of Freedom Shield, the largest German drills in Lithuania to date. Days earlier, troops ran their first tests of Helsing's AI targeting software Clarity, which fuses satellite, drone and sensor data and recommends action. Germany only decided in 2025 to buy loitering munitions; Panzerbrigade 45 (about 5,000 troops and 2,000 vehicles) is due to be fully operational by the end of 2027.

**Context:** on 20 November Defense News reported that two German one-way attack drone variants passed live-fire trials ahead of first deliveries. The 20 November 14:30 edition covered Helsing's Clarity as a European alternative to Palantir's Maven Smart System adopted by NATO.

**Why it matters for Europe:** both the munitions and the AI targeting layer come from European firms, in a field where the reference software supplier for NATO has been the US company Palantir.

**What to watch:** order volumes and delivery dates; Clarity's adoption beyond the Lithuania brigade.

## UK to fund production of Ukrainian-designed strike drones on British soil

**Ukraine's Defence Minister Evgeniy Khmara and UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting signed project arrangements in Kyiv on 21 November for the joint production in the United Kingdom of Ukrainian-designed middle-strike and deep-strike drones, funded by London, with the finished drones going to the Ukrainian Armed Forces (Ukraine's Ministry of Defence).**

The two sides also discussed Ukraine's long-range capabilities and the modernisation of Ukrainian-made missiles; the drone models were not named. Separately, Khmara met Sweden's Supreme Commander, General Michael Claesson, to discuss the next steps in transferring Gripen fighters to Ukraine, their armament (including Meteor missiles) and the delivery schedule (Ukrainian MoD).

**Why it matters for Europe:** the arrangement moves production of Ukrainian designs into a European country outside the EU; whether such lines can connect with SAFE and other EU instruments is an open question for the European defence industrial base.

**What to watch:** which firms and sites build the drones, and in what volumes; Gripen delivery dates and numbers.

## EU's 2 GHz satellite-band plan, partly for IRIS², draws US pressure over Starlink

**The EU plans to reallocate the 2 GHz mobile satellite services band before the Viasat/EchoStar licences expire in May 2027: according to Space Law Journal, one third would go to a secure system linked to IRIS², one third to new European operators and one third would be open, with Starlink able to bid.**

Candidates would need an ITU filing from an EU member state (or contractual access to one); Starlink would be capped at 10 MHz and would have to control its satellites from the EU. The European Parliament's ITRE committee has voted and ministers aim for a deal on 1 December. Les Echos reports that Washington has threatened retaliation and that Europe says it "will not change position".

**Context:** the 14:30 edition covered NATO armed forces' dependence on private networks such as Starshield.

**Why it matters for Europe:** spectrum is the basis of sovereign satellite connectivity; the outcome will show whether the EU can reserve capacity for IRIS² and European operators under pressure from the US and its dominant provider.

**What to watch:** the 1 December Council meeting; any formal US trade measure.

## Boom Supersonic fires its first engine core prototype

**Boom Supersonic fired its first engine core prototype, the shared "Sprint Core", on 21 November, reaching 6,500 rpm; the complete Symphony turbofan has not yet run (Aviation Week).**

Boom presents the 42 MW core as serving AI data-centre power first and supersonic jets second, and unveiled a "Superpower Superfactory". Founder Blake Scholl posted: "A new engine was born today".

**Why it matters for Europe:** a US start-up is building its own engine and a dual-use power business; Rolls-Royce withdrew from Boom's engine work in 2022, and Europe's civil engine know-how remains with Safran and Rolls-Royce.

**What to watch:** the first full Symphony engine run.

**Sources:** [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/aerospace/emerging-technologies/boom-fires-first-superpower-engine-core-prototype), [Boom Supersonic](https://boomsupersonic.com/press-release/superpower-superfactory), [Blake Scholl](https://x.com/i/status/2124529594006995296)
