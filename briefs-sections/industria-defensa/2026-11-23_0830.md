## Update: Patriot interceptor output hinges on a French fabric made only by ArianeGroup

**The Pentagon's push to ramp up Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptor production runs into a chokepoint: Raycarb C2B, a carbonised rayon fabric that protects the rocket motor's nozzle components from extreme heat, is made only by ArianeGroup in France, according to a Wall Street Journal investigation summarised by AeroTime.**

ArianeGroup supplies the fabric to Kansas-based Park Aerospace, which turns it into a heat-resistant composite; Park's CEO told investors on 8 October 2026 that the firm is "sole-source qualified" on the PAC-3 MSE rocket motor. L3Harris builds the motor and Lockheed Martin assembles the missile in Camden, Arkansas. The material grew out of work begun in 1996 by Snecma Propulsion Solide (now ArianeGroup), backed by France's CNES and DGA; it is also used on the US Navy's SM-3. Park agreed on 27 March 2025 to advance ArianeGroup €4.59 million against future purchases to fund new equipment in France, but extra capacity will take several years.

**Context:** Antecedentes: on 18 November @F_CEAFE quoted the first WSJ report, which described a supply chain where the fibre is produced in the Czech Republic, woven in France and carbonised by ArianeGroup near Bordeaux ([X](https://x.com/i/status/2123065001279737984)).

**Why it matters for Europe:** the usual case is Europe's dependence on US systems such as Patriot; here a key US interceptor depends on a European technology developed with French state support and made near Bordeaux by the firm that also builds the nozzles of Ariane 6. Every European Patriot order also waits in this queue. The European alternatives in ground-based air defence are SAMP/T NG and the Aster missiles by Eurosam (MBDA and Thales).

**What to watch:** the timing of ArianeGroup's capacity expansion; whether the US seeks a second, domestic source.

**Sources:** [WSJ](https://x.com/i/status/2124722663029358978), [AeroTime](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/french-fabric-bottleneck-patriot-missile-production-wsj), [WSJ (18 Nov)](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/patriot-missile-rayon-problem-3f39dc98)

## WSJ: Ukraine's defence industry has probably reached its growth ceiling

**The Wall Street Journal reports that Ukraine's defence industry, which expanded rapidly after 2022, has probably reached a ceiling.**

Funding is spread across more than 1,300 companies, which leaves the largest producers short of money to expand; skilled workers are hard to find; and factories cannot safely scale up while Russia strikes them. Procurement is decentralised, with individual commanders buying equipment, according to a former US Navy officer who advised Kyiv on production. Firms are increasingly seeking foreign investment and production sites abroad.

**Context:** on 22 November the UK announced it would fund the production of Ukrainian-designed strike drones on British soil (20:30 edition).

**Why it matters for Europe:** the search for production sites abroad points to Ukrainian designs being built in EU member states and the UK, under models such as the "Danish model" and the EU's SAFE instrument.

**What to watch:** new EU–Ukraine joint production agreements under SAFE.

**Sources:** [WSJ](https://x.com/i/status/2124663861038940235), [Voennoe Delo](https://voennoedelo.com/en/posts/id22544-wsj-ukraine-s-defense-industry-faces-production-limits), [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/9127321)

## India seeks 20% more defence capital spending to close the Rafale and Project 75(I) deals

**India's defence ministry has asked for a 20% increase in capital outlay for 2027–28 as it seeks to finalise big contracts, Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said at a FICCI event (Times of India).**

They include a proposed deal of about Rs 3.25 lakh crore with France for 114 Dassault Rafale jets and one of nearly Rs 90,000 crore with Germany for six TKMS conventional submarines under Project 75(I). Separately, the ministry signed a Rs 1,976.40 crore contract with Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI) to upgrade the Barak-1 point-defence system on Indian warships, in service since about 2000.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the two largest pending deals in India are French and German, in a market long contested by Russian, US and Israeli suppliers.

**What to watch:** India's 2027–28 budget (February); Rafale and Project 75(I) contract signatures.

**Sources:** [Times of India (budget)](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/defence/news/with-big-deals-on-anvil-defence-ministry-seeks-rs-20-hike-in-budget-allocation-for-2027-28/articleshow/136031621.cms), [Times of India (Barak-1)](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/defence/news/indias-ageing-barak-missile-shield-gets-rs-1976-crore-upgrade-what-changes/articleshow/136018917.cms)

## Update: Arianespace postpones the Ariane 6 launch (10th mission, VA271, 36 Amazon Leo satellites)

**Arianespace has postponed Ariane 6 flight VA271, which was due to place 36 Amazon Leo satellites into orbit from Kourou today at 12:09 Madrid time, after a technical event affecting the launcher's ground support systems.**

The launch chronology was interrupted before the mobile gantry was removed and before launcher system operations began. Arianespace says Ariane 6 and its 36 Amazon Leo satellites remain in stable and safe conditions. Technical teams are assessing the event and preparing for operations to resume; the next launch opportunity is no earlier than 24 November, with further updates to follow. The flight would have been Ariane 6's tenth and Arianespace's fourth for Amazon, in the Ariane 64 configuration.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Ariane 6 was due to carry a US commercial constellation while the EU's own IRIS² constellation is still years away.

**What to watch:** Arianespace's next official update (14:30 edition); whether a new launch date is confirmed.

**Sources:** [Arianespace](https://newsroom.arianespace.com/mission-va271le-04-launch-update/?lang=eng), [Arianespace on X](https://x.com/Arianespace/status/2124751667795022008)

## Colombia files charges against Starlink over signals reaching prisons

**Colombia's ICT ministry (MinTIC) has opened an administrative investigation and filed charges against Starlink Colombia S.A.S. for allegedly failing to block telecommunications signals covering prisons, according to the Colombian presidency.**

A raid at Bogotá's La Picota prison found six Starlink antennas, and MinTIC technical checks found Starlink-provided Wi-Fi in four prisons. Local media report that charges also cover Claro, Tigo and WOM.

**Why it matters for Europe:** a state applying its national law to a US satellite operator, the same question the EU faces in deciding how much secure connectivity to leave to Starlink rather than IRIS² and European operators.

**What to watch:** Starlink's response; possible fines.
