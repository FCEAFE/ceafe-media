## Indra's Forward2030: Spain's defence champion aims to more than double in size by 2030

At its investor day on 25 November, Indra Group presented its Forward2030 strategic plan (Indra press release). Targets: revenue above €12bn in 2030 (more than double the 2025 close), EBIT above €1.5bn with a margin over 12.5%, cash generation over €3.1bn in 2026–2030, and an ambition of €20bn revenue in 2035 with 50% earned abroad. Indra will put €1bn of its own money into R&D and €240m into reskilling. The group is reorganised around three axes, Defence and Space, Intelligent Technology and Critical Infrastructure, with IndraMind (sovereign AI, command and control and integration; portfolio of €400m) as the transversal layer. Its order book is over €22bn. Bloomberg reports that Indra raised its guidance as new chairman Ángel Simón presented the strategy.

**Context:** Antecedentes: @F_CEAFE has followed Indra's role in European programmes: in July 2024 it reposted Lockheed Martin's SPY-7 radar manufacturing orders to Indra for Spain's F-110 frigates (a US radar at the heart of a Spanish ship); in February 2026 OCCAR signed the co-funding contract for the Indra-led REACT II electronic-attack programme ([X, Feb. 2026](https://x.com/i/status/2020096115614335173)); and on 9 November 2026 it reposted reports that Spain is negotiating with Germany to produce the future fighter, with Airbus and Indra taking on the project ([X, Nov. 2026](https://x.com/i/status/2119759031082315902)).

The contrast in scale makes clear that national strategies, however ambitious, are not enough against competitors of a different order of magnitude.

**Why it matters for Europe:** a Spanish group setting out to become a "product company" in defence and space adds European scale. Even at €12bn, Indra would stay far below the large US primes; European growth plans only close that gap if they are joined to common programmes (FCAS, ESSOR, EDF) rather than run as national champions.

## Belgium opens Europe's largest satellite factory, which will build IRIS² satellites

King Philippe inaugurated on 24 November Aerospacelab's "megafactory" in Charleroi, presented as the EU's largest satellite factory, with an annual capacity of about 500 satellites (Euronews/AFP). It is to produce satellites for the EU's IRIS² secure-connectivity constellation, the European answer to Starlink, with IRIS² production to start by the end of 2028. CEO Benoit Deper said the company grew from a "tiny startup" in 2018 to a player in Europe's "space sovereignty".

**Context:** Antecedentes: @F_CEAFE has followed IRIS² since November 2022, when it argued that the EU should build R&D facilities so that new technologies are developed and mastered in Europe, "as with Galileo & Iris2" ([X, Nov. 2022](https://x.com/i/status/1594387040610623490)). In December 2024 ESA agreed to support the constellation's development; on 31 August 2026 SES awarded OHB a contract worth almost €1bn for 18 MEO satellite platforms ([X, Aug. 2026](https://x.com/i/status/2094485921127170265)); and on 1 November 2026 Canada's MDA Space secured a €64m IRIS² antenna agreement.

**Why it matters for Europe:** mass satellite production in a former industrial city is the kind of intra-European capacity that reduces dependence on SpaceX/Starlink; it only pays off if IRIS² keeps its funding (see item 3).

Sources: [Euronews](https://l.euronews.com/eLw0) (via [@euronews](https://x.com/euronews/status/2125526486501228975)).

## Update: Kubilius warns a 30% cut to defence and space in the next EU budget would endanger IRIS² and EU defence projects

EU Defence and Space Commissioner Andrius Kubilius wrote on X that "drastic cuts" to defence and space in the next Multiannual Financial Framework (30%, minus €39bn) would endanger the most important pan-European programmes: IRIS², the European Earth Observation Governmental Service (EOGS), and the European Defence Projects of Common Interest (Eastern Flank Watch, the drone defence initiative, air, maritime and space shields) and strategic enablers. "Putin escalates, EU cuts. Wrong signal!" The cut figures are his; the Council and Parliament negotiating texts were not checked this run.

Europe has resources; what it lacks, time and again, is the scale that only joint action can provide.

**Why it matters for Europe:** no member state can fund IRIS² or an air shield alone; cutting the common budget pushes capitals back to national, often US, off-the-shelf purchases.

Sources: [@KubiliusA 1/2](https://x.com/KubiliusA/status/2125565352578859346), [2/2](https://x.com/KubiliusA/status/2125565356127268997).

## Germany's 2027 budget lifts defence spending to €139.4bn

Germany plans total defence spending of €139.4bn in 2027, up €31.2bn from €108.2bn in 2026: €109.7bn in the regular budget plus €29.6bn from the Bundeswehr special fund set up in 2022 (Anadolu, citing the budget approved by the Bundestag budget committee, to be voted this week). Procurement is planned to exceed €60bn. Development aid falls to €9.58bn from €10.05bn and UN contributions are cut.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the biggest European procurement budget will shape the market; whether those €60bn+ go to European suppliers or to US systems (F-35, P-8, Arrow, Patriot) decides how much European industry gains.

Sources: [Anadolu](https://v.aa.com.tr/4137005).

## Czech engine maker PBS Aerospace aims to double its US production capacity

Defense News reports that Czech jet engine producer PBS Aerospace aims to at least double its manufacturing capacity in the United States, to grow as a supplier of small turbojets for US drones and missiles. Defense News also reports that Italian officials say Italy reached defence and domestic-security spending equal to 2.8% of GDP in 2026, a figure not visible in the defence budget itself. (Index summaries only; full articles not fetched.)

**Why it matters for Europe:** a European engine maker expanding in the US serves US programmes under US rules; Europe's own drone and missile programmes need the same small-engine capacity at home.

Sources: [Defense News Europe](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/).

## Space: Bulgaria's EnduroSat books two New Glenn launches; White House seeks $2bn more for Artemis

- **EnduroSat on New Glenn:** the Bulgarian satellite maker has booked two dedicated Blue Origin New Glenn launches from 2029 for its customers, as SpaceX winds down its Falcon 9 rideshare programme (SpaceNews). A European satellite firm again turns to a US launcher; Vega C, Ariane 6 and the coming European micro-launchers (Isar, RFA, MaiaSpace) are the European alternatives.
- **Artemis:** the White House asks Congress for up to $2bn more for NASA's Artemis programme in 2027, with few details on its use (SpaceNews).
- **Orbital data centres:** European companies seek supporting roles in US and Chinese orbital data centre projects rather than compete directly (SpaceNews, Bremen) — another sign of Europe settling for subcontractor roles.
- **Hera:** ESA releases Hera's first close-up images of asteroid Dimorphos tonight at 19:45 CET from Berlin; Spain's GMV and JAXA's TIRI instrument are on the mission (ESA, GMV, JAXA).
