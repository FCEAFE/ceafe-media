NATO Parliamentary Assembly in Berlin: Rutte warns of “intensifying malign activities” and the EU’s top soldier says Europe’s defence is NATO’s job

At the NATO Parliamentary Assembly’s annual session in Berlin, Secretary General Mark Rutte warned of “intensifying malign activities targeting our nations”: arson, drones over airports, sabotaged railways, cargo ships targeted at sea and “relentless” cyberattacks.

Rutte cited Friday’s “dangerous incident” in Poland and the Black Sea, where NATO works with Romania, Bulgaria and Türkiye. He called for more defence spending (several allies are projected to meet or exceed the 5% target this year) and more production of air defence, drones, counter-drone systems and deep-strike weapons, and said NATO would be able to produce about 4 million artillery shells a year by next year, nearly double the 2025 level. German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said NATO will “defend every inch” of its territory, called on Putin to “stop this war” and “come to the negotiation table”, and said Russia “is not seriously considering attacking NATO territory in the short term… because we are strong.” The same morning, the Chairman of the EU Military Committee, General Seán Clancy, told Euronews: “The defence and deterrence in the Euro-Atlantic area, including Europe, is NATO’s responsibility.” In Belgium, Defence Minister Theo Francken blamed a far-left collective for three suspected arson attacks on defence suppliers near Liège, including the radar and counter-drone maker BATS; the intelligence services are still checking the claim (Anadolu).

CEAFE: Without a common industrial, technological and defence base, Europe will remain dependent on decisions taken in Washington or Beijing.

Context: Antecedentes: in October 2019 @F_CEAFE already argued that a European army was the answer to Europe’s exposure, under the tag #EUdefence (X, 2019). The 5% target was agreed at NATO’s 2025 summit at US insistence.

Why it matters for Europe: the EU’s most senior military officer says Europe’s defence belongs to NATO, an alliance whose military commander (SACEUR) is a US officer, on the same day Rutte lists the threats on European soil. According to SIPRI, EU member states together spent about half as much as the US in 2025.

What to watch: NATO decisions after Monday’s North Atlantic Council on Poland; the Belgian arson investigation; the European Parliament plenary this week.

Sources: Anadolu (Rutte), NATO, Anadolu (Wadephul), Euronews (Clancy), Anadolu (Belgium), SIPRI

Nuclear signals from Moscow: Medvedev on the “obligation” to use nuclear weapons and a German warning about a nuclear-armed satellite

Dmitry Medvedev, deputy chairman of Russia’s Security Council, wrote on Monday that Ukraine and NATO countries “have already created situations” in which Russia considers a nuclear strike, although events have so far “not created a state of direct necessity” to use nuclear weapons.

Medvedev argued that if the conditions of Russia’s doctrine are met, the head of state is “not only entitled but obliged” to use them, and that Western deliveries of nuclear weapons to Kyiv, “open or covert”, or a massive use of non-nuclear means could justify a pre-emptive strike (Anadolu; TASS, Russian state agency). Separately, NDR, WDR and Süddeutsche Zeitung reported that the BND and Western partners warned that Russia may launch a nuclear-armed satellite, “Sputnik-S”, before the end of the year; Defence Minister Boris Pistorius has been briefed. The Russian Embassy in Berlin rejected the reports as “unfounded allegations” and “part of a propaganda campaign”.

Context: Antecedentes: in January 2023 @F_CEAFE posted Medvedev’s warning to NATO of nuclear war if Russia were defeated in Ukraine (X, 2023).

Why it matters for Europe: many European leaders treat Russia’s arsenal as unusable; CEAFE holds the opposite view, because Moscow keeps signalling that a war with Europe would be short and nuclear, and its warnings usually leave out the US. The current escalation at sea (Libya, Baltic, Black Sea) is taking place under that rhetoric. A nuclear detonation in low Earth orbit would disable satellites for months, according to the German reports.

What to watch: NATO and EU comments on the satellite reports; any Russian launch notice; whether other Russian officials repeat Medvedev’s framing.

Sources: Anadolu (Medvedev), TASS, Anadolu (satellite)

Five European states launch a defence-AI centre in Rennes, while Washington courts Polish and Italian industry

France’s Ministry of the Armed Forces announced the launch of the International Center for Artificial Intelligence in Defence (ICAID): Germany, Italy, Poland and Sweden formally announced their intention to join France in it at a conference in Rennes on 19–20 November.

The centre aims to speed up the adoption of military AI and share best practice and lessons from operations, and is open to other “affinity” states. The same morning, the US Ambassador to NATO promoted the “Transatlantic Defense Industry Access Forum”, which brought together more than 500 participants from the US defence industry with counterparts from Poland, Italy and Ukraine “to forge new partnerships and expand business opportunities”.

Context: France, Germany, Italy, Poland and Sweden are EU member states and NATO members. NATO chose Palantir’s Maven system as its AI-enabled targeting platform.

Why it matters for Europe: ICAID is the kind of intra-European cooperation CEAFE considers positive: states building a capability together instead of buying it. Poland and Italy appear on both sides on the same day; whether European defence AI stays European or depends on US software will decide who controls the data.

What to watch: which other states join ICAID; its budget and governance; contracts coming out of the US forum.

Sources: Ministère des Armées, Ministère des Armées on X, US Ambassador to NATO

Update: “Article 5-like” guarantees: Zelensky asks who would fight; Moscow says it will sound out European capitals

After Washington’s talk of “Article 5-like” guarantees (08:30 edition), Zelensky asked: “Which countries will support Ukraine if Ukraine is attacked again? What exactly will our partners do in response? Which troops will be stationed on our territory in advance?”

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow “will initiate contacts with European countries to determine their readiness to contribute to peace efforts” (TASS), while telling Vedomosti that Russia is in no hurry to appoint ambassadors to the UK and France because “there are no relations with these countries” (Anadolu). He called the Belgian prime minister’s opposition to seizing frozen Russian assets “measured”.

Context: the guarantees under discussion would rely mainly on the troops and money of the Coalition of the Willing’s participating states.

Why it matters for Europe: Moscow chooses to talk to individual European capitals; Europeans count at the peace table only if they speak with one voice, not as a list of national governments.

What to watch: answers from the E5 and the Coalition of the Willing’s participants; which capitals Moscow contacts.

Sources: Zelensky, Zelensky (2), TASS, Anadolu
