APEC in Shenzhen: Xi defends free trade and meets Putin before Thursday’s meeting with Trump

Xi Jinping opened the APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen on 18 November, warning against “rampant” protectionism and unilateralism and calling for free and open trade and global cooperation on AI (Bloomberg, Reuters, France 24).

He then met Vladimir Putin, who arrived on 17 November for the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting (18–19 Nov) (Xinhua, Russian MFA), and South Korea’s Lee Jae Myung (Xinhua). Bloomberg reported that Putin will meet Xi and Trump on Thursday; Trump lands in China after Manila and plans to meet Mexico’s Claudia Sheinbaum there (White House, via Bloomberg). China said it would take countermeasures if the US imposed new chip curbs (Reuters headline), and Global Times quoted Chinese experts criticising reported EU plans to expand the Foreign Subsidies Regulation. Taiwan’s APEC envoy said he would speak with other members (Reuters).

Why it matters for Europe. The three nuclear powers that matter most for Ukraine and for the global economy meet in Shenzhen, and the EU is not in the room (it is not an APEC member). Decisions on chips, trade and possibly the war may be shaped there. At the same time Beijing is warning Brussels over the FSR. Europe can only bargain on equal terms with both the US and China as one bloc.

What to watch. The US-China-Russia meeting on 19 November; any Ukraine language; Chinese countermeasures on chips.

Sources: Bloomberg, X, X, X, X, X, X, X, X, X

Quad summit in Manila: “grave concerns” about economic coercion and export restrictions

The Japan–Australia–India–US (Quad) Leaders’ Summit was held in Manila on 17 November for about 90 minutes with Sanae Takaichi, Anthony Albanese, Narendra Modi and Donald Trump, and issued a joint press release (Japan PMO).

According to Japan’s summary, the four leaders “expressed strong opposition to any unilateral attempts to change the status quo by force or coercion” in the East and South China Seas, and “grave concerns about economic coercion, including through arbitrary export restrictions”, pledging to secure critical supply chains; they also discussed the Middle East, including Iran. Takaichi and Trump had a brief separate talk at President Marcos’s dinner. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Trump is “serious” about engaging Kim Jong Un but no meeting is scheduled for the China trip (Yonhap, AP). ASEAN’s Secretary-General briefed on the outcomes of the 49th ASEAN Summit (15–17 Nov). In Manila, police blocked protesters marching against the US-led “Pax Silica” tech initiative and the cost of living, which they blamed on the war with Iran (Anadolu).

Why it matters for Europe. Export restrictions and supply-chain security are now the language of Indo-Pacific summits. Europe depends on the same critical inputs but negotiates them country by country.

Sources: Japan PMO, and, X, X, X, X, X

Norway asks for “a clear European mandate” to talk to Russia, as the Commission proposes new Ukraine funding today

The European Commission will propose on Wednesday 18 November a new loan package for Ukraine backed by all member states, “as some capitals continue to balk” at the funding (Bloomberg).

Defence Commissioner Andrius Kubilius said the Commission has disbursed €1.71 billion under the defence component of the Ukraine Support Loan (UASL). Zelensky asked the EU to deliver the “additional 8 billion euros for Ukraine this year” in the Ankara Summit declaration and to focus SAFE on what “has already been tested” in war. Norway’s foreign minister Espen Barth Eide called on Euronews for a clear European mandate for Europe to talk to Russia: “This war has been going on longer than the First World War, and wars end”, and “if anybody is going to talk about European security order, Europe has to be there”. He also said Moscow must not be allowed to dictate Europe’s security arrangements and warned against a return to spheres of influence; Euronews reported that the next phase of the peace talks remains uncertain as Russia plays down face-to-face talks with Europeans. Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis said “the stronger pressure we put on Russia, the faster we can arrive at a real peace process”. Zelensky said there are “21 billion euros in unused SAFE funds” that should go to joint projects with Ukraine (via Clash Report). after the EEA Council in Brussels he spoke of “economic security, competitiveness and strategic autonomy”. Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev wrote that “Europe should focus on funding Ukraine’s reconstruction, not on prolonging the war”. The EU’s Schuman Security and Defence Forum runs on 17–18 November (EEAS).

Why it matters for Europe. CEAFE’s line is that funding more weapons prolongs and escalates the war; the money Europe raises should serve a negotiated, pan-European peace and reconstruction. Barth Eide’s call for a European mandate to talk to Moscow is the kind of diplomatic step CEAFE highlights: a peace among Europeans, Ukraine and Russia, negotiated with Europe present rather than settled over its head.

What to watch. The Commission proposal’s size and conditions; Berlin meeting on 19 November.

Iran-linked assassination plot in North Macedonia and Greece

North Macedonia and Greece said they had uncovered and foiled an Iran-linked assassination plot (Reuters, Haaretz headlines; articles not readable).

CNN, cited by The Times of Israel, reported US intelligence suggesting Iran was working on plans to target senior Trump administration figures with a “dirty bomb” or chemical weapon in Washington; Trump said Washington takes the reports “very seriously” (Anadolu); Iran’s foreign minister Abbas Araghchi and deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi denied them (Anadolu, Al Jazeera), and Rubio later said Iran poses no “imminent threat” (Al Jazeera). Rubio also said there is “zero” evidence that the recent US technology outages (flights, banks, email, the NSA’s classified network) are linked to each other or to a hostile actor (AP, State Dept). Clash Report, citing a report, said Tehran has hardened its negotiating position after the Republican midterm losses.

Why it matters for Europe. The US-Iran war is reaching European soil through covert operations in the Balkans, while the Gulf exchanges (see Early warning) keep energy prices and shipping at risk. Europe pays the costs of a war it did not decide.

Sources: Reuters, X, X, X, X, X, X, X, X

Israel: an Eisenkot ally becomes Knesset speaker as the Gaza ceasefire is called “at risk of collapse”

Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party was elected Speaker of the Knesset with 66 votes, defeating Likud’s Ofir Katz, with support from Arab parties (Clash Report, Al Jazeera, France 24); CNN called it “a critical blow” to Netanyahu’s grip on power.

Netanyahu said “the results of these elections are not what I wanted” and that today’s threats are “no less serious than they were before the 1967 Six-Day War”; Likud called it a “low point” (Netanyahu’s account). The US-backed Board of Peace warned the UN Security Council that the year-old Gaza ceasefire is at risk of collapse without immediate steps (Al Jazeera). After the UK’s sanctions on eight Israeli companies supporting West Bank settlements (20:30 edition), Australia imposed new sanctions on settlement expansion (Haaretz). Opposition leaders warned that Netanyahu could reignite an all-out war with Iran to stay in office (Washington Post).

Why it matters for Europe. A weaker Netanyahu and a fragile Gaza truce change the regional equation. The UK and Australia are acting on settlements while the EU, Israel’s largest trading partner, has still not used its collective weight.

Sources: Clash Report, X, X, X, and, X, X, X
