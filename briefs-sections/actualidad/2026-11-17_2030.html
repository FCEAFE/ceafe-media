Ukraine Defence Contact Group: Europe pays, but part of the money still buys American

The 37th Ukraine Defence Contact Group met at NATO headquarters in Brussels, co-chaired by UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting, with about 50 nations.

The UK announced a £240 million package of more than 1,000 short-range air-defence missiles and launchers, effective against Russia’s jet-powered Shahed drones, plus more than a dozen radars; the missiles come from Thales Belfast and the radars from the UK and Europe, with deliveries from mid-December (UK MoD). Germany’s Boris Pistorius said Germany and Norway will jointly buy another package of US weapons for Ukraine through NATO’s PURL mechanism, focused on air defence, with a German contribution of about €273 million. Ukraine signed a memorandum on joint defence production with Finland and a letter of intent with Sweden and the UK on arming the Gripen jets it has procured (munitions, equipment, spare parts). Minister Khmara told the EU ministers that Ukraine plans to double drone supplies in 2027, when drones will be more than half of its weapons needs, and proposed joint production in Europe.

Why it matters for Europe. Two models were on show on the same day. The British package, the Finnish memorandum and the Swedish Gripen deal keep money, jobs and know-how in European industry. PURL does the opposite: European taxpayers fund US production lines, and Europe stays dependent on American export permission for the air defence that protects Ukraine and, tomorrow, itself. Ukraine’s call for joint drone production in Europe points to the better path.

What to watch. The UK prime minister will set out the rest of the winter support at the Coalition of the Willing meeting on Thursday 19 November (UK MoD).

Poland says Washington gave the “green light” for a US base, and claims a leading role in Europe’s security architecture

Poland’s deputy defence minister Paweł Zalewski said in Brussels: “We have received confirmation that Washington gives the green light for a US military base in Poland.

We are now awaiting the announcement of further details.” Deputy Prime Minister and Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz, after meeting Elbridge Colby, whom he called deputy head of the Pentagon, wrote that “a week after the visit to Washington we returned to talks on a permanent US troop base in Poland. The matter is going in a good direction and is a priority for us.” At the same doorstep he said: “There can be no European security architecture without Poland playing a leading role.” The Polish foreign ministry separately met the US-Poland Business Council on American investment.

Why it matters for Europe. Warsaw wants to lead European security while negotiating its core security bilaterally with Washington. A permanent US base deepens the “special relationship” bet that CEAFE questions: it ties Poland’s defence to decisions taken in Washington. A leading role in European security would mean investing in European capabilities and a common army, not in a separate deal with Washington.

What to watch. Any Pentagon or White House confirmation; cost-sharing terms; how this fits the EU’s CARD findings.

Update: The EU prepares its 22nd sanctions package as Moscow puts “European security architecture” on the table

The European Commission began consulting member states on 17 November on a new sanctions package against Russia, the 22nd since February 2022, expected to target banks, crypto channels and the “shadow fleet”, to be presented before the end of the month and agreed in December (Euronews).

High Representative Kaja Kallas called the Kremlin’s demands to redesign Europe’s security architecture “absurd” (Euronews). The EEAS said new sanctions target international shipping and fleet-management firms helping Russia move oil, and that EU money flowing into Russia’s energy sector is down 85% since 2021. The EU Institute for Security Studies wrote that the Abu Dhabi talks produced no breakthrough and that Russia’s “interest” in a new European security architecture is “an attempt to sow discord”, meant to keep the Americans talking and confront Europeans with the fear that a new order could be negotiated “over their heads”. Zelensky said a prisoner exchange, one of the main agreements reached at the trilateral meeting in Abu Dhabi, is being prepared.

Why it matters for Europe. Sanctions are one of the few tools the EU wields as a bloc, and the last package was watered down by national vetoes (Euronews). Russia’s line on a “new security architecture” is aimed at precisely that weakness. Euronews also notes that Moscow could use the package as a pretext to keep refusing talks with the European delegation, so Europe needs both unity on sanctions and a single negotiating voice.

What to watch. Formal Commission proposal before end-November; whether any member state blocks; Russia’s reaction.

Update: Quad summit in Manila and a US-Thailand trade deal, as Lavrov attacks NATO’s “presence” in Asia

On the sidelines of the ASEAN summits in Manila, Donald Trump held the first leader-level Quad summit in two years with Narendra Modi, Sanae Takaichi and Anthony Albanese; the group launched a new initiative on AI and deals to secure critical-mineral supply chains, plus maritime-security cooperation (The Japan Times, Al Jazeera).

Trump and Thai PM Anutin Charnvirakul signed a joint trade statement: according to Trump, Thailand will eliminate all tariffs on US exports and remove “substantial non-tariff barriers” on US vehicles, medical devices, pharmaceuticals, ethanol and poultry, with the deal to be finalised in the coming weeks (Anadolu, Bloomberg). Asked about a separate meeting with Vladimir Putin, Trump said: “If it’s available, I would do that” (Euronews). At the East Asia Summit, Sergey Lavrov said NATO members are “expanding their presence in the Asia-Pacific, openly seeking to contain China”, and called the coming US-China-Russia trilateral “useful under any circumstances, because these are three nuclear powers”. In Shenzhen, Xi Jinping received the leaders of Chile, Peru, Mexico, Vietnam and Papua New Guinea before APEC; PNG’s prime minister told Xi it “wasn’t easy” to close his country’s Taiwan office (Reuters).

Why it matters for Europe. The Quad is one of the US-led blocs CEAFE tracks: Washington now ties AI and critical minerals to it, two areas where Europe has no seat. The Thai deal shows the US method: bilateral, tariff-free access for American goods in exchange for relief, the same template Washington wants with each EU state. And on 19 November the three nuclear powers will meet in Shenzhen while Europe meets in Berlin: as Lavrov says, they have never met in this format, and Europe is not in the room.

What to watch. APEC and the Shenzhen trilateral on 19 November; India-Japan 2+2 on 18–19 November (Indian MoD).

The UK sanctions Israeli settlement builders; Israel calls it a “hostile act”

The UK sanctioned eight Israeli construction companies involved in building settlements in the occupied West Bank and the activist group Tzav 9, which blocks humanitarian aid into Gaza (Anadolu, Al Jazeera).

Ed Miliband said: “We will not be bystanders in the destruction of the two-state solution.” Israel’s foreign minister Gideon Sa’ar said Israel “views with the utmost gravity” the sanctions, called them “a hostile act targeting private, law-abiding companies” and said Israel “will not yield and will not change its policy under external pressure”. On the same day Benjamin Netanyahu addressed Europe directly: “EUROPE, WAKE UP! … Europe is not a spectator. Europe is the target”, referring to Iran’s missiles and nuclear programme.

Why it matters for Europe. London acted alone; the EU as a bloc still has no agreed measures of this kind. Israel answers European criticism by casting Europe as Iran’s next target, which also feeds Israeli arms sales such as the Arrow 3 that Czechia is studying (07:30 edition). European policy on the Middle East is credible only if it is common.

What to watch. EU reaction; whether other member states follow the UK.
