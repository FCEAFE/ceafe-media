Rutte against a European army: “you cannot also have national militaries”; Merz wants “a stronger, more European NATO”

Speaking in Berlin on the margins of the NATO Parliamentary Assembly, NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said: “If you have a European military, you cannot also have national militaries. The United States has one military… If Europe will be able to pull that off, fine. I don’t think it will happen soon because it has to do with this big question of sovereignty.”

Rutte said he would “counsel against” national armies working together in NATO “and then on top of that having a big European structure”. His reason: “if Putin attacks, we will first need two or three weeks to agree who will do what, and then he will probably end up in Rotterdam before we have decided”. He added that there is “no principal problem against a European military”, but no “big push for it”. After meeting Rutte, Chancellor Friedrich Merz wrote that Germany’s “perhaps most important lesson” from the Ukraine war is that “we are building, at high speed, a stronger, more European NATO”. @F_CEAFE commented that Rutte is “an employee—an important one—of NATO, an organization led by the USA”, and that Washington does not want the EU “to end up having its own army and being able to act independently of the USA”.

CEAFE: When capabilities are compared — economic, technological or military — European unity stops being a slogan and becomes a condition.

Context: Antecedentes: on 15 October 2019 @F_CEAFE wrote that the EU’s defence goals were “impossible to achieve without a European Army (#PESCO) equipped with European systems” (X, 2019). NATO’s military commander (SACEUR) is always a US officer.

Why it matters for Europe: the head of the Alliance is publicly arguing against a European army, the goal CEAFE defends as the route to European sovereignty. Rutte’s “Rotterdam” argument assumes Europe stays a set of national armies coordinated by NATO. “A more European NATO” (Merz) still leaves strategic decisions, the nuclear umbrella and key enablers in Washington. The US has one army because it is one federal state, the integration step Europeans have not taken.

What to watch: reactions from the European Parliament (plenary in Strasbourg this week) and the Commission’s defence portfolio; whether any EU government answers Rutte on the record.

Sources: Rutte (clip 1), Rutte (clip 2), @F_CEAFE, Merz, NATO

Update: Kh-101s in Poland: NATO meets, Merz demands an end to “dangerous provocations”; Finland summons Russia’s ambassador, Moscow protests Estonia’s border closure

NATO allies held an urgent meeting on Monday on a possible response to the Russian missiles that crashed on Polish territory on Friday (Washington Post). The Lublin prosecutor’s office says all three objects were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles.

According to dpa, they were made in the fourth quarter of this year at a plant in Dubna, north of Moscow, and Poland will summon the Russian ambassador. At a joint press conference with Rutte, Merz said Russia “must immediately stop its dangerous provocations. Poland can count on our full solidarity. If necessary, NATO will of course defend every inch of territory.” He called for stronger protection of the EU’s and NATO’s external borders. Rutte said the incident “clearly originated with Russia”. France’s Foreign Ministry also expressed solidarity with Poland. Finland summoned Russian Ambassador Pavel Kuznetsov and called Russia’s “dangerous and irresponsible actions in Europe” unacceptable. It cited a suspected Russian helicopter incursion off Loviisa on Saturday (up to 4.1 km, per the Finnish Defence Ministry). Moscow, for its part, summoned Estonia’s chargé d’affaires, Marek Ühtegi, over Tallinn’s decision to suspend all road crossings (Narva-1, Narva-2, Koidula, Luhamaa, Saatse) from 1 December for six months. The Russian MFA called the move “openly confrontational”, accused Estonia of tacitly letting Ukraine use its airspace for drones (Estonia rejects this) and warned of “the brink of an open war” (TASS, Russian state agency). There is still no Russian statement on the missiles in Poland (tagesschau).

Context: Antecedentes: on 15 November 2022 Poland’s MFA said a Russian-made missile had fallen in Przewodów and summoned the Russian ambassador (X, 2022). Poland, Finland and Estonia are EU member states and NATO members.

Why it matters for Europe: three EU member states are responding nationally on the same day, with summonses and border closures. Solidarity statements are many; a common EU instrument for border protection and air defence on the eastern flank is still missing.

What to watch: the outcome of NATO consultations (any Article 4 request); Poland’s summons of the Russian ambassador; Russian “retaliatory measures” against Estonia; Zakharova’s briefing on Thursday 26 November.

Sources: tagesschau.de, Freie Presse/dpa, Merz, Washington Post, France Diplomacy, Euronews (Finland), Anadolu, Russian MFA, TASS, The Moscow Times, Estonian government, ERR

Update: the peace track: Merz says Europe is ready to negotiate with Russia and calls 2008 a “mistake”

Merz said “Europe is ready to negotiate with Russia” and that talks “are expected to take place again soon at the adviser level, in a format involving Ukraine, Russia, Europe and the United States”, possibly again in Abu Dhabi.

He backed “Article 5-like” guarantees for Ukraine that would come “very close to NATO membership” (Der Spiegel). He also said that, in retrospect, not following the US proposal at the 2008 Bucharest summit to admit Ukraine was “a mistake… this war could probably have been prevented”. Zelensky, by video to the Assembly, again argued that membership is better than guarantees that only resemble Article 5. Rutte replied that there is currently “no unanimity” among allies on Ukraine’s membership and that “only NATO decides who can become a member”. He said the US is making “enormous efforts” to start talks, but “Putin must also play along”. Rutte also claimed Ukraine is “killing or seriously wounding up to 50,000 Russians each month”. Kyiv Independent, citing media analysis of leaked Russian government data, reports a Russian death toll “likely near 700,000”; Bloomberg cites hacked documents showing at least 470,000 deaths (both unverified, different methods). Former Ukrainian PM Arseniy Yatsenyuk called the US-led talks “sham talks” (C-SPAN). The Munich Security Conference published a brief on E5 (Germany, France, Italy, Poland, UK) defence leadership.

Context: Antecedentes: on 13 November Russia’s Deputy Foreign Minister Sergey Ryabkov said that bringing the Europeans into the Abu Dhabi talks “could undo progress” (X, 2026). The Shenzhen trilateral of 19 November excluded Europe.

Why it matters for Europe: Merz places Europe as a party at the table (“Ukraine, Russia, Europe and the United States”) after Shenzhen. Whether “Europe” means the EU or a group of capitals is not said, and the format is still driven by Washington.

What to watch: date and venue of the adviser-level round; who represents Europe (EU, E3/E5 or national advisers); Moscow’s answer.

Sources: Merz (clip 1), Merz (clip 2), Der Spiegel, Rutte, TASS, Kyiv Independent, Bloomberg, C-SPAN (Yatsenyuk), Munich Security Conference

Update: Tajikistan, the fourth country to leave the ICC since the US push; the EU and the Assembly of States Parties object

The UN depositary received Tajikistan’s withdrawal notice on 20 November 2026; it takes effect on 20 November 2027. Reuters says Tajikistan is the fourth country to withdraw since a US diplomatic push against the Court.

An EU statement by High Representative Kaja Kallas says the ICC “does not target sovereign states, nor does it constitute a threat to their sovereignty”. It urges Dushanbe to reverse the decision and recalls the withdrawals initiated this year by Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali, Chad, Venezuela and Nauru. The EEAS had criticised Tajikistan on 22 November for hosting Vladimir Putin on 19–20 November despite the ICC arrest warrant. The Presidency of the Assembly of States Parties said such decisions “risk undermining the collective pursuit of justice”.

Context: Antecedentes: in October 2025 Germany said Hungary still had to honour the ICC warrant for Putin during the twelve-month withdrawal period (X, 2025).

Why it matters for Europe: the ICC is one of the pillars of the rules-based order the EU defends almost alone among the great powers. The US, Russia and China are not members, and the US is pushing others to leave.

What to watch: further withdrawals; the Assembly of States Parties session in December.

Sources: Council of the EU, ICC/ASP, Reuters, 2eu.brussels

London: Burnham rules out rejoining the EU without a referendum

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said it would be “unthinkable” to rejoin the EU without a new referendum.

He is not proposing one now. He said he would consult the public if his government later recommended rejoining, and that closer ties do not mean rejoining (Politico, ITV, Anadolu).

Context: Antecedentes: on 24 October 2026 London Mayor Sadiq Khan called Brexit “catastrophic” and said “it’s time to rejoin the EU” (X, 2026).

Why it matters for Europe: the UK is seeking a closer relationship with the EU without membership.

What to watch: the scope of the UK–EU “reset” agenda.

Sources: Politico Europe, ITV, Anadolu

Geographical indications: Italy and 16 other EU member states ask for a common strategy against US pressure

Italy, backed by 16 other EU member states, is asking the EU for a common strategy to defend European geographical indications (Feta, Asiago, Gorgonzola, Parmigiano, Jerez…) against US legislative texts that would treat them as freely usable “common names” (Bloomberg; Italian Agriculture Ministry).

CEAFE: No member state, on its own, has the scale of the powers it hopes to sit beside at the same table.

Context: Antecedentes: on 14 September 2020, the day the EU and China signed an agreement protecting 100 European geographical indications, @F_CEAFE noted that “some other key allies do not recognize Geographical Indications…guess who” (X, 2020).

Why it matters for Europe: a case of economic coercion on the EU’s trade rules, answered here by a group of member states rather than one capital alone.

What to watch: the Commission’s response; whether the issue enters EU–US trade talks.

Sources: Bloomberg, Masaf, Eunews
