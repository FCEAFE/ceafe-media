## Update: After Shenzhen, Putin tells the Europeans to "come to Moscow"; Merz calls the summit a "Yalta format"

**At his press conference in Shenzhen after the first US–China–Russia leaders' meeting, Vladimir Putin said Russia had proposed, "through diplomatic channels", that European representatives come to Moscow to set out their positions, after which Moscow "will decide in what format to continue the dialogue with Ukraine" (Reuters via Internazionale; AFP via Arab News; Politico).**

He said Washington had asked "quite insistently" for European participation and that "discussing security issues, including security in Europe, without Europeans is pointless", but called any military cooperation between Kyiv and the EU "a problem" and the deployment of any troops to Ukraine "unacceptable" (DW; Anadolu). He added that "without a change of political elites in some European countries, probably nothing can be done to fully normalise relations" (Anadolu, Turkish state agency). @F_CEAFE quoted his line that the EU representatives were invited "to discuss… ensuring security for Ukraine, Russia, and Europe as a whole."

**Context:** The Declaration toward Peace adopted in Shenzhen (14:30 edition, story 1) was negotiated by three leaders with no European present. In Berlin a few hours later, Friedrich Merz called the trilateral a "photo op" and said such a "Yalta format cannot bring peace" to Ukraine; he named Germany, France, the UK, Poland and Italy as the countries that would speak for Europe (DW live). Michel Barnier, reposted by @F_CEAFE: "En Chine aujourd'hui il manque un drapeau, un continent: l'Union européenne." Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen: "I think Putin will be even more emboldened after today's meeting… he will hit us Europeans even harder" (DW, citing Reuters). NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, asked if he was worried about the summit: "No, no, no… it is important that leaders meet… also from nuclear powers" (NATO transcript).

**Why it matters for Europe.** Moscow is now offering to decide whether, and how, Europe sits at the table, and is addressing "the Europeans" rather than the Union and its High Representative. Europe's own answer in Berlin was again a group of five capitals (the E5), not the EU institutions that the Dublin formula of 13 November also named. The invitation tests whether the Union can answer with one voice or will be split into national channels to Moscow, which is the scenario CEAFE warns against: no member state alone has the weight to sit at a table with the US, China and Russia.

**What to watch.** Whether the EU, the E5 or individual capitals reply to Moscow, and who would go; the EU leaders' special summit on 26–27 November; Trump's press conference at the White House on 20 November at 12:00 (Washington time).

## Update: Berlin Coalition of the Willing: Macron reopens Ukraine's NATO path, Kushner reports progress on guarantees

**The Coalition of the Willing meeting hosted by Merz ended with Europe's leaders pressing for a seat in the talks.**

Emmanuel Macron said: "Faced with the maximalist nature of the Russian position, I believe we need to reopen the question of Ukraine's accession to NATO," and called for anti-missile systems and interceptors to be deployed now (DW). US envoy Jared Kushner said Trump joined by phone with an update on Shenzhen, that the talks "made a lot of progress on what those security guarantees can look like" and a post-war prosperity plan, and that Russia was "comfortable" with NATO-like guarantees (DW, citing Reuters). Zelensky: "America fully understands why strong and coordinated European participation at the negotiations is necessary." Ursula von der Leyen said "our new €45 billion loan" supports Ukraine and that the 22nd sanctions package is on the way; the Commission says Ukraine's 2026 needs are covered and the €45 billion would cover two thirds of its 2027 needs (@vonderleyen; @ComisionEuropea). António Costa restated Europe's "principles and priorities for the peace negotiations" (@eucopresident).

**Context:** The meeting opened in the 14:30 edition. On the same day, Russian strikes on Kyiv damaged the embassies of the Netherlands and Austria and drone debris fell on the grounds of Ukraine's Foreign Ministry (@MFA_Ukraine; Germany's Foreign Office expressed solidarity with the Netherlands, Austria, Brazil and South Africa).

**Why it matters for Europe.** Europe is again paying for the war (the €45 billion loan) and planning the guarantees, while the terms are discussed by Washington and Moscow. Spain's foreign ministry summed up the position: "Nada sobre Ucrania se puede decidir sin Ucrania. Nada sobre la seguridad europea se puede decidir sin Europa" (@MAECgob). Macron's call on NATO accession goes the other way from Putin's red line in story 1, so the two statements made on the same day frame the next round.

**What to watch.** A written outcome from Berlin; the 22nd sanctions package; Russia's reply to the guarantees Kushner described.

## Denmark ratifies the Greenland defence deal with the US, 109–0

**The Danish Parliament unanimously approved (109–0) the agreement between Greenland, Denmark and the United States that amends the 1951 defence agreement on Greenland; Greenland's parliament approved it on 3 November, so Copenhagen can now notify Washington and the agreement becomes legally binding (Danish Parliament; AP).**

The deal, signed on 22 September in New York, gives the US underwater access around Greenland and specific permission to build a missile defence system, recognises Greenland as an equal partner and paves the way for more US bases (The Guardian; AP). President Trump could conclude it without Congress (Danish Parliament). Marco Rubio: the ratification "will make us all safer, stronger, and more secure" (@SecRubio). Denmark's foreign minister said the deal "removes all uncertainty" but that trust in the US needs rebuilding (The Guardian).

Background: CEAFE has followed US designs on Greenland since November 2019, when it warned that US interest could mean backing the island's independence from Denmark, and so from the EU. In November 2025 it noted that Denmark's push to defend Greenland was "not #EUDefense", and in January 2026 it criticised Copenhagen for continuing to buy US weapons.

**Why it matters for Europe.** The pact closes a crisis in which a US president threatened to take territory from an EU member state. It was negotiated bilaterally by Denmark and Greenland, not by the Union, and it extends US military infrastructure, including missile defence, on an Arctic territory linked to the EU. It shows how far the US security umbrella in Europe is still negotiated state by state.

**What to watch.** Formal notification to Washington; the location and scope of new US bases and the missile-defence system.

## ICJ rules that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

**The International Court of Justice found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in northern Rakhine State during the 2017 "clearance operations", the first time the Court has held a State responsible for genocide (ICJ press release No.**

2026/30; UN News). It found Myanmar failed to prevent and punish genocide, but did not directly and publicly incite it. It ordered Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those responsible, allow the safe and voluntary return of displaced Rohingya, and compensate them; Myanmar must report within two years, and the amount of compensation will be set later (ICJ). The case (The Gambia v. Myanmar) had 11 intervening States. About 10,000 Rohingya were killed and more than 700,000 fled to Bangladesh (BBC).

**Why it matters for Europe.** Several European states intervened in the case, and the UK and Norway welcomed the ruling (@FCDOGovUK; @NorwayMFA). Critics on X contrasted their position on Myanmar with their stance on Israel (reposted by @F_CEAFE). The judgment is binding but the Court can only refer non-compliance to the UN Security Council (AP).

**What to watch.** Myanmar's response; Security Council follow-up; the Bangladesh refugee camps.

## Reuters: Trump official floated Guyana ceding part of the Essequibo to Venezuela

**Jeremy Lewin, then State Department policy planning chief, told Guyana's foreign secretary Robert Persaud in September that he could help broker territorial talks with acting Venezuelan President Delcy Rodríguez, and, according to two sources, floated Venezuela taking partial control of the Essequibo region, two thirds of Guyana's territory (Reuters via Investing.com).**

Lewin said the talks were "preliminary" and directed by the White House after Rodríguez wrote to Trump in May. The State Department said its position on the boundary "had not changed"; Guyana's Foreign Ministry said it had received that assurance and that the focus remains the pending ICJ ruling (Guyana DPI).

**Why it matters for Europe.** The case comes days after the US and Venezuela restored military ties (07:30 edition, story 4) and shows how quickly a small state's territorial integrity can become negotiable in great-power deals, the risk CEAFE sees for European states acting alone.
