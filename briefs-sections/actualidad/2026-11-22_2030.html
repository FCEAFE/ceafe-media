Update: All three Russian objects that fell in Poland were Kh-101s built this autumn; NATO Council meets on Monday

Polish prosecutors in Lublin said on Sunday that all three objects that crashed in Poland during the Russian attack on Ukraine on Friday 20 November were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, not two missiles and a jet drone as first reported, and that preliminary examination of the debris shows they were made in the fourth quarter of 2026 at plants in Dubna, Moscow region.

They fell in Nowy Lipowiec (Lublin province) and in Rudnik and Mołodycz (Subcarpathia), leaving a crater and damaging a house and a garage, with no injuries; military counter-intelligence (SKW) is taking part in the investigation. The North Atlantic Council will meet in Brussels on Monday 23 November on the violation of Polish airspace (NATO official to Anadolu; dpa). Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz expects “strong support” for the eastern border; Warsaw has asked for more airborne early-warning aircraft and F-35s (Stars and Stripes).

Context: in the Baltic, Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys repeated that his country “has never authorized the use of its airspace for attacks against targets in the Russian Federation, including in the present case”, after the drone alert in Kaliningrad; Estonia’s Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said the strike on the Sparta II is a consequence of Russia’s war in Ukraine (Anadolu). The ship was still burning off Finland on Sunday (Reuters).

Why it matters for Europe: three missiles produced only weeks ago landed on the territory of an EU member state and NATO member, and the response is again channelled through NATO, whose military commander (SACEUR) is a US officer; Poland is asking for early-warning and air-defence assets that Europe largely sources from the US.

What to watch: Monday’s NAC outcome and any Article 4 request; new deployments (AWACS, fighters); a Russian reaction.

Sources: Reuters via The Straits Times, Rzeczpospolita/PAP, Anadolu, dpa, Stars and Stripes, Kęstutis Budrys via mazzenilsson, Anadolu, Tsahkna

Fico replies to Zelensky after the European Council: “Militarily, Ukraine is losing this war”

Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico replied publicly to President Zelensky’s remarks about him after their closed-door exchange at the European Council, writing that Slovakia “is not waging war, either with the Russian Federation or with Ukraine” and that “militarily, Ukraine is losing this war completely”.

In a long statement posted in Polish, Fico says Slovakia called the invasion a violation of international law from the first day but refuses to send weapons, and will not be treated as a subordinate “expected to fulfil every wish of President Zelensky”. He says he raised the Brazilian–Chinese peace plan and the UN “Friends of Peace” group, that Zelensky “categorically rejects any form of ceasefire”, and that his question on transiting non-Russian gas through Ukraine was rejected.

Context: the same day Marine Le Pen said France already gives Ukraine €6 billion a year besides the EU loan. Ukraine’s Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, after a Russian drone destroyed part of a residential building in Dymer (Kyiv region), said “Europe cannot afford to respond to each incident in isolation”; Reuters reports at least four people killed in Russian drone attacks on the Kyiv region.

Why it matters for Europe: the dispute shows how far apart EU leaders are on ceasefire, peace plans and energy transit a week after Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen without any European at the table; the Brazilian–Chinese initiative Fico cites is one the EU has not engaged with as a bloc.

What to watch: Zelensky’s reply; Slovak–Ukrainian gas-transit talks; the European Council follow-up on Ukraine financing.

Sources: Robert Fico, Marine Le Pen, Andrii Sybiha, Reuters, Kyiv Independent

UK Foreign Secretary Miliband “deeply worried” by evidence of war crimes in Gaza

UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the BBC he is “deeply worried” by “increasing evidence of war crimes” in Gaza and supports the International Court of Justice “looking at the issue” of genocide.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot, for his part, recalled the ICJ judgment of 19 November finding that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first such ruling against a state (UN News).

Why it matters for Europe: European positions on Gaza are converging on the role of international courts, an area where European states act through international law rather than military power.

What to watch: any further UK or EU statements; the ICJ case on Gaza.

Sources: BBC Politics, Jean-Noël Barrot, UN News
