## Update: after the budget-summit walkout, Costa prepares deeper cuts and leaders give themselves until 17 December

POLITICO Europe reports that the "Sensible Seven" governments demanding a smaller 2028–2034 budget walked out of António Costa's small-group sessions on Thursday night; Denmark, Sweden, Belgium and Austria are named, and Austria said in a statement that "further sessions would not bring us closer to a solution". According to two EU diplomats quoted by POLITICO, Costa told leaders he will table a new proposal with a further €220–230 billion of cuts to the Commission's draft of about €2 trillion (he had already proposed €204 billion), falling mainly on competitiveness and foreign aid rather than cohesion and farming. Macron called the cuts "a strategic mistake" and said dragging talks into 2027 "is not democratically possible" before France's April election. Italy's Meloni said "I will not sign under these conditions" (Anadolu). Merz said Germany pays "around a quarter of the European budget" but receives "only around 8% … directly in return". On Friday von der Leyen said she was "pleased" at "wide support" for the proposed loan for Ukraine (Reuters). Talks resume at the European Council of 17 December.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** this follows the collapse of the special summit reported in the 08:30 edition. Two days earlier, on 25 November, EU Commissioner Andrius Kubilius warned that the European space industry considered a 30% cut to the MFF defence and space line would make its plans impossible (https://x.com/i/status/2125661996825907429).

**Why it matters for Europe:** the cuts Costa is now pointing to fall on competitiveness, the heading that carries defence-industrial and research money. A Union that trims its common budget while each capital buys alone cannot build the capabilities that no member state can afford by itself.

**What to watch:** Costa's new "negotiating box"; the 17 December European Council; whether Ukraine's 2027 financing is decoupled from the budget.

## Germany and Austria want TikTok's European business under European ownership

Germany and Austria called on Thursday for "a European ownership structure for TikTok's European business and European responsibility for the key technological infrastructure — from the recommendation algorithm to data access", Euronews and AFP report. German culture and media minister Wolfram Weimer said the Chinese-owned platform poses "fundamental security policy risks for Europe"; Austrian digital state secretary Alexander Pröll said TikTok "must not remain in the hands of a geopolitical systemic rival". The two will seek EU-wide support at the Telecommunications Council in Brussels on 2 December. The Commission preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act on 6 November. TikTok said the proposal had "zero merit", citing its €12 billion "Project Clover" data programme.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** ByteDance ceded control of TikTok's US operations in January to a majority American-owned joint venture after a threatened US ban. CEAFE has followed the US pressure on TikTok since July 2020, when Washington weighed a ban and Microsoft held talks to buy the US business (https://x.com/i/status/1290245943498018816).

**Why it matters for Europe:** the aim is control over strategic digital infrastructure, not regulation alone. The same reasoning applies to US platforms and cloud providers that hold European data under US law (CLOUD Act), a test of whether the principle is applied symmetrically.

**What to watch:** the 2 December Telecommunications Council; China's reaction (MOFCOM/MFA); whether other member states join.

## Turkey completes ratification of the Mecca joint-defence pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan

Erdoğan signed Presidential Decision No. 12017 on 26 November, published in the Official Gazette on 27 November, completing Turkey's ratification of the "Mecca Agreement for Joint Defense" with Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, signed on 7 August. Article 2 states that "any external armed attack on any of the Parties will be considered an attack against all"; the text also covers logistic support (arms, ammunition, fuel), joint exercises and deployments, defence co-production and intelligence sharing. The Turkish parliament approved it by 279 votes to 119 (AP).

**Background (CEAFE archive):** in May 2026 CEAFE shared the SCMP's analysis of whether the Saudi–Pakistani defence pact would give Chinese arms risk-free exposure (https://x.com/i/status/2051735435005542791). The 08:30 edition covered Turkey's extension of its Iranian gas contract to 2032.

**Why it matters for Europe:** a NATO member and EU candidate is building a collective-defence commitment outside NATO, in the Gulf, during the war with Iran. Middle powers act on their own interests; Europe, which has more weight together, still struggles to agree on a common budget.

**What to watch:** deposit of the last ratification and entry into force; trilateral talks in Islamabad, with Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia on the agenda (Türkiye Today).

## Spain votes on Sunday: Sánchez frames the snap election as a choice on sovereignty and foreign policy

Spain holds a snap general election on Sunday 29 November. In the final debate, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez asked whether Spaniards want "an open, sovereign, peaceful Spain, or an impoverished, closed Spain subjected to the interests of oligarchs on the other side of the Atlantic", recalling that his government "said no to the war in Iran" (clip via Clash Report). PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo, the poll frontrunner, clashed with rivals over whether to call Israel's war in Gaza a genocide; Reuters describes Vox's Santiago Abascal as "tipped to be kingmaker". Euronews quotes a political scientist saying "it's not unthinkable that the socialists win".

**Background:** Spain has just secured the ECB presidency for Pablo Hernández de Cos (26 November, 20:30 edition) and is in the camp seeking a larger EU budget.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Spain is the EU's fourth-largest economy. The result will shape Madrid's line on the EU budget, defence spending and relations with Washington before the 17 December European Council.

**What to watch:** turnout and seat count on Sunday night; whether a PP–Vox deal is needed.

## Israel: Eisenkot reaches 36 of the 61 seats he needs to replace Netanyahu

Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) struck coalition deals with Avigdor Liberman's Yisrael Beytenu and Yair Golan's Democrats, bringing his bid to 36 of the 61 Knesset seats needed ahead of his 6 December deadline, Clash Report and France 24 report. Naftali Bennett is still pushing for a unity government. Haaretz reports that UAL leader Mansour Abbas is set to declare Hamas a terror group amid talks with Eisenkot.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** on 7 November President Isaac Herzog said he would task Eisenkot with forming a government (Haaretz, https://x.com/i/status/2119163536496554083), after Arab-majority parties, the Joint List and Ra'am, backed him (Al Jazeera, https://x.com/i/status/2118838426287886790).

**Why it matters for Europe:** a change of government in Israel would reset EU–Israel relations: the Association Agreement review, Gaza reconstruction and sanctions on violent settlers.

**What to watch:** the 6 December deadline; whether Arab parties' support becomes part of the arithmetic.
