## EU defence ministers approve the 2026 defence review: "cooperate earlier", from the planning stage

**EU defence ministers met in Brussels on 17 November at the Foreign Affairs Council (Defence) and the European Defence Agency (EDA) Steering Board.**

According to the EDA, ministers approved the 2026 Coordinated Annual Review on Defence (CARD), whose core message is to cooperate earlier, from the capability planning stage, and endorsed eight collaborative opportunities "from cruise missiles to a secure defence cloud". They also endorsed a joint statement on security of supply, to keep defence supply chains secure and resilient, and approved the Agency's 2027 budget of €78 million (+8.7%). At the same Council, Germany's defence minister Boris Pistorius announced $300 million for another PURL air-defence package for Ukraine, and Spain's minister Margarita Robles called for an EU Special Representative for Women, Peace and Security. The Council also highlighted Ukraine's battlefield expertise in defence technology as a potential boost to Europe's capabilities.

If Europe wants to sit as an equal, it has to pool what is today fragmented.

**Why it matters for Europe.** The CARD diagnosis repeats what CEAFE has long argued: fragmented national planning is the root of Europe's capability gaps. At the same time, the German contribution goes through PURL, the NATO mechanism that pays for US-made weapons for Ukraine, so European money keeps feeding the US industrial base. An EDA budget of €78 million is tiny next to national defence budgets.

**What to watch.** Whether the eight CARD opportunities turn into funded joint programmes, and how much of Europe's Ukraine aid keeps flowing to US suppliers rather than European ones.

## Update: East Asia Summit in Manila; Trump meets the Quad and ASEAN adopts a critical-minerals statement

**The 21st East Asia Summit took place in Manila on 17 November, as President Trump arrived for the ASEAN meetings and met the Quad leaders (US, Japan, India, Australia).**

ASEAN published a leaders' statement on critical minerals, which stresses the region's "significant critical mineral endowment" and its growing role as a producer, refiner and consumer of minerals. Russia's foreign minister Sergey Lavrov also attended the East Asia Summit and gave a press conference afterwards. Japan's foreign minister Motegi briefed on the Japan–India "2+2" meeting and the Quad leaders' meeting. The 07:30 edition covered the ASEAN–UN summit and Trump's arrival.

**Why it matters for Europe.** Southeast Asia's minerals are a key prize in the US-led critical-minerals coalitions, and the Quad is one of the US-led blocs that group allies under Washington's leadership. Europe is not at the table in Manila. Its own access to these minerals depends on bilateral deals such as France–Malaysia rather than on a single EU voice.

**What to watch.** The text of the Quad leaders' statement, and whether ASEAN's minerals statement favours any one bloc or keeps the region open to all partners.

## Xi receives Vietnam's To Lam and Mexico's Sheinbaum in Shenzhen before APEC

**China's leader Xi Jinping held a summit with Vietnam's party general secretary and president To Lam in Shenzhen on 17 November, ahead of APEC Economic Leaders' Week.**

According to Bloomberg, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum also reached out to Xi during a meeting in Shenzhen, seeking to overcome a recent trade spat. These meetings follow the Carney–Xi meeting covered in the 07:30 edition: Canada, Mexico and Vietnam, all deeply tied to the US economy, are each negotiating their own terms with Beijing.

**Why it matters for Europe.** While US neighbours and partners rebalance their ties with China, the EU is absent from APEC and has no comparable channel at this summit. CEAFE's view is that Europeans should cooperate with all global actors on their own terms, and do it as a bloc.

**What to watch.** Any tariff or investment announcements from the Sheinbaum–Xi meeting, and the APEC leaders' meeting later this week.

## UK sanctions those blocking Gaza aid and companies backing West Bank settlement expansion

**The UK announced on 17 November sanctions on those blocking aid from entering Gaza and on companies supporting illegal settlement expansion in the West Bank, the Foreign Office reported, sharing a post by minister Ed Miliband.**

"We will not be bystanders in the destruction of the two-state solution," the post says. Separately, Al Jazeera reported that Israeli settlers opened fire in the village of al-Duyuk near Jericho, killing a woman.

**Why it matters for Europe.** London acts on its own, outside the EU, while the Twenty-Seven remain divided on measures against settlement expansion. Each European capital acting alone carries less weight than a common EU position would.

**What to watch.** The names on the UK list, and whether EU member states follow with national or EU-level measures.
