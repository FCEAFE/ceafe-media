Update: EU budget summit collapses as seven “frugal” countries boycott Costa’s talks; leaders now aim for a deal “this year”

The special European Council of 26 November ended without a deal on the 2028–2034 budget. According to Rapporteur, UNN and POLITICO Europe, seven countries (Denmark, Germany, the Netherlands, Austria, Finland, Sweden and Belgium) refused to take part in Costa’s small-group format, and leaders dropped the planned second day. Costa’s compromise of €1.78 trillion in current prices (a 10% cut on the Commission draft) was attacked from both sides, Euronews reported. Costa said the meeting was “a necessary step”, adding: “Everybody knows that we cannot fail Europe, and we need to have a new budget from the 1st of January 2028.” Merz said positions are “still far apart” but he is confident of a deal “this year – for a strong economy and a secure Europe”; von der Leyen asked leaders to “maintain our ambition” on size, new own resources and timing. Russian state agency TASS reported that the €45 billion extra aid for Kyiv also stalled (this is TASS’s claim; EU sources say Ukraine’s 2027 financing was discussed).

CEAFE angle (owner’s reposts): @F_CEAFE reposted Greek Prime Minister Mitsotakis: “If we continue to insist on further cuts to the EU budget, we can throw the Draghi report and its lofty ambitions into the dustbin of European history.” He also quoted the FT on the Franco-German clash over “Made in Europe” in the EU defence budget, and reposted the FT report that the UK will pay an extra “participation fee” as the price of its EU reset deal.

Why it matters for Europe: the budget is where common defence money (European preference, EDIP, a successor to SAFE) is decided. A Union that cannot agree on 1.1% of GNI cannot build the domestic capabilities that no member state can afford alone. The “frugals” who walk out are also among those who buy US systems.

What to watch: next attempt at the December European Council; whether “Made in Europe” survives in the defence chapter; Ukraine’s 2027 financing.

EU prepares new loans and grants to replace US war-fighting capabilities, while Washington delays its Europe force-posture review

Bloomberg reported on 27 November that the EU is preparing “tens of billions of euros in new loans and grants to replace US war-fighting capabilities as the continent pushes to cut its reliance on Washington”, citing people familiar with the matter. It would follow the €150 billion SAFE loan scheme (a second SAFE call of €21.3 billion opened on 29 October, prioritising air and missile defence, drones, counter-drone systems, ammunition and missiles, according to the Commission). At the same time, RFE/RL reported that the US deputy ambassador to NATO told allies that Washington will publish its Europe force-posture review after the provisional 1 December deadline, missing next week’s NATO foreign ministers’ meeting; the Pentagon is preparing five options and is still assessing European offers to backfill US forces and equipment. Trump said allied pledges to raise defence spending toward 5% and to buy “750 F-35” jets “made me feel a little bit differently” about cutting the roughly 80,000 US troops in Europe.

Why it matters for Europe: this is the core of CEAFE’s line. Replacing US enablers with European ones builds sovereignty, jobs and R&D. Paying for US withdrawal by buying 750 F-35s does the opposite: it locks in dependence (software, spare parts, export permission). Same day, @F_CEAFE reposted a report that Berlin ruled out Palantir’s Maven for the Bundeswehr over “kill switch” fears (reported by @piotrmiecz; not yet confirmed by an official German source).

What to watch: the size and rules of the new instrument (European preference or not); the posture review options; NATO foreign ministers next week.

Trump to troops: Iran’s missiles “can reach Europe”, NATO is “in various parts of Iran” and “we got Greenland”

In Thanksgiving calls to US troops in the Gulf and remarks at Mar-a-Lago on 26 November, Trump said Iran has missiles capable of reaching Europe and that without peace in the Middle East there could be a “nuclear holocaust” (Al Jazeera, Dawn, Times of India). He said “we have victory at hand” in the Iran war, “whenever I want it to be”, that the US is giving Tehran “every chance to make a deal”, and that if strikes resume “we will definitely hit Pickaxe Mountain”. He downplayed Russian and Chinese intelligence help to Iran: “involvement to me would mean shooting, not giving them little information… We do the same thing.” He also said NATO is “involved with us… in various parts of Iran”, that “we got Greenland”, that “we don’t need Middle East oil” and the US is there “protecting Israel”, and that Putin may attend the G20 at Doral. Kyiv Independent noted that Trump added Ukraine to his warning against expanding the war, unlike the published joint statement with Rutte. Merz welcomed Trump’s call on Putin to end hostilities.

Why it matters for Europe: Europe is cited as a target in a war in which Europeans are not party to the decisions, while NATO is described as already involved. “We got Greenland” concerns the territory of an EU member state’s realm and was not explained.

What to watch: Danish and Greenlandic reaction to “we got Greenland”; NATO’s comment on its role in Iran; any resumption of strikes.

Estonia closes its land border with Russia on 1 December; Moscow says it will hurt “Estonia itself”

Estonia will close all five road crossings with Russia (Narva-1, Narva-2, Koidula, Luhamaa, Saatse) at 07:00 on 1 December for an initial six months, after a series of Russian attacks including one on the defence industry, according to ERR and the Estonian government; rail freight through Narva continues. Russian MFA spokeswoman Zakharova replied that Moscow is “monitoring the tense situation on our western border, caused by the openly hostile actions of the Baltic states” and that the closure “will deal a painful blow – primarily to Estonia itself”. She also attacked Kallas for calling for Russian databases on participants in the war to be obtained, and welcomed Tajikistan’s withdrawal from the Rome Statute of the ICC.

Background (CEAFE archive): @F_CEAFE has followed the Estonian–Russian border for years: in November 2023 it reposted reports that Norway and Estonia might close crossings with Russia (https://x.com/i/status/1727643175668387930); in May 2024, that Russia had removed the buoys marking the border on the Narva river (https://x.com/i/status/1793708062655779082); and in October 2026, that Estonia was moving troops permanently closer to the border under an “active defence” strategy (https://x.com/i/status/2107026546913689796).

Why it matters for Europe: the EU’s external border is being hardened state by state. A common EU decision would carry more weight than national measures.

What to watch: Russian countermeasures; Finnish and Latvian follow-up; families cut off on both sides (Al Jazeera).

Switzerland votes on Sunday on loosening its arms-export rules

On 29 November Swiss voters decide on the amendment to the War Material Act passed by Parliament on 19 December 2025. It would let the Federal Council allow exports to certain partner states even when they are in an armed conflict (Bloomberg: 36 mostly Western countries), allow exceptions to the export criteria and reduce re-export declarations. Opponents say it would hurt neutrality (SECO, Bloomberg).

Why it matters for Europe: Swiss re-export bans blocked ammunition for Ukraine and pushed buyers away from Swiss suppliers. A “yes” would make Swiss industry a more reliable European supplier.

What to watch: the result on Sunday evening.
