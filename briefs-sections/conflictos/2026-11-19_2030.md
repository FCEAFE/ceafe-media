## Putin vows “much more painful” retaliation for the Crimean Bridge strike, rules out an energy ceasefire

**At his press conference closing the China visit, Putin said Russia's response to Ukraine's Neptune strike on the Crimean (Kerch) Bridge “will be much more painful than what Kyiv did to the Crimean Bridge”; the Kremlin's own channel quotes him: “The Crimean Bridge will be rebuilt…**

It all comes down to retaliation. And there will be retaliation.” He said Russia will protect drivers on the land route to Crimea (the “Novorossiya Highway”) while the bridge is repaired. Putin also said he had discussed a possible ceasefire in several areas with the Security Council, but after the bridge strike Russia will not negotiate any form of energy ceasefire (@ClashReport, @FaytuksNetwork). He added that Russia has invited EU representatives to Moscow to discuss security for Ukraine, Russia and Europe. A Western satellite image circulating on X shows the damaged road arch of the bridge (unverified).

**Why it matters for Europe.** An announced reprisal raises the risk of a heavy Russian strike on Ukraine's energy grid as winter starts, precisely while the Coalition of the Willing meets (topic 2); the Moscow invitation is an attempt to split the EU from Kyiv.

**What to watch.** Size and targets of the next Russian combined strike; repair timeline; EU reply to the Moscow invitation.

## Russian drone damages the Dutch Embassy in Kyiv during hours-long daytime attack

**A Russian drone struck or crashed in the courtyard of the Dutch Embassy in Kyiv on 19 November, damaging cars, a security post and the building's windows; it is unclear whether the area was targeted or the drone was shot down (Kyiv Independent, OSINTtechnical).**

Kyiv authorities report one man, 26, killed and four injured in the hours-long daytime drone attack, and a three-storey building hit in broad daylight. Russia's MoD (state source) claims daytime UAV strikes on port infrastructure and vessels “used in the interests of the AFU”.

**Why it matters for Europe.** An EU member's embassy was hit days after Moscow's diplomatic attack on the Netherlands over Dutch firm Destinus and the Crimean Bridge strike (07:30 edition); watch for a Dutch and EU response.

**What to watch.** Dutch government reaction; summoning of the Russian ambassador.

## Update: Houthi strike on Riyadh's PIF Tower confirmed; Saudi Arabia “will not tolerate” attacks; GCC and Kuwait condemn

**Saudi Civil Defence said a Houthi attack damaged several floors of a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District and nearby buildings, with no injuries; the 80-storey Public Investment Fund Tower houses the $900 billion sovereign wealth fund (@ClashReport).**

Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry said it “will not tolerate” attacks on its territory. The GCC called it a dangerous escalation; Kuwait condemned Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia with ballistic and cruise missiles and drones. New alerts were sounded over Riyadh in the afternoon (Early warning).

**Why it matters for Europe.** Houthi strikes on Gulf financial centres widen the Iran war's economic front and raise risks for Red Sea and Gulf shipping and energy supplies to Europe.

**What to watch.** Saudi-led coalition retaliation in Yemen; further launches at Riyadh, Jizan and Abha.

## Update: Iran — IRGC says blockade must end before any nuclear talks; oil-export breakout signals; Bloomberg: US strikes more likely

**IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said Iran will not allow the US-imposed blockade to continue and will not discuss its nuclear programme until the Strait of Hormuz issue is resolved (Al Jazeera); IRNA (state agency) quotes the IRGC claiming a significant upgrade of air defences, and Iranian accounts relay a claim that missile range has risen to 4,500 km (unverified).**

The IRGC-affiliated Sabereen News says Iran is considering resuming oil exports despite the blockade, after TankerTrackers said Iran appears to be preparing to ship oil again. Bloomberg reports Western officials see an increased likelihood of renewed large-scale US strikes on Iran; Tasnim relays Trump telling Israel's Channel 12 there is “no rush” and “we'll see what happens after the new year begins”. At the IAEA Board, Iran's envoy Reza Najafi warned that information gathered at Iranian sites “will certainly be used for further attacks” and Iran rejected “unrestricted inspections under threat”.

**Why it matters for Europe.** A blockade-running attempt could trigger the next US–Iran clash at Hormuz; the E3 are directly involved at the IAEA Board.

**What to watch.** Movement of Iranian tankers through Hormuz; IAEA Board resolution; US force posture.
