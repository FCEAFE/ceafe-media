Update: Zelensky claims the sinking of the Russian corvette Merkury off Libya; Albania denies any role

President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Monday that Ukraine carried out Sunday’s attack in Benghazi, calling it “an exemplary Ukrainian operation in response to Russia’s war” by the SBU and the Defence Forces.

Zelensky released footage of the Project 20380 corvette Merkury being hit and said the dry-cargo ship Angara, which Kyiv says carries weapons, was also struck; “We continue our operations in designated waters” (Zelensky; AP; Defense News). Libyan eastern-based military officials had earlier said six drones approached, four were intercepted and two hit the ship, slightly injuring ten Russian and Libyan personnel (Anadolu). Pro-Russian channels say seven sea drones were used and a Ka-27 helicopter destroyed (not verified). Albania’s Foreign Ministry “categorically” rejected reports that the drones were launched from its territory. Speaking to the NATO Parliamentary Assembly, Zelensky said Russia feels Ukraine’s response “from western Siberia… to the coast of Libya, where its warships are sinking”. The Kremlin’s Monday remarks did not mention Libya (TASS).

Context: the 08:30 edition reported the sinking, a second drone-boat attack and the UN call for an independent investigation. Albania is an EU candidate country and a NATO member.

Why it matters for Europe: the Ukraine war at sea now reaches the central Mediterranean, near EU shipping lanes and Libya’s fragile balance between Russia and Türkiye.

What to watch: an official Russian response; the identity of the ship hit off Benghazi; reactions from Malta, Italy and the EU; the Libyan complaint at the UN Security Council.

Sources: Zelensky, Anadolu, Defense News, AP, Albanian MFA, Anadolu (Albania), Zelensky at NATO PA, TASS

Baltic: the Kremlin accuses Ukraine of “terrorism” after the drone attack on the Sparta II; GPS jamming reaches Stockholm

The Kremlin accused Ukraine of terrorist acts in the Baltic Sea after a drone attack on the Russian cargo ship Sparta II near Finland, and urged the Baltic states to use their influence over Kyiv to stop them (Reuters; TASS).

Over the weekend the Baltic satellite-navigation jamming area expanded north-west: 40–50% of flights at Stockholm Arlanda were affected over two days, against about 1% before, according to flight-tracking data. In Finland, allied fighters, including French Rafales, are training dispersed road-base operations in Exercise Protective Fence 26.

Context: Finland, Sweden and the three Baltic states are EU member states and NATO members. Bulgarian Deputy Prime Minister Atanas Pekanov said on Monday that “escalation in the Black Sea is completely unacceptable” after attacks on cargo ships off Romania and Bulgaria (Euronews).

Why it matters for Europe: a Ukraine–Russia naval confrontation in the Baltic runs through EU and NATO waters, and the jamming already affects civil aviation in an EU capital.

What to watch: details of the Sparta II attack; Swedish and Finnish statements on the jamming.

Sources: Reuters, TASS, Flightradar24 data, Protective Fence 26, Armée de l’Air, Euronews

Update: Iran war: Tehran’s parliament says it is ready to leave the NPT; Hormuz oil exports down about 40% since early November

The Iranian parliament’s National Security and Foreign Policy Commission said the Majles is ready to pass a bill to withdraw from the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT) as soon as the Islamic Republic takes that decision (TASS).

IRGC deputy commander-in-chief Maj. Gen. Mostafa Izadi said Iran would respond to any threat “without geographical limits” (Al Jazeera; IRNA). State broadcaster IRIB quoted Iran’s legal-affairs chief as saying several US-linked ships “have just been seized” to enforce $290 billion in domestic court damages (state media claim, not verified). An economist’s count says at least seven tankers were struck in the past week and Hormuz oil exports are down about 40% since early November. Iran denied that an IRGC-linked group carried out a cyberattack on French supermarket refrigeration systems. In Israel, Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich said Israel has “two years” to “eliminate the threat of the Iranian regime once and for all” (Clash Report clip; Times of Israel).

Context: Trump told Fox News on 22 November he is “inclined” to resume strikes on Iran after the new year if there is no deal (08:30 edition).

Why it matters for Europe: an Iranian NPT withdrawal would hit the non-proliferation regime the EU helped build with the JCPOA; Europe pays the energy and security costs without a say in the decision, and the French cyber accusation brings the war into an EU member state.

What to watch: an actual bill in the Majles; the IAEA; US and Israeli statements on resuming strikes.

Sources: TASS, IRNA, Al Jazeera, IRIB, Hormuz count, Iran on French cyberattack, Smotrich (Clash Report), Times of Israel

Update: Ukraine war: the Novoshakhtinsk refinery halts operations after a six-hour attack

The governor of Russia’s Rostov region says the Novoshakhtinsk refinery suspended operations after a combined drone and missile attack lasting more than six hours; thick smoke was still rising on Monday morning.

Citing Reuters, visegrad24 says operations at 15 of Russia’s 35 largest refineries had been disrupted or halted by mid-November and daily refining fell by roughly 40–45%. Russian attacks killed four people, including a 15-year-old boy, and injured 19 across Ukraine over the past day (Kyiv Independent).

Context: the 08:30 edition reported the overnight strikes on the Novoshakhtinsk and Perm refineries.

Why it matters for Europe: the refinery campaign strains Russia’s war economy, while diesel supply in Europe remains tight (Reuters).

What to watch: repair time at Novoshakhtinsk; Russian fuel export restrictions.

Sources: Rostov governor, smoke footage, visegrad24 (Reuters data), Kyiv Independent, Reuters
