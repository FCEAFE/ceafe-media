## Update: Gulf of Finland and Kaliningrad: the drone strike on the *Sparta II* and the alert in the exclave bring the war to the doorstep of EU member states

**Two separate episodes on Saturday 21 November have brought the Ukraine war to the waters and borders of EU member states.**

In the morning, between 07:40 and 07:50 Madrid time, at least two drones hit the Russian cargo ship *Sparta II* in international waters of the eastern Gulf of Finland, about 50 km west of the Russian island of Gogland (Suursaari), according to the Finnish Defence Forces. The 121-metre ship had left St Petersburg on Friday evening bound for Baltiysk, in Kaliningrad. It belongs to Oboronlogistika, a logistics company set up within Russia's defence ministry and under US sanctions since 2022; its cargo on this voyage is unknown. Estonia's Police and Border Guard Board says the explosions began while the ship was in an international lane inside Estonia's exclusive economic zone, outside its territorial sea. Burning and without steering, the ship drifted into the territorial waters of Finland, an EU member state and NATO member, and anchored south of Loviisa. Its 16 crew were evacuated to a Russian ship; no injuries or pollution have been reported. On Sunday the Finnish Border Guard, which leads the rescue with Estonian and Russian vessels, said there were no more visible flames but the cargo was still hot, the ship had a slight list and sinking could not be ruled out; the plan is for the Russian rescue ships *Spasatel Karev* and *Murman* to tow it into Russian waters (Finnish Border Guard, STT). In the evening, Kaliningrad Oblast went on drone alert: sirens sounded and Khrabrovo airport was briefly restricted. Governor Alexei Besprozvannykh said a "suspicious object" had been detected in the Neman district "from the direction of Lithuania" and, after checks, that there was "no threat" (Kommersant).

**Confirmed, claimed and unknown.** Finland's Defence Forces say Ukraine is "likely" behind the ship strike, without conclusive evidence; a Border Guard officer told AFP a Ukrainian drone hit the ship; Kyiv has not claimed it. Ukrainian monitoring channels reported explosions near the Baltic Fleet base at Baltiysk, but no official source confirms a strike, damage or casualties in Kaliningrad, and a widely shared "explosion" photo was in fact a fire on Iran's Qeshm Island. Lithuania's Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys said his country "categorically rejects" claims of involvement and "has never authorized the use of its airspace for attacks against targets in the Russian Federation, including in the present case"; Lithuania's crisis centre said the air force detected no drones. No Finnish, Estonian, Lithuanian, Latvian, Polish or NATO source confirms that any drone overflew an EU member state; the route of the drones is unknown. No statement from the Russian defence ministry or the Kremlin had been found by Sunday midday.

**Context:** Prime Minister Petteri Orpo wrote that "war is spilling over into our immediate vicinity" and that "Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor". Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen told Yle the Gulf strikes are "a step in a more serious direction", that Finland had no prior warning and that Helsinki contacted Kyiv to stress the risks of the area. Finland is also investigating a suspected incursion of up to 4.1 km into its airspace by a Russian helicopter off Loviisa, and Swedish fighters were scrambled in support of Finland. Estonia's navy commander, Commodore Ivo Värk, said further attacks "cannot be ruled out" and that everything outside coastal states' territorial waters is "an entirely legitimate place for both warring parties to conduct their military operations". On 11 November a Ukrainian-claimed strike had already hit the Russian corvette *Naro-Fominsk* near Gogland, then inside Russian territorial waters. Russia has repeatedly linked Kaliningrad to its nuclear arsenal: in late September 2026 its embassies warned that any attempt to isolate the exclave could bring the use of its full arsenal, including nuclear weapons, and on 2 October President Putin said a direct attack on the Russian Federation, "in this case, we are referring to Kaliningrad", would put the deployment of "all the weapons at our country's disposal" on the agenda.

**Why it matters for Europe:** CEAFE's assessment is that these incidents differ from strikes between Russia and Ukraine on the front or in the Black Sea: they immediately involve the waters, airspace, rescue services and political responsibility of EU member states and NATO members such as Finland, Estonia and Lithuania. Opening another front around Kaliningrad, an exclave bordered by Lithuania and Poland, is very negative and dangerous for the EU. So far the response has come capital by capital — Helsinki, Tallinn, Vilnius and Stockholm — rather than as a common European position, although the risk is shared.

**What to watch:** whether the ship is towed out of Finnish waters or sinks; any Ukrainian claim or Russian defence ministry statement; the outcome of Finland's helicopter investigation; Monday's North Atlantic Council meeting on the Russian objects in Polish airspace.

## Iran strikes Bahrain: the IRGC claims hits on US Patriot radars, Manama says a civilian site was hit

**Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on Saturday evening that it fired missiles and drones at US Patriot radars at two bases used by US forces in Bahrain, Sheikh Isa and Juffair, in response to US drone "violations" of Iranian airspace.**

Bahrain's Interior Ministry said a fire broke out at a civilian industrial facility near Manama after what it called "Iranian aggression"; Bahraini and Pakistani citizens were injured, one of them critically (Anadolu, Al Jazeera). Bahrain condemned the "unlawful" attacks and urged the UN Security Council to take "firm measures to compel Iran" to implement Resolution 2817. Saudi Arabia condemned the "heinous Iranian attacks" "in the strongest terms", the UAE spoke of a "flagrant violation" of sovereignty and Jordan of a "dangerous escalation".

**Context:** on Sunday the IRGC claimed it had shot down a 20th US MQ-1 drone east of the Strait of Hormuz (IRGC statement via IRNA, Iranian state media; no US confirmation). CENTCOM again denied Iran's earlier F-16 claim. The US naval blockade of Iran reached 130 consecutive days on Saturday (Euronews). Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said the US–Israeli war on Iran has created an "economic burden" and that a ceasefire is needed.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Bahrain hosts the US Fifth Fleet and is a naval hub for European navies in the Gulf. Each strike on a Gulf state raises the risk for European personnel and shipping and feeds the energy prices Europe pays, while the responses come from Riyadh, Abu Dhabi, Amman and the UN Security Council, where the EU as such has no seat.

**What to watch:** a US response; a Security Council meeting; a joint Gulf Cooperation Council statement.

## Ukraine war: Russian drones destroy a building in the Kyiv region; a Ukrainian drone hits a fuel truck on Crimea's only land route

**A Russian drone strike brought down part of a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, in the Kyiv region, overnight; up to 14 people were feared trapped, and by Sunday morning one body had been recovered and three people were injured, according to President Zelensky.**

Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched 186 attack drones overnight and that 161 were shot down; 30 people were injured over the day (Kyiv Independent, Ukrainian Air Force). Zelensky called US delays in delivering Patriot missiles already paid for by allies "difficult to understand", after Russia fired 165 missiles in a week.

**Context:** on the Ukrainian side, a mid-range strike drone hit a Russian fuel tanker truck on the R-280 highway, the only overland route to Crimea since the road span of the Kerch (Crimean) Bridge was destroyed in mid-November (@OSINTtechnical). Ukrainian drones reportedly hit a Rosgvardia facility in Krasnodar (unverified). The UK will produce long-range "middle strike" drones for Ukraine in Britain.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the air war is being fought with weapons whose supply depends on decisions in Washington (Patriot) and in European capitals, while each strike on Crimea's supply routes widens the war's reach.

**What to watch:** the final toll in Dymer; any US decision on Patriot deliveries; strikes on the R-280 corridor.

## Yemen–Saudi war: Houthi forces within 80 km of Aden

**Houthi forces have advanced to within 80 km north and 110 km west of Aden, the seat of Yemen's Saudi-backed government, Reuters reports.**

Government forces are reinforcing checkpoints, but rifts between Saudi-backed and southern separatist factions hamper a coordinated defence. The Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed a Houthi drone heading for Riyadh. Taiz remains under siege, and the UN says food and medicine supplies there have fallen by 90%.

**Context:** the US Senate could vote as early as the week of 30 November on blocking the US–Saudi nuclear cooperation pact; Houthi attacks have raised concerns over reactor security.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the fall of Aden would give the Houthis control of both sides of the southern approach to the Red Sea, where the EU's naval operations Aspides and Atalanta protect shipping to and from Europe.

**What to watch:** the fighting on Aden's approaches; further Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites.
