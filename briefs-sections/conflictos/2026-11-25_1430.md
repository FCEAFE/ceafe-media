## Update: Yemen–Saudi war — Houthi drone strike hits Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery

After the Saudi-led strikes on Hodeidah reported in the 08:30 edition, a drone launched from Yemen struck the Ras Tanura oil refinery and port terminal in the Eastern Province on the morning of 25 November, sending a large black plume over the site (video: @MenchOsint, https://x.com/i/status/2125534944340451328). The Saudi spokesman said only that "debris fell on the intended target"; the Houthis say they targeted the facility and the KAFD business district in Riyadh (https://x.com/i/status/2125513000966603127). Pro-Iranian accounts claim Ras Tanura supplies about 40% of Saudi domestic fuel (https://x.com/i/status/2125537495446155593; unverified). The strike came despite layered Patriot and fighter defences reportedly involving several partner air forces (https://x.com/i/status/2125525551871885756, OSINT commentary). Al Jazeera reports oil prices fell more than 1% on the day despite more tanker incidents (https://x.com/i/status/2125550387247616442).

- **Why it matters for Europe:** Ras Tanura is one of the world's largest oil export hubs; a second energy front on top of Hormuz exposes Europe's dependence on Gulf supply routes and the limits of air defence against cheap long-range drones, a lesson for Europe's own critical infrastructure.
- **Key facts:** strike morning of 25 Nov (plume visible 09:15 UTC); distance from Yemen about 1,300 km (OSINT estimate).
- **Follow-up:** Saudi damage assessment and Aramco statement; Saudi retaliation in Yemen; Houthi claims against KAFD.

## Ukraine claims hits on Russian MiG-31s and Su-34s at Savasleyka; Russian glide bombs on Odesa region

Ukraine's General Staff claims hits on three MiG-31 and two Su-34 aircraft at Savasleyka air base in Nizhny Novgorod region (https://x.com/i/status/2125556277027967393; graphic: https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HX9-I-4boAAmnWB.png). OSINT watchers note the airfield has been under cloud on public satellite imagery for a month, so the presence of the aircraft could not be confirmed (https://x.com/i/status/2125557947271098797). Overnight, Russian Su-34s launched UMPK-guided glide bombs at the Odesa region from about 55 km, including on a substation in Artsyz (reposted by @GeromanAT from @MilitarySummary and @AMK_Mapping_, https://x.com/i/status/2125495282297115108, https://x.com/i/status/2125485424244101461). Russia claims the capture of Barvinkivka north-east of Orikhiv while DeepState confirms Russian advances elsewhere on the front (https://x.com/i/status/2125491182235013398), and Ukraine has extended the mandatory evacuation of families with children to all of Donetsk oblast (https://x.com/i/status/2125499823600087422).

- **Why it matters for Europe:** Savasleyka hosts MiG-31s that carry Kinzhal missiles, the type used against Ukrainian cities and close to NATO borders; glide-bomb strikes on the Odesa coast threaten Ukraine's Black Sea export route to Europe.
- **Follow-up:** satellite confirmation of the Savasleyka damage; Russian strikes on Ukraine's grid.

## Update: Ukraine's war at sea off Libya — Benghazi imagery and Italy's objection

Satellite imagery of the port of Benghazi from 24 November shows the Mercury corvette almost completely submerged and the ro-ro vessel Angara damaged at the stern (ItaMilRadar, https://x.com/i/status/2125514213313442070). Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said Ukraine should not attack Russian vessels off North Africa and opposed the spread of the war (via @MilitarySummary, https://x.com/i/status/2125501920521441737; original statement not yet checked). The BBC's Frank Gardner described the night attack as co-ordinated by Ukraine's SBU (repost, https://x.com/i/status/2125487842201624893).

- **Why it matters for Europe:** the war has reached the central Mediterranean, next to Italian and Maltese waters, and EU members are split on how to respond.
- **Follow-up:** Russian naval escort of the Sicily Channel convoy; Libyan (Haftar) reaction.

## Italy tests Europe's sea-based ballistic missile defence and claims NATO spending target

The Italian Navy says the frigate Giovanni delle Bande Nere intercepted a high-speed ballistic target with two Aster 30 missiles; La Repubblica called it Europe's only operational sea-based ballistic missile shield (@ClashReport, https://x.com/i/status/2125558870240079943). Separately, Italian officials say Rome reached defence and domestic-security spending of 2.8% of GDP in 2026, although the figure cannot be found in the defence budget itself (Defense News, https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/11/25/buried-in-italys-budget-officials-see-rome-meeting-nato-spending-target/).

- **Why it matters for Europe:** Aster is a European missile (MBDA/Eurosam), so a successful naval ballistic intercept shows a home-grown alternative to US systems; the budget accounting shows how hard it is for one country to fund the effort alone.
- **Follow-up:** official Italian Navy release and test details; NATO spending figures.

## Iran war: tighter Hormuz surveillance and crypto 'insurance' tolls

Iran's army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia said the armed forces will surveil the Strait of Hormuz more strictly and warned that vessels attempting "unauthorised" transit face heightened risks (Al Jazeera, https://x.com/i/status/2125534866859278437). AGBI reports that ship owners pay Iran an "insurance" toll in cryptocurrency to cross the strait (https://www.agbi.com/analysis/shipping/2026/11/inside-irans-hormuz-toll-ships-pay-with-crypto-for-insurance/, via https://x.com/i/status/2125522144754855966). Al Jazeera also asks whether a new war with Iran could derail Israeli opposition leader Eisenkot's coalition talks (https://x.com/i/status/2125548360912257426).

- **Why it matters for Europe:** a tolled strait run by Tehran raises shipping and energy costs for Europe and shows how sanctions can be bypassed with crypto payments.
- **Follow-up:** US response to the toll scheme; UKMTO advisories.

- **Why it matters for Europe:** a European engine maker growing its capacity in the US rather than at home is another sign of the pull of the US market; China offers partners like Pakistan the human spaceflight cooperation Europe dropped when ESA left Tiangong plans.
