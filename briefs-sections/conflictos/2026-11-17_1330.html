Update: Zelensky hails an “unprecedented operation” against the Crimean Bridge; OSINT mappers say the Sea of Azov is cut off

President Zelensky thanked Ukraine’s Security Service (SBU), the Defence Forces and Ukraine’s weapons manufacturers on 17 November for “an unprecedented operation in response to” Russian strikes on Ukraine’s infrastructure, a reference to the overnight attack on the Crimean (Kerch) Bridge covered in the 07:30 edition.

The OSINT account AMK Mapping wrote that, besides destroying the roadway section and stopping vehicle traffic, debris from the collapsed span has blocked ships from passing through the channel below, “effectively cutting off the Sea of Azov” and its ports. CEAFE has not been able to verify that claim independently. Russia struck Kyiv’s North and South bridges late on Monday, as reported in the 07:30 edition.

Why it matters for Europe. If confirmed, the closure of the Kerch Strait would hit Russia’s logistics to Crimea and the Azov ports, while peace talks continue and Moscow has still not accepted the European negotiating team.

What to watch. Official Russian statements or satellite imagery on the state of the channel, and any Russian retaliation against Ukrainian infrastructure.

Update: Iran’s IRGC claims a drone strike on a US radar in Bahrain, as Qatar says mediation continues

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) said it carried out a drone attack on the morning of 17 November against a US radar system at Isa Air Base in Bahrain, Al Jazeera reported, and Reuters reported that Iran had attacked a US military radar in Bahrain with drones.

Qatar’s foreign ministry spokesman Majed al-Ansari said mediation to end what Al Jazeera calls the “US-Israel war on Iran” is still under way, and that a meeting with Iranian officials would take place within hours. The 07:30 edition covered the tanker attacks in Hormuz and the arrival of a third US carrier.

Why it matters for Europe. A strike on a US installation in a Gulf state widens the war’s geography to the bases that also protect European shipping and energy supplies. Europe is a bystander in the mediation, which runs through Qatar.

What to watch. Any US response, damage reports from Bahrain and the outcome of the Qatari meeting with Iranian officials.

Regulators from 38 countries warn of a growing risk of a nuclear accident in Ukraine this winter

Nuclear safety regulators representing 38 countries said on 17 November that the risk of a nuclear accident in Ukraine is increasing this winter, and urged Russia to stop its attacks on the country’s power infrastructure, Reuters reported.

Nuclear plants depend on stable grid power for cooling and safety systems, which is why regulators link the risk to strikes on the power network.

Why it matters for Europe. A nuclear accident in Ukraine would directly affect EU territory. It is a reminder that Ukraine’s security is European security.

What to watch. Statements by the IAEA and any new strikes on Ukraine’s substations and power plants.

Komyshuvakha: search ends with 72 dead, one of the deadliest strikes of the war

Ukraine’s Foreign Ministry said on 17 November that search and rescue operations in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, are complete.

It said the Russian strike on the settlement on 2 November was one of the deadliest attacks of the full-scale war, killing 72 people, among them rescuers, police officers and school and administrative staff.

Why it matters for Europe. The toll comes as Europeans try to secure a role in the peace talks; any settlement will have to address accountability for strikes on civilians.

What to watch. International reactions and any investigation by the International Criminal Court or the UN.
