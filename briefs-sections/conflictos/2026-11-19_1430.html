Update: Crimean Bridge rail span hit by Neptune missiles, Ukraine confirms; train traffic halted

Ukraine’s Defence Ministry and General Staff confirmed that Navy Neptune coastal missile systems struck the railway section of the Crimean (Kerch) Bridge during the night of 19 November, with hits near the arch and on the tracks; all train traffic has been halted while damage is assessed.

Kyiv Independent notes this comes less than two days after the previous strike. Ukraine’s MoD framed it with the line “This train terminates here” / “Please vacate Crimea”.

Why it matters for Europe. The bridge is the main logistics link to Crimea; repeated strikes, together with Ukraine’s deep strikes into Russia, are the backdrop to the US–Europe rift in Berlin (topic 3).

What to watch. Russian MoD response and repair timeline; satellite imagery of the span.

Sources: General Staff, General Staff, General Staff

Russia’s overnight attack: 98 drones and Onyx missiles; daytime drone raid on Kyiv

Russian attacks killed 4 and injured 36 across Ukraine over the past day, including two children, per Kyiv Independent; Russian forces launched Onyx anti-ship missiles and 98 drones (Shahed-type, Gerbera and Parodiya decoys) overnight.

Kyiv had a sixth air-raid alert of the day during a daytime drone attack, with roof fires in the Podilskyi and Desnianskyi districts; a strike on a residential building in Izium injured 12. Russia’s MoD (state source) claims strikes on Ukrainian power and port infrastructure and on ships supporting Ukraine’s forces; the Kursk governor says a Ukrainian attack injured 11 in Kursk (TASS, state agency, claim).

Why it matters for Europe. The air-defence gap is the first of Zelensky’s four priorities in Berlin today.

What to watch. Whether the large bomber raid forecast for 18–19 November materialises tonight.

Sources: X, X, t.me, t.me

Update: Hormuz tanker war escalates — two more supertankers hit; Iran sets IAEA conditions

After the Army Navy’s claim of three tankers hit by mines, the IRGC Navy says two more “violating” supertankers exploded near the southern coast of the Strait, alleging they were escorted by US fighters and surveillance drones while using a “prohibited route” (IRNA/IRGC claims).

A drone strike on a tanker off Shinas (Oman) is also reported but unconfirmed. Tehran urged regional states to deny the US their territory for attacks, and IRNA reports that Iran, China and Russia set conditions for resuming IAEA verification (an end to hostile acts, among others).

Why it matters for Europe. Energy supply and shipping insurance; Trump said today the US and China share an interest in keeping energy flowing through Hormuz.

What to watch. UKMTO advisories (page unreadable without JS); ship names and flags; IAEA Board of Governors.

Sources: X, X, t.me, X

Space as a battlefield: MBDA and Atmos study orbital deep-strike; Italian space sigint by 2028

Defense News reports that MBDA and German start-up Atmos Space Cargo are teaming up to study deploying deep-strike weapons from Earth orbit using re-entry vehicles.

Aviation Week reports that Italian sigint specialist VirtuaLabs aims to have the first of a space-based signals-intelligence constellation operational in 2028. In the US, Ursa Major and Avio USA won a joint Raytheon contract for a new Tomahawk solid-rocket booster, and Lockheed Martin tested the warhead of its low-cost PAC-3 ACE interceptor against Group 3 drones.

Why it matters for Europe. A European industrial path to space-based strike and space ISR (positive intra-European capability), while Italian firms also feed US munitions chains (Avio USA).

What to watch. Whether the MBDA study is tied to the European Long-Range Strike Approach (ELSA); counter-space implications.

Sources: defensenews.com, aviationweek.com, X, X

Update: Houthi strike damages Riyadh’s tallest tower; Saudis warn against filming impacts

The WSJ reports a projectile struck the tallest tower in Riyadh on Thursday, damaging multiple floors of a building that houses the sovereign-wealth fund (via @MenchOsint).

New early-warning alerts were issued for Abha after launches from Yemen, and the Saudi government released an ad warning citizens not to film impacts or air-defence activity.

Why it matters for Europe. The Yemen front is now hitting Gulf financial centres; Red Sea and Gulf shipping risk.

What to watch. Coalition retaliation in Saada; WSJ original (not checked this run).

Sources: X, X, X

Update: Israeli tanks east of Nuseirat in central Gaza

Israeli tanks moved east of the Nuseirat refugee camp, with witnesses reporting heavy shelling, gunfire and airstrikes, as the Israeli military said it had launched an operation in a Hamas-controlled area; strikes killed at least three people (France 24).

What to watch. Whether the central Gaza offensive, coordinated with the US, widens further.
