## Update: Iran war — Hormuz attack count revised; Katz says a strike decision rests with Trump, as Netanyahu presses for action

The Hormuz picture worsened in the afternoon. UKMTO has reclassified Sunday's (22 November) warning: three vessels sailing eastbound through the strait were struck at the same time, around 20:50 UTC (21:50 Madrid), and are now counted as three separate incidents (@sentdefender, https://x.com/i/status/2125276448261042466). They are reported to be the Sinokor-owned tankers *Cameroon Prosperity*, *Bahrain Prosperity* and *Romania Prosperity* (https://x.com/i/status/2125287958940946927). Iran says four more tankers exploded after hitting mines east of the strait (Iranian claim; identities not released), and Tehran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority added 26 tugs and offshore support vessels to its "non-compliance list" for servicing US-backed shipping (@visegrad24, https://x.com/i/status/2125259883482357917).

On the political side, Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons, that the IDF and US CENTCOM are preparing for possible strikes and that the decision rests with President Trump; he also claimed Iranian cells inside the US are targeting Trump and his family. Israel's public broadcaster Kan reported that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pressing Trump to strike "as soon as possible" (The Independent), while Ynet reports a split: IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir sees no immediate threat requiring a strike and Mossad director Roman Gofman favours acting sooner; Haaretz's editorial says Netanyahu "keeps finding pretexts for his political survival". Clash Report says Trump administration officials are debating whether to assassinate senior Iranian leaders (unverified; no primary source given). Qatar urged Washington and Tehran to show restraint (Al Jazeera, Euronews). Iran says it has filed lawsuits in US and European courts over alleged war crimes, including a missile strike on a school that killed at least 178 people (Iranian figure via Al Jazeera), and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi accused the US of "crippling" the OPCW. A Knesset committee approved about NIS 17.3 billion in extra 2026 defence spending (Katz).

- **Why it matters for Europe:** a resumed US–Israeli air campaign would hit European energy prices and shipping again, while European courts may now be drawn into the war's legal fallout. The EU has no seat in these decisions.
- **What to watch:** whether Trump authorises strikes; identities of the mined tankers; insurers' response to Iran's expanded list.

## Update: Yemen–Saudi war — PIF tower in Riyadh hit; Jizan damage confirmed by satellite

A projectile from Yemen hit a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, identified as the Public Investment Fund (PIF) tower; Saudi state media published a photo of the damage and Saudi Civil Defence reported 11 injured (via Anadolu). @MenchOsint notes that the Saudi-led coalition spokesman admitted a direct hit, without the usual wording about falling debris, that witnesses heard a loud boom without any civil-defence alert, and that it is the third time a tower in the district has been struck.

Sentinel-2 imagery confirms impacts at the Shuqaiq power station and at Jizan port, where a large warehouse was hit (claims first reported in the 14:30 edition). Clash Report says Riyadh has asked the US, the UK, France and others for more interceptors; France 24 reports that Houthi fighters are closing in on Aden. Pro-Houthi accounts report Saudi airstrikes in Sana'a (unverified). Rashad al-Alimi, head of Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council, said government forces can defeat the Houthis and reunify the country. From the CEAFE archive: as early as February 2022, the UAE, then reeling from Houthi attacks, was seeking advanced Israeli radar (Breaking Defense).

- **Why it matters for Europe:** strikes on Saudi energy and financial sites add risk for Red Sea shipping and oil supply on top of the Hormuz crisis, and Gulf partners are now turning to European suppliers, among others, for air defence.
- **What to watch:** Saudi retaliation in Sana'a; whether Riyadh's air-defence warnings failed; replies to the interceptor requests.

## Ukraine's war at sea: Putin vows retaliation for attacks on Russian ships

Vladimir Putin said Russia will retaliate for Ukrainian attacks on its vessels and accused Kyiv of continuing to strike Russian refineries despite a direct request from President Trump (Al Jazeera). Zelensky defended Saturday's drone strike on the Russian cargo ship *Sparta II* in the Gulf of Finland: "We did everything right", he told US media and Bloomberg.

On Libya, Kyiv insists the attack on Russian ships at Benghazi was self-defence (Reuters). Al Jazeera notes that Ukraine is taking its fight against Russia beyond Europe, from Libya to Sudan and Mali.

- **Why it matters for Europe:** a strike in the Gulf of Finland and a vow of retaliation bring the maritime war into NATO's northern waters, between EU member states Finland and Estonia.
- **What to watch:** the form of any Russian retaliation; Baltic shipping security.

## North-eastern and Black Sea flank: Finland summons Russia's ambassador; Romanian disclosure on Neptun Deep

Finland, an EU member state and NATO member, summoned Russia's ambassador over Moscow's recent actions in Europe, including a violation of Finnish airspace by a Russian military helicopter on Saturday 21 November (@FaytuksNetwork). Former Romanian defence minister Radu Miruță said Russia declared a no-fly zone over the Neptun Deep gas project in Romania's exclusive economic zone on 20 August and threatened to strike Romanian jets; Romania sent an F-16 anyway, which destroyed a naval drone, and Russia did not respond.

In the Mediterranean, Italy's offshore patrol vessel (PPA) *Giovanni delle Bande Nere* intercepted its first ballistic target with Aster 30 missiles (Army Recognition). ItaMilRadar tracked an Italian Air Force P-72A on a SIGINT mission over Bosnia and Herzegovina for EU Operation Althea and a Portuguese P-3 patrolling the central Mediterranean.

- **Why it matters for Europe:** Russian pressure is growing around EU energy infrastructure in the Black Sea, and the Aster interception shows European-made ballistic-missile defence at sea.
- **What to watch:** Bucharest's official confirmation of the August episode; Moscow's reply to Helsinki.

## Update: counter-space — Russian nuclear satellite could launch "within 60 days", German report says

German broadcasters WDR and NDR and the Süddeutsche Zeitung report an early-November intelligence assessment that Russia could launch "within 60 days", by the end of the year, a nuclear-armed satellite dubbed "Sputnik-S", said to have been stored near Moscow for years (Defense News). Asked in Berlin, NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said the Alliance will defend "every inch of NATO territory", including "the space element".

Placing nuclear weapons in orbit is banned by the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, to which Russia is a party; a detonation in orbit could disable many satellites and damage power grids through its electromagnetic pulse.

- **Why it matters for Europe:** European military and civil satellites would be exposed; the report ties in with the 2024 US warnings on a Russian nuclear anti-satellite programme.
- **What to watch:** official German and US comment; launches from Plesetsk and Baikonur.

**Sources:** [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/11/24/russia-may-launch-nuke-laden-satellite-by-years-end-german-report-warns/), [X](https://x.com/i/status/2125211904536723929)

## Indo-Pacific: PLA Navy group transits the Osumi Strait; Taiwan's submarine on its 26th sea trial

Japan's Joint Staff Office reports that four PLA Navy ships, the Type 055 destroyer *Lhasa* (102), the Type 052D *Xining* (117), the Type 054A *Yancheng* (546) and a Type 903A replenishment ship, transited the Osumi Strait westbound into the East China Sea on 24 November.

Taiwan's first domestically built submarine, *Hai Kun* (711), left Kaohsiung for its 26th sea trial, including submerged tests.

- **Why it matters for Europe:** routine PLA transits near Japan and Taiwan's push for its own submarines keep the Taiwan Strait, a key route for European trade, at the centre of regional tension.
- **What to watch:** the PLA group's onward route; results of *Hai Kun*'s submerged trials.

## US: Golden Dome and the "Trump battleship" in the funding stopgap

The White House is seeking funding exemptions for the Golden Dome missile-defence programme and the planned battleship in the next stopgap budget (Breaking Defense).

US Representative Thomas Massie separately criticised talk of a "$350 billion emergency military supplemental" for Gaza and Iran.

- **Why it matters for Europe:** Washington protects its own flagship defence programmes even under stopgap budgets, while Europeans still debate how to fund theirs.
- **What to watch:** whether Congress grants the exemptions in the next continuing resolution.
