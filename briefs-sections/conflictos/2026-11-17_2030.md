## Update: Kerch bridge road span collapses; Zelensky says it ended a seven-phase operation

**New since the 07:30 edition: images at dawn showed a whole section of the road part of the Crimean (Kerch) Bridge had collapsed.**

The Kyiv Independent reports it was hit by a Ukrainian Flamingo cruise missile overnight on 17 November, the final part of what President Zelensky called a seven-phase operation over “several weeks” using Flamingo and Neptune missiles and Ukrainian long-range drones. OSINT mapping estimates the fallen roadway at about 270 m. Russia's Transport Ministry suspended road and rail traffic; by midday rail traffic had resumed on one track and a passenger-only ferry was running. Russia answered with a large overnight missile and drone attack: Russia's MoD claims it hit drone plants in Kyiv (Techno-Links, Ukrplastic), the Yuzhny and Severny road bridges in Kyiv and the Motor Sich and Ivchenko-Progress engine plants in Zaporizhzhia (Russian state source). OSINT accounts counted about 30 missiles and about 300 Geran-2 drones, many aimed at western Ukraine. Nuclear safety regulators from 38 countries warned the risk of a nuclear accident in Ukraine is rising this winter because of attacks on the power grid (Reuters).

**Why it matters for Europe.** Ukraine destroyed Russia's most protected target with Ukrainian-made missiles, without needing anyone's permission to use them. ItaMilRadar notes the cut also blocks Russian naval movement between the Caspian and the Black Sea. Europe should keep funding Ukrainian production of such weapons.

**What to watch.** Russian repair timeline for the road span; whether Kyiv's bridges reopen (partial traffic resumed on the Southern Bridge); 7:30 edition story linked: “Update: Kerch bridge hit again; Salavat petrochemical fire”.

## France's secret “Volia” military mission in Ukraine; Moscow calls French troops “legitimate targets”

**Radio France Internationale reported that French troops have been working in Ukraine in non-combat roles since at least summer 2026, maintaining French equipment (Caesar howitzers, Mirage 2000 jets, precision missiles) and training Ukrainian soldiers.**

The mission, called Volia, is led by a French general and deployed under the cover of the Gerfaut training mission in Poland; France Télévisions says several dozen soldiers are involved and that parliament has not been informed (Defense News, citing RFI). Le Monde was told by a source that small French contingents rotate in Ukraine for training and repairs. Russia's Foreign Ministry said it was aware of French troops in Ukraine and called it “direct participation” in the war (Euronews). The Russian Embassy in France said France had “de facto become embroiled” in the conflict and that foreign contingents would be “legitimate targets” (Russian MFA Telegram, state source). Separately, troops from an 11-nation force led by the UK and France tested drone defences in Poland to prepare to support Ukraine after a ceasefire (Reuters).

**Why it matters for Europe.** The French mission shows how European weapons need European people on the ground to keep them working. It also tests whether Europe will stand together behind a member state threatened by Moscow. A European framework would make such missions less exposed than one country acting alone.

**What to watch.** Paris's official response; French parliamentary questions; any Russian strike on sites linked to foreign personnel.

## Russian drones over Moldova and Hungary's rail halt: the war spills onto Europe's borders

**Moldova closed its airspace on 17 November after its Defence Minister said 7 drones entered between 15:45 and 18:10 local time; police received calls about explosions in several districts and bomb-disposal teams were sent out.**

A Gerbera-type drone was filmed over Chișinău. Earlier, Hungary suspended rail traffic with Ukraine for 17–18 November after a Russian drone struck near Mukachevo station, about 300 m from a train with a Hungarian crew. In the Black Sea, an OSINT tally counts six Russian attacks on ships in Romanian and Bulgarian waters since 13 November, as NATO ISR flights (a US P-8A and a French ALSR Vador out of Constanța) patrol the area.

**Why it matters for Europe.** Russian drones are now hitting or crossing the borders of an EU candidate (Moldova) and touching EU members' transport (Hungary) and waters (Romania, Bulgaria). No single small state can defend its skies alone; a common European air-defence and drone-wall effort is the answer.

**What to watch.** Moldovan statement on the drones' origin and debris; Romanian and NATO response at sea.

## Update: Yemen — Houthis close in on Aden and claim drone strikes on Saudi pipeline

**France 24 reports, citing military and government sources, that Houthi forces are getting closer to Aden, seat of the internationally recognised government, which has stepped up security in the port city.**

Saudi aircraft carried out four strikes on northern Sanaa (@ILRedAlert via @GeromanAT). Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed drone attacks on Pumping Stations No. 8 and No. 9 of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline west of Riyadh (via Tasnim, Iranian state agency); Saudi Arabia has not confirmed. **Why it matters for Europe.** A Houthi push on Aden and attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure threaten the Bab al-Mandab and Red Sea route to Suez, which is vital for European trade, while EU naval mission Aspides is small.

**What to watch.** Front line north of Aden; Saudi Aramco statement on the pipeline.
