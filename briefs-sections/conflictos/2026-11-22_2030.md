## Russian corvette *Merkury* struck in Benghazi: the Ukraine war reaches the Mediterranean

**A Russian Navy corvette moored at the Benghazi naval base in eastern Libya was hit by an explosion before dawn on Sunday and caught fire; naval analyst H I Sutton provisionally identified it as the *Merkury*, a Project 20380 (Steregushchiy-class) corvette commissioned in 2023, and assessed it as "almost certainly destroyed".**

The eastern Libyan government of Osama Hammad, backed by Khalifa Haftar, said the explosion occurred aboard a Russian ship that was "on its way back to Russia" and had stopped to refuel, that it was most likely an attack "using unmanned vehicles", and ordered a joint inquiry with Russia. Two Libyan military officials told AP that, according to an initial investigation, several Ukrainian sea drones were launched from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya. Satellite images published by the Telegram channel Strategic Aviation and reported by Militarnyi show fire across the stern and part of the midsection; by the afternoon OSINT accounts said the ship had sunk after at least two naval drones hit it. In the evening, unmanned surface vessels were filmed striking at least one Russian military-linked cargo ship off Benghazi while helicopters of Haftar's Libyan National Army fired at them; Al Jazeera and Anadolu, citing Libyan media, report an attack on a Maltese-flagged commercial vessel (not independently confirmed). A Russian Emergencies Ministry Il-76 flew from Moscow to Benghazi after the blast (Defence Blog).

**Confirmed, claimed and unknown.** Kyiv has made no official claim; Axios's Barak Ravid, citing a source, attributes the attack to Ukraine's SBU. No Russian defence ministry statement had been found by 19:00 Madrid. If confirmed, it would be the first attack on a Russian Navy ship in the Mediterranean (ItaMilRadar).

**Context:** the *Merkury* had escorted a convoy of Russian government-linked cargo ships; Haftar's Libya is Russia's logistics hub towards the Sahel. The attack comes a day after the drone strike on the *Sparta II* in the Gulf of Finland and the drone alert in Kaliningrad (14:30 edition).

**Why it matters for Europe:** CEAFE's assessment is that the war now has a maritime front in the central Mediterranean, a few hundred kilometres from Malta, Sicily and Crete, coasts of EU member states. A Maltese-flagged ship being targeted would put an EU member state's flag directly in the line of fire. No EU statement had been found by Sunday evening, although the EU naval operation IRINI patrols these waters.

**What to watch:** a Russian MoD statement and the Libyan–Russian inquiry; any Ukrainian claim; confirmation and flag of the second vessel; reactions from Malta, Italy, Greece and the EU.

## Update: Iran war: Trump "inclined" to resume strikes after the new year as attacks on Hormuz shipping hit a record

**President Trump told Fox News on Sunday that he is "inclined" to resume strikes on Iran after the new year if there is no deal, called the Iranian negotiators "professional tap dancers", and said of Iranian tankers testing the US blockade: "I hope they do, they'll all be at the bottom of the sea like the rest of the navy."**

At sea, Iran's navy said two supertankers struck naval mines after entering a "restricted area" east of the Strait of Hormuz (IRNA, Iranian state media; not independently confirmed). The Joint Maritime Information Centre (JMIC), in an advisory distributed by UKMTO, says seven tankers have been hit in the strait since 16 November and that no cargo vessel above 1,000 dwt transited between 19 and 21 November. UKMTO also reported an incident involving a tanker and "military forces" in the Gulf of Oman, near the US blockade line. A CNN analysis of UKMTO data finds that reported attacks on shipping exceeded 30 in October and at least 22 so far in November, against an average of about 10 a month earlier in the war.

**Context:** the US Embassy in Bahrain urged Americans to "heightened vigilance". War-risk insurance is quoted at 3.5–7.5% of hull value, against 0.10–0.25% before the war (Al Jazeera). The 14:30 edition covered Iran's strikes on Bahrain and the "line-breaker" tanker spoof.

**Why it matters for Europe:** the timetable for a new US campaign is being set in a television interview in Washington, while Europe, which depends on Gulf oil and LNG and pays the insurance and price premium, has no seat in the indirect talks.

**What to watch:** any deadline for talks; Iranian attempts to run the blockade; new JMIC/UKMTO advisories; Monday's oil-price reaction.

## Yemen–Saudi war: the Houthis say they struck the Rabigh refinery on the Red Sea

**Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed "two precision military operations" against Saudi Arabia's Rabigh oil refinery on the Red Sea coast and a radar at Wadi al-Dawasir.**

The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted Houthi drones aimed at southern Saudi Arabia and the Riyadh region. Saudi-backed government forces claim to have killed or wounded at least 86 Houthi commanders and fighters, while the Houthis published footage of weapons depots they say they seized at Mokha. The internationally recognised government called the Houthi threat "a direct threat to international peace and security". All figures are claims by the parties.

**Why it matters for Europe:** Saudi Red Sea energy infrastructure and the Bab el-Mandeb route, vital for Suez traffic to European ports, are again under attack.

**What to watch:** damage assessments at Rabigh; the front near Aden; any Red Sea shipping advisories.

## Nuclear deterrence: French Rafale land on a Finnish highway; IISS says Sarmat may be entering service

**French Rafale fighters of the 30th wing carried out their first touch-and-go on a Finnish highway during the exercise *Protective Fence 26*, alongside Finnish F/A-18s and Dutch F-35As, days after dual-capable Rafale B trained in the High North with Sweden (*Excalibur*).**

Finland and the Netherlands have agreed to contribute to French forward deterrence, although the Finnish exercise had no nuclear dimension (Etienne Marcuz). Separately, the International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS) says satellite imagery suggests Russia's RS-28 Sarmat intercontinental ballistic missile may have begun to enter service with the Strategic Rocket Forces.

**Why it matters for Europe:** French extended deterrence for the Nordic EU member states is a European option alongside the US umbrella; Sarmat's entry into service would add to the Russian arsenal that many European leaders treat as unusable, while Moscow has warned that a war with Europe would be quick.

**What to watch:** further French deployments in the Nordics; confirmation of Sarmat's operational status.

**Sources:** [Etienne Marcuz](https://x.com/i/status/2124560329937932312), [IISS](https://x.com/i/status/2124488323129282801)
