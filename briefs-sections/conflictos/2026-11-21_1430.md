## Drones hit a Kaliningrad-bound Russian cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland

**Russia says two drones struck a cargo ship sailing from St Petersburg to the Kaliningrad exclave in neutral waters of the Gulf of Finland, about 3 nautical miles from Russian waters, on the morning of 21 November.**

Leningrad region governor Alexander Drozdenko said the crew was being evacuated and the damage assessed. Posts citing Reuters and the Estonian broadcaster ERR name the vessel as *Sparta II*, a US-sanctioned ship linked to military cargo, and say it caught fire and drifted into Finnish waters. The Finnish Defence Forces had already restricted an area off Kotka because of possible drone-interception operations. Overnight, dozens of Ukrainian drones were reported heading for the St Petersburg area and the Ust-Luga oil export terminal.

**Why it matters for Europe.** This is a strike on Russia's sea link to Kaliningrad, inside the Baltic, at the edge of Finnish and Estonian waters. It tests how the EU's Baltic states would handle incidents, debris or drifting vessels in their own waters, and shows again that Europe's security depends on a war it does not control.

**What to watch.** Finnish government and Border Guard statements; any Ukrainian claim; whether Russia escorts Kaliningrad shipping; reaction from Estonia and NATO.

## Update: Two more tankers struck in Hormuz as Iran claims new US drone shoot-downs

**UKMTO reported two tankers struck in the Strait of Hormuz early on 21 November, and footage showed a tanker burning near the Omani coast.**

Iran's IRGC Navy said the US military, with tanker aircraft, F-16, F/A-18 and F-35 fighters, P-8 patrol aircraft, helicopters and MQ-series drones, tried to escort oil tankers through the Strait; the IRGC claimed it shot down a US MQ-9 and later an MQ-1 over Qeshm, putting its claimed totals at 58 MQ-9 and 19 MQ-1. These are Iranian claims, unconfirmed by US sources read.

**Why it matters for Europe.** Energy prices and shipping insurance for Europe depend on Hormuz; European navies and shipowners are exposed to a conflict whose escalation is decided in Washington and Tehran.

**What to watch.** UKMTO advisories; CENTCOM statement on the claimed drone losses; ship identities.

## Update: Houthis hit Saudi power plant and target Riyadh; coalition strikes Yemen

**The Houthis struck Saudi Arabia's Shuqaiq power complex in Jizan on 20 November; video shows smoke from two locations and a fire at the plant.**

Late that night the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh after repeated alerts. Reuters reports coalition strikes on a Yemeni port while the Houthis warned airlines away from the kingdom, and that divided government forces in Aden are preparing for a Houthi assault. Houthi-run al-Masirah TV claimed at least 41 people were killed in airstrikes attributed to Saudi Arabia in 24 hours (Houthi claim, relayed by Chinese state broadcaster CGTN).

**Why it matters for Europe.** A Saudi-Houthi war threatens Red Sea and Gulf energy routes on which European trade depends, alongside Hormuz.

**What to watch.** Damage at Shuqaiq; airline suspensions; front lines around Aden.

## Drone alert at NAS Sigonella, a day after drones closed Genoa airport

**A suspected drone intrusion triggered an alert at Naval Air Station Sigonella in Sicily on the evening of 20 November, with precautionary instructions to US personnel.**

ItaMilRadar notes an exceptionally busy week at the base: Black Sea surveillance missions, a Russian naval flotilla transiting south of Sicily and the deployment of two British P-8 Poseidons. The incident came about 24 hours after drone activity suspended operations at Genoa airport.

**Why it matters for Europe.** Drone incursions over European bases and airports are spreading from northern Europe to Italy; Sigonella hosts Italian, US and NATO ISR flights over the Black Sea and Middle East.

**What to watch.** Italian Defence Ministry statement; whether drones are identified.

## Russian double-tap strike on Kryvyi Rih; Zelensky says Crimean Bridge "will not stay"

**A Russian ballistic missile attack on Kryvyi Rih late on 20 November killed a 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old paramedic and injured 42, later 47, in what Kyiv Independent describes as a double-tap strike.**

Overnight on 21 November Russia launched three ballistic missiles and 148 drones (87 jet-powered), per Ukraine's Air Force; at least 2 were killed and 83 injured across Ukraine. Zelensky said Putin must understand the Crimean Bridge "will not stay" and warned Russia is increasing double-tap strikes.

**Why it matters for Europe.** The Ukraine war keeps escalating in parallel with US-Russia talks from which Europe is absent.

**What to watch.** Strikes on Crimea; Ukrainian energy grid.

## Update: ISW says Russian missiles and drones were fired into Polish airspace on purpose

**The Institute for the Study of War assessed that Russian forces intentionally fired missiles and drones into Polish airspace during strikes on Ukraine on 20 November.**

Poland's military is investigating the incursions; Al Jazeera reports Warsaw said its airspace was violated three times overnight, which Prime Minister Donald Tusk called a "provocation". One post claims two Swedish JAS Gripens failed to intercept missiles and a drone over Poland (single source, unverified).

**What to watch.** NATO consultations; Polish findings on the debris.
