## Update: Strait of Hormuz — Iran says it fired on a US F-16, and the IRGC reports a tanker on fire

Iranian and pro-Iranian accounts say Iranian air defences fired on, or fired a warning shot at, a US Air Force F-16 overnight near Qeshm and Hengam islands, and that the jet left the area; Times of India also reports the IRGC's claim of warning shots near Hengam. These are unverified Iranian claims, and no US statement had been seen by midday. The IRGC also says an oil tanker caught fire this morning north of Jazirat Umm al Ghanam and that a second tanker turned back; Anadolu quotes the IRGC Navy as saying a tanker exploded after hitting a naval mine. If confirmed, this would be the first direct Iran–US air contact reported in this phase of the war.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** the 08:30 edition reported two more tankers hitting mines near Hormuz and Brent near $96. CEAFE has tracked Iranian pressure on Hormuz traffic since the spring, when Chinese-owned tankers kept crossing the strait (@MenchOsint, May 2026, https://x.com/i/status/2054595874169233796).

**Why it matters for Europe:** Hormuz carries a large share of the oil and LNG Europe buys. Europeans have no naval or diplomatic weight of their own here while Washington and Tehran escalate.

**What to watch:** any US Central Command statement; UKMTO notices; insurance and freight rates.

## Update: Saudi–Yemen war — drone intercepted near Riyadh and a Houthi-side claim on Ras Tanura

Airspace over Riyadh and western Saudi Arabia was disrupted from about 12:28 Madrid time, and the Saudi spokesperson said a drone was intercepted near Riyadh (via @MenchOsint). A pro-Houthi mapping account claims that Ansarallah drones destroyed two oil tanks at the Ras Tanura terminal; the claim is unverified.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** on 25 November Houthi-aligned accounts reported a drone strike on the Ras Tanura refinery, Saudi Arabia's largest (@HormuzReport, https://x.com/i/status/2125568366932897990); the 08:30 edition reported new launches and Houthi pressure on Aden.

**Why it matters for Europe:** attacks on Saudi export terminals and on Hormuz shipping at the same time put pressure on Europe's energy supply. A shared European position on the Gulf is still missing.

**What to watch:** Saudi Aramco statements on Ras Tanura; Saudi strikes in Yemen.

## Ukraine war: drones over St Petersburg as Zelenskyy warns of an anti-ballistic missile shortage

Russian aggregator accounts report a large Ukrainian drone attack on St Petersburg overnight; citing the Russian defence ministry, they say 318 fixed-wing drones were intercepted (Russian claim, figure not verified). Drone strikes on industrial sites in Zaporizhzhia's Khortytskyi district on the evening of 26 November are also reported. Zelenskyy said: "It's all about air defense… we have a deficit of anti-ballistic missiles" (as quoted by @MilitarySummary). Russia has extended its ban on aviation fuel exports to 31 March.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** in October Ukraine resumed systematic strikes on Russian oil infrastructure, including a St Petersburg oil terminal (https://x.com/i/status/2111775078006378688). On 17 November Zelenskyy asked partners for Patriot and IRIS-T interceptors from their existing stocks (https://x.com/i/status/2122791598572380323).

**Why it matters for Europe:** Ukraine's shortage of anti-ballistic interceptors depends on US Patriot output (Raytheon, Lockheed Martin); Europe's own SAMP/T (Eurosam: MBDA and Thales) is not produced at that scale. Every new round of strikes deep inside Russia also raises the risk of escalation on the continent.

**What to watch:** Russian retaliation; new interceptor deliveries; the December talks.

Sources: @MilitarySummary https://x.com/i/status/2126203179402490340, https://x.com/i/status/2126203395778216310, https://x.com/i/status/2126232393493430713, Zaporizhzhia https://x.com/i/status/2126208540704412138, aviation fuel ban https://x.com/i/status/2126210736363815103, @ZelenskyyUa https://x.com/i/status/2122791598572380323.

## Zelenskyy on the BBC: talks to resume in December, but it is unclear whether Europeans will take part

In a BBC interview reported by Faytuks, Zelenskyy said the Abu Dhabi round made no obvious progress, that talks should resume in December and that it is unclear whether Europeans will take part. He accused Washington of a "neutral" stance that in effect supports both sides, and criticised Trump for calling Putin a friend, comparing it to calling Hitler a good friend during the Second World War. He said Starlink and Starshield remain restricted in occupied areas, but that he does not know whether SpaceX or political authorities decided this. A poll shared on X says 53% of Germans oppose military aid to Ukraine and 46% back it, against 59% in favour a year earlier (the pollster is not named in the post).

**Background (CEAFE archive):** on 17 October Zelenskyy had already said the restrictions on Ukraine's Starlink systems remained (https://x.com/i/status/2111425679376130421). On 26 November Kushner said Europe had been kept out of the Abu Dhabi talks (26 November, 08:30 edition).

**Why it matters for Europe:** a peace that decides Europe's security without Europeans at the table would be imposed from outside. CEAFE argues for a pan-European peace involving Ukraine, Russia and the EU. Ukraine's reliance on a US company's satellites is also a clear case of technological dependence.

**What to watch:** date, venue and participants of the December round; any EU invitation.

## Cyberattacks hit the US Air Force cloud, SAP and AFP

Reuters reports that hackers breached the US Air Force's Cloud One platform, possibly exposing military, personnel and logistics data; US Cyber Command is assessing the damage and the attackers are unknown. Der Spiegel reports a cyberattack on SAP, and another report says hackers entered SAP's GitHub with stolen employee logins. The French news agency AFP shut down its content platform after a cyberattack.

**Background (CEAFE archive):** in May 2026 the chief executives of Airbus, ASML, Ericsson, Mistral AI, Nokia, SAP and Siemens published a joint call, "Let's act as One Europe, Now" (https://x.com/i/status/2052093644195131602). On 23 November CEAFE shared Reuters' report that Europe's cloud-sovereignty push is gaining ground while US hyperscalers remain dominant (https://x.com/i/status/2124819328076001694).

**Why it matters for Europe:** SAP is Europe's largest software company and is widely used by governments and armed forces. Attacks on it and on a European news agency show that cyber defence is a shared European problem that no member state can solve alone.

**What to watch:** SAP's own statement on the scope of the breach; attribution of the Cloud One attack.
